Can You Explain What Dental Implants Are?

Dental implants replace missing teeth, serving as substitutes for adults. They offer viable tooth roots. These roots can help the new teeth work like before. They let you grind and chew food and regain your once-immaculate smile. Doctors recommend fitting patients with full mouth dental implants. This is for people who have lost all or nearly all of their teeth.

Most dental implant procedures succeed 90 to 95% of the time. But, this requires that patients follow the doctor’s instructions exactly. So be sure to follow your doctor’s orders.

  • Easier to clean and maintain
  • Restored eating and chewing functions
  • Does not need removal

How To Fit Dental Implants

This will explain how surgeons fit full mouth dental implants for their patients.

Starting With a Consultation

Before any dental implants procedure, the patient first sees the doctor. They will have an initial consultation. After this, your doctor will then decide if you are a good candidate and qualify for dental implants. They will use the following criteria:

  • You should be in good health to get dental implants. You should have few illnesses and vices like smoking. Your illnesses should be well-managed before the dental implants procedure. This is important because they may affect the stability of your dental implants.
  • Your jawbone should be adequate in size and density to fit and support the full dental implants. Your doctor will want to put them in your mouth without any problems. This will restore your smile to its fullest.
  • Your other teeth, which are not affected, should be healthy and free of any malformations. The artificial roots in your teeth can also affect connections with other teeth. So, the other teeth must be free of issues.
  • Most candidates for dental implants should have developed teeth and jawbones. So, young adults and adults are fit for the procedure.
  • As mentioned, only those with managed conditions are fit for full dental implants. Doctors will exclude those with severe medical conditions. These include cancer, jaw problems, uncontrolled diabetes, and hypertension as well as pregnant people. They can't get full mouth dental implants.

Dental Preparation and Implant Placement

Your mouth may have teeth pulled or bone added. This will stretch your jawbone to a bigger size. Afterward, your surgeon will open your affected gum by drilling a hole. This will provide enough space to position the implant. This dental implant will be the new root. It will be the base for the crown. After some healing time, the doctor will place the crown.

Setting Up The Crowns

There is a waiting period between placing the implant and the crown. Surgeons wait for the bone to form over the new root before finishing. This is to ensure that the jaw can adapt to the new fixture on the gums. It is also to check for any problems the patient has. After a long time, the surgeon may place the abutment. It is a bridge connecting the crowns, which will then form your smile.

Surgeons will often let you have custom-fit crowns. They follow the structure of your jawbone and the dental impressions captured.

Recovery and Follow-up

Your surgeon will then provide instructions on caring for your teeth. These include the usual forms of oral hygiene, like brushing and flossing. You must also avoid chewing hard food. It can affect your implant placement. Follow up with your surgeon after your dental implants procedure. Your doctor will want to check if there are any problems after the procedure.
