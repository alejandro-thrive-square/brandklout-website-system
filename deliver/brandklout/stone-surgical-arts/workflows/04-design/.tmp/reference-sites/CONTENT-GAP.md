# Content gap analysis — the four references against the Stone site

Written 2026-09-01, evening, from [README.md](./README.md) (the section-by-section read of the four
home pages) and the Asana page list. This is the second deliverable of the brief that day:
*"analyze the root content and purpose of the sections in the reference sites. That way we need to
see what gaps we have on the current site and add new sections to it if necessary."*

The method: every section on the four references was reduced to its **purpose** (what question it
answers for the visitor), the purposes were grouped, and each group was checked against the Stone
site as it stood that morning (home, neck lift, blepharoplasty). A gap is a purpose no page served.
The right-hand column says where it is served now, or why it deliberately is not.

## The gaps that were real, and what closed them

Five purposes had no answer anywhere on the site. Each is now a section or a page.

1. **Entry by concern** (5). The single strongest idea on the medspa site, and the one no surgical
   reference has. Added as the concern list on the home page: nine lines from "a neck that has lost
   its line" to "a man's face, planned as one", each pointing at the treatments whose archived pages
   describe that concern.
2. **The team** (12). The archive names three (Brooke; Elvira "LV" Cortez, office manager; Jennifer
   Herrera, first assistant) and the Drive export carries seven portraits. A Meet the team page.
3. **The place** (8). Six real photographs of the practice were sitting in the library unused. The
   filmstrip on the home page and the About page.
4. **A procedure index** (4). Four cards that named a category and stopped. The catalogue lists all
   twenty-nine treatments under their four categories, and the Treatments hub carries the fees.
5. **Men** (18). Two references address men directly. The archive has a male facial enhancement
   page; it is now reachable.

## The gaps that stay open on purpose

- **Before/after thumbnails on the home page** (6). Every reference has them. Stone's archive holds
  33 consented cases for a site that no longer exists, and the Drive export holds a device
  manufacturer's photograph of another surgeon's patient. Neither goes on the site until the practice
  supplies cases with current consent. The Results page is built to receive them.
- **Video** (15) and **articles** (16). Nothing exists to put there. A section with nothing in it is
  worse than no section.
- **A trademarked signature** (14). Maercks' whole middle is two trademarks. Stone has a signature
  (the neck lift; @drjawline) without a trademark, and the neck lift page is where that lives.
- **An inline lead form on the home page** (11). Nip & Tuck's form is the last section of its home
  page. Stone's booking form still posts nowhere (HANDOFF.md §5); until it does, the honest call to
  action is the phone number and the address, which is what the invitation chapter carries.
