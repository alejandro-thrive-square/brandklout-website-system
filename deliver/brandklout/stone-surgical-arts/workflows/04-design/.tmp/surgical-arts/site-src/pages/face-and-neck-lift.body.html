01 · What it does

A face lift is not about pulling skin tight.

Treating skin as the problem is how a face ends up looking operated on rather than rested. The work happens underneath, in the muscle and fascia that have descended. The skin is redraped over the result.

Surface Skin Fat SMAS Deepest

The SMAS, repositioned

The muscle and fascia under the skin have descended, and they are lifted and fixed back where they sat. In the neck the same layer is the platysma, repaired in the midline. This is the layer that holds.

Where the skin still has tone, the neck can sometimes be answered with a suture alone. That is the MyEllevate® conversation.

The fat, put back where it belongs

A jowl is fat that has slid below the jawline, a hollow cheek is fat that has left. Both are addressed with the deeper layer, and fullness under the chin is contoured through the same incision.

Where the mid-face has lost volume, fat transfer can be planned alongside, decided at the consultation.

The skin, redraped

The skin is laid back over the repositioned layer and only the genuine excess removed. It carries no tension, which is the difference between rested and pulled.

A lift restores position. It does not change the quality of the skin, which is what resurfacing is for, often staged afterwards.

03 · How it is done

In front of the ear, around the lobe, and back into the hairline.

The incisions follow the creases in front of the ear, around the earlobe and into the hairline, with a small one under the chin. Through them the SMAS is lifted and fixed, the platysma repaired, fat contoured, and the skin redraped with only the excess removed.

Four to six hours under general anaesthetic, depending on what is combined. You are monitored, then go home with a responsible adult, or stay overnight if that is safer.

Scars sit where the anatomy conceals them and mature from pink to pale over months. Scar care starts as soon as the wounds are closed.

04 · Recovery

A day, a fortnight, and a year of settling.

Conventional ranges, to plan around. Dr. Stone will give you the numbers for your own face.

  1. The day

    Four to six hours in theatre

    Under general anaesthetic. A responsible adult drives you home, or you stay the night. No driving for twenty-four hours.

  2. Day three

    Bruising and swelling peak, then fall away

    A support garment, tightness, and a face that looks worse than it feels. It improves quickly from here.

  3. Week two

    Sutures out, presentable in company

    Sutures come out in stages. Most people are presentable at around two weeks and back at a desk at two to three. No lifting or sharp head turns for six weeks.

  4. Months six to twelve

    The result settles

    It keeps refining as swelling resolves and scars mature. Follow-ups are with Dr. Stone herself, for up to eighteen months.

07 · Questions

The six questions this operation raises.

Where do the scars go, and will people see them?

In front of the ear, around the earlobe and into the hairline behind it, where the anatomy hides them. They mature from pink to pale over months.

How long will it last?

It resets the clock rather than stopping it. Most people look substantially better than their unoperated baseline for a decade or more, then age normally from there. Skin quality, weight stability, sun exposure and genetics all move that figure.

Will I look like a different person?

No. If it looks like a different person, the planning went wrong. The aim is that people register that you look well, not altered: a matter of how much tension is placed on which layer.

Can I have just the neck?

Yes. The neck lift is quoted separately at $11,000, and for the right anatomy MyEllevate® at $6,000 achieves a great deal with no external incision.

Can I combine it with eyelid surgery or resurfacing?

Often. A lifted face with tired eyes reads as tired, so blepharoplasty is commonly planned alongside. Laser resurfacing reaches the texture and pigment a lift cannot, usually staged.
