Chapter 05 · The Path

Four visits, not four years.

01

Consultation & 3D Scan

A cone-beam scan shows the bone we actually have to work with. You leave knowing whether you are a candidate, what it costs, and how long it takes.

02

Digital Smile Design

The final teeth are designed before a single implant is placed. You approve the result in advance rather than hoping for it.

Guided Placement

Implants are placed through a surgical guide built from your scan, under sedation if you want it. Many patients leave the same day with fixed teeth.

Once healing is complete the definitive teeth go in, shaped, shaded and bite-balanced by the same specialist who planned them.

Thin Arch

FP1, FP2, FP3, and why we plan for the first.

Every full-arch implant prosthesis is one of three types. What separates them is how much of it is teeth, and how much is pink acrylic standing in for gum and bone. Most practices fit one kind. Here the arch is planned as thin as the case will carry.

FP1

Replaces teeth only

  • Natural crown-and-bridge look
  • No added pink, no extra bulk
  • Typically on more natural gum architecture
FP2

Longer teeth, minimal pink

  • Longer teeth, minimal pink acrylic
  • The pink that is added still follows the natural gum contour
  • Rarely indicated, and often not ideal
FP3

Replaces teeth, gums and bone

  • Thick pink acrylic stands in for gum and bone
  • Thicker, stronger prosthesis
  • Typically best where there is bone loss, and a lower hygiene risk

A thin arch is not always possible. Where bone loss is advanced, FP3 is both the honest answer and the stronger one. What is worth asking is whether anyone planned for the alternative. How a full arch is planned
