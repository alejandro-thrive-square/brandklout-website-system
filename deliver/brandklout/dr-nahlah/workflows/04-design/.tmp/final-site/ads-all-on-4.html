Why Four Is Enough

The back two are angled into the bone you still have.

The two rear implants tilt forward into the denser bone at the front of the jaw. Four posts then carry a full arch without rebuilding the back of the mouth first.

Which is why bone loss often makes you a candidate rather than ruling you out. Some cases still need grafting, and the scan is what decides that.

  • Four implants per arch, upper, lower or both
  • A fixed PMMA provisional arch fitted the same visit
  • Three to four months to the definitive zirconia arch
  • A further two to four months completes healing around it

Before You Book

The questions people actually ask.

Will I leave surgery without teeth?
No. In most cases you leave the same visit wearing a fixed PMMA provisional arch, which stays in place while the implants integrate.
What if I have been told I do not have enough bone?
Do not rule yourself out. All-on-4 is often the answer because of bone loss, since the rear implants are angled to reach denser bone. Some cases still need grafting first, and the cone-beam scan at the consultation is what determines that.
Does it hurt?
The surgery is done under sedation, so you feel nothing during it and most patients have no memory of the appointment. Afterwards there is minor discomfort that over-the-counter medication handles.
Can it do both jaws?
Yes: upper, lower, or both. That is why the cases above are described as single arch or double arch.
How long until it is finished?
Three to four months from surgery to the final zirconia arch, then a further two to four months for complete healing around it.
What is included afterwards?
A warranty conditional on Precare: cleanings every six months, which is the same interval any set of teeth needs.
Do you take my insurance?
Most dental PPO plans are accepted. Coverage for implants is often limited, but limited is not nothing, and the practice files the claims on your behalf and works the plan for every benefit it entitles you to.
