The Argument

Reinforce the tooth before you replace it.

The instinct is to have a damaged tooth out and be done with it. Usually the wrong one: extraction starts a second problem. The bone that held the root resorbs, the neighboring teeth drift, and the bite changes. A tooth kept is a problem that stops there.

Crowns

Repair a damaged tooth.

A fixed cap that covers the tooth to restore strength and appearance, for when a tooth is too far gone for bonding or a filling. It is also how a tooth is protected after a root canal, and how an implant is topped.

  • Restore strength to a damaged tooth
  • Improve tooth size, shape and color
  • Repair a tooth after severe decay
  • Preserve a tooth after a root canal
  • Restore a dental implant

Designed in the in-house lab, with fit and color verified against the teeth on either side before it is seated. Cared for properly, fifteen years or more.

Bridges

Replace a missing tooth.

If a tooth is already gone and you are not ready to commit to an implant, a bridge is the next best option: a run of crowns fused together, spanning the gap and anchored to the teeth on either side.

  • Restore function and appearance after tooth loss
  • Improve the health of the whole mouth
  • Eliminate gaps in the smile
  • Keep the remaining teeth from shifting
  • Long-term strength and stability

A bridge can also be anchored to implants rather than natural teeth, which avoids reshaping two healthy neighbors to carry it. Worth asking about before you decide.

Cost

What decides the number.

Two questions come first on every call: do you take my insurance, and what will this cost. The front desk checks the first before you come in. The second turns on two things:

  • How many teeth are being treated
  • Which material you choose

Both are settled at the consultation, in writing, with financing options if you want the cost spread monthly.
