The Anatomy

Three parts. One of them is yours within four months.

Bone grows onto the titanium post and locks it in place. That middle step, roughly four months, is the reason implants do not slip, rub or need adhesive.

Which is why planning matters more than placing. A post in the wrong position integrates just as firmly as one in the right position, and then the tooth on top of it can never be right. The specialist behind the plan

Your Options

One tooth, several teeth, or all of them.

These four cover almost every case that walks in.

01

Single Implant

One post, one crown. The right answer for a tooth lost to impact or fracture, where the teeth either side are healthy and should be left alone.

02

Implant-Supported Bridge

Two implants carry a span of teeth between them, without putting the load on the natural teeth beside the gap.

03

Implant-Supported Dentures

A denture that clips onto implants instead of resting on gums. The pressure moves off the tissue and onto the bone.

The Process

What actually happens, and when.

A few months to over a year, depending on whether bone has to be rebuilt first.

01

Consultation & 3D scan

The scan shows the bone you actually have. You leave knowing whether you are a candidate, what it costs, and how long it takes.

02

Surgery, in one visit

Often a few hours, through a guide built from your scan, under sedation. Most patients have no memory of the appointment.

03

Integration: about four months

Bone grows onto the posts. Back to normal within a week, soft foods while it fuses, and a temporary worn throughout.

Zirconia for full-arch work, shaped, shaded and bite-balanced by the specialist who planned it.

Fixed vs. Removable

Dentures rest on you. Implants are part of you.

Dentures sit on the gum and move: hence the rubbing, the adhesive, the food restrictions, and over years the bone loss that changes the shape of a face. An implant loads the bone instead of the tissue.

  • Permanently anchored: no slipping, no adhesive, no removal at night
  • Brushed and flossed like natural teeth; no separate care routine
  • Loads the jawbone, which limits recession and supports facial structure
  • Higher up front, lower over time: less repair, relining and replacement
  • Restorations resist decay, but still need six-month hygiene visits

Questions

The ones people actually ask.

Will the surgery hurt?
Done under sedation. You feel nothing during it, and most patients have no memory of the appointment. Afterwards, minor discomfort that over-the-counter medication handles.
What is recovery like?
Rest for a few days. Most people are back to normal activity within a week. A soft-food diet is needed for the next several months while the implants integrate with the jawbone.
Am I a candidate?
Many patients already are. Others qualify after bone grafting or extractions. Candidacy is determined from a comprehensive consultation and a 3D scan, not guessed at.
How long do implants last?
They are designed to last decades, and can last the rest of your life. Surgical precision, materials and your own habits are what decide it. Implants have held a published success rate above 95% since they were introduced.
Do I still need check-ups?
Yes. Implant restorations resist decay, but the gum around an implant can still develop peri-implantitis from plaque and tartar. Brush twice daily, floss once, and keep the six-month hygiene appointments.
What does it cost?
It depends on how many teeth are missing, how long for, the materials, and whether anything has to be rebuilt first. Claims are filed on your behalf. What drives the cost
