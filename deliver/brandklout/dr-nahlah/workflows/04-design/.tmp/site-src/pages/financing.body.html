The Principle

Affordable and accessible, whatever the treatment.

Whatever the procedure, the practice makes it a priority to offer practical payment options for patients in any financial situation. That is a stated position rather than a promotion, and it comes from a specific belief: oral health has a direct correlation to physical health.

Which makes deferred dental care a health decision, not a budget decision. A tooth left because the number looked bad rarely gets cheaper, and the conditions that follow tooth loss are considerably more expensive than the treatment that would have prevented them.

So call and speak to somebody about it before you decide. Working out what is actually payable is a conversation, and it costs nothing to have.

Payment

Five accepted forms.

  • Cash
  • Check
  • Credit card — most major cards accepted
  • Dental insurance — most PPO plans

On insurance: bring your plan details to the first visit and the practice will check what it covers and help you use the benefits you already have. Claims are filed on your behalf rather than left to you.

No Insurance?

You have got options. That is the whole message.

Not having dental insurance is the single most common reason people assume a treatment is out of reach, and it is very often wrong. The financing terms above are not contingent on having a plan.

The practice's financial coordinator will walk you through an application and set up a payment plan that fits. It is quick, many patients are approved instantly, and in some cases without a credit check.

Bring the estimate you already have, if you have one — second opinions are free, and they occasionally change the number as well as the plan.
