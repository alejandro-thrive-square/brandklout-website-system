The Choice

Fixed, or removable. Everything else follows.

If you have many failing or missing teeth, or already wear dentures, you are probably having small daily experiences that are uncomfortable or embarrassing, and have stopped expecting that to change.

It can. The real decision is one question: does the replacement come out, or does it stay in? Removable covers traditional dentures and implant-supported dentures. Fixed means a prosthetic bridge of teeth permanently carried on four to eight implants.

Each has its own advantages, drawbacks and cost, and the honest version of this conversation includes all three. Knowing what you want out of treatment is what makes the choice obvious, which is why the comparison below leaves the unflattering lines in.

Comparison

Three tiers, listed honestly.

Every line below is from the practice's own comparison, including the ones that do not flatter the more expensive options.

01

Traditional dentures

Around 20% of natural function. Artificial teeth on an acrylic base that sits over the gums. The bottom tier of tooth replacement, and the cheapest.

  • Made from acrylic
  • Offers minimal support
  • Will need relines and repairs
  • Often loosens or falls out
  • Does not halt jawbone loss
  • Must be removed to clean
  • May be bulky or ill-fitting
  • Low-cost, and qualifies for financing
02

Implant-supported dentures

Around 60% of natural function. A removable acrylic denture, but stabilized by two or more implants so it stops moving.

  • Acrylic or porcelain
  • Support from two or more implants
  • May still need relines or repairs
  • Will not slip, loosen or fall out
  • Greater stability for eating
  • Removable for easy cleaning
  • Improves jawbone health
  • May not look or feel fully natural
  • Moderate cost
03

Full-arch fixed teeth

Over 98% of natural function. A zirconia bridge fixed permanently to four to eight implants. It does not come out, and it is cared for like teeth.

  • Zirconia porcelain
  • Fixed to four to eight implants
  • Comparable to natural teeth
  • Promotes a healthy jawbone
  • Resistant to bacteria and decay
  • Fracture- and chip-resistant
  • Improves oral and overall health
  • No additional maintenance
  • Highest cost

The Process

Four stages, and you are not toothless for any of them.

01

Consultation

A personalized evaluation with the implant dentist, and a plan that fits your smile goals, your oral health and your budget rather than the practice's preference.

02

Preparation, if needed

Extractions, bone grafting or gum disease therapy first, where they are needed, to build a sound foundation. Skipping this is what makes implants fail later.

Oral surgery
03

Surgery

Done in this office under sedation, guided by the scan, and generally finished in a few hours. In many cases you leave with temporary teeth, or your existing denture modified, so you are functional while you heal.

04

Final teeth

Once healing is complete the final set is crafted to your specifications alongside the clinical recommendations, then attached. That is the version you keep.
