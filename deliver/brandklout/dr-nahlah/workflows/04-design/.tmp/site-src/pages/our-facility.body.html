Welcome

You are welcome here.

A great many adults put off dental treatment out of anxiety, and almost none of them will say so at the desk. So the building is arranged on the assumption that you might be one of them.

The waiting area is quiet and daylit rather than clinical. Treatment happens in a private suite with the door closed, not a row of open bays. And the whole first appointment is a conversation and a scan — nothing is done to you on the day you arrive to find out what your options are.

If that is still not enough, it does not have to be white-knuckled. Nitrous, oral and IV sedation are all offered here, and the practice holds the credential to administer them.

Technology

Six machines, and what each one saves you.

Equipment is only worth listing if it changes the appointment. Here is what each of these actually does to your visit.

01

CBCT scanner

Cone-beam computed tomography images the jaw in three dimensions, so bone volume and nerve position are measured before surgery rather than found during it.

02

Intraoral scanner

Digital impressions. No tray, no putty, no gagging — and an accuracy a physical impression cannot reach, which is the real reason for it.

03

Computer-guided surgery

The implant position is planned on the scan and placed through a guide. Smaller incisions, less bone disturbed, and a shorter recovery.

04

In-house digital lab

Prostheses are designed on site by a lab designer on staff, so a shade or a shape can be argued about in person instead of described on a form.

05

Milling machine

The design is cut here. Nothing is posted to another state and waited on, which is what removes weeks from a full-arch case.

06

Zirconia & porcelain oven

Final fabrication and firing happen in the building, so a remake is days rather than the better part of a month.

Why It Matters

The whole chain, in one building.

Any one of these machines can be rented time on. What is unusual is holding the entire chain — scan, plan, surgery, design, mill, fire, fit — inside a single practice, under one person's responsibility.

It matters most when something needs changing. A prosthesis that seats a fraction proud is a same-week fix here and a six-week round trip almost anywhere else. Over a case that runs four to six months, that difference is the case.

  • Fewer appointments across the whole treatment
  • Days, not weeks, when a remake is needed
  • One practice accountable for the fit, the bite and the look
