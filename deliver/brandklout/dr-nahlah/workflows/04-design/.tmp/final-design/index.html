Most implant failures are not surgical. They are planning failures — and planning is what a prosthodontist is trained to do.

A prosthodontist is one of twelve dental specialties recognised by the American Dental Association. It takes three additional years of hospital-based training beyond dental school, focused entirely on rebuilding teeth — how they fit, how they bite, how they last.

Dr. Nahlah plans the final smile first, then places the implants to serve it. That order is the whole difference between teeth that work for a decade and teeth that work for life.

The Path

Four visits, not four years.

Surgery and restoration happen under one roof, which removes the handoffs where most implant timelines quietly fall apart.

01

Consultation & 3D Scan

A cone-beam scan shows the bone we actually have to work with. You leave knowing whether you are a candidate, what it costs, and how long it takes.

02

Digital Smile Design

The final teeth are designed before a single implant is placed. You approve the result in advance rather than hoping for it.

03

Guided Placement

Implants are placed through a surgical guide built from your scan, under sedation if you want it. Many patients leave the same day with fixed teeth.

04

Final Restoration

Once healing is complete, the definitive teeth go in — shaped, shaded and bite-balanced by the same specialist who planned them.
