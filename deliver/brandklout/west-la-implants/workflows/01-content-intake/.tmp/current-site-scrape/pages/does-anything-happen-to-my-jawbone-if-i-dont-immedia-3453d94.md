Dental implants in Los Angeles, FL, are a gold standard tooth replacement option that are surgically placed in a patient’s jawbone. A dental implant post is designed to act like a natural tooth root, giving patients a permanent and stable new smile.

When people don’t replace their missing teeth with restorative dental implants, their oral health and facial structure could be negatively affected. Continue reading to learn more about what could happen to the jawbone if missing teeth aren’t immediately replaced with dental implants.


## What Happens To The Jawbone If Missing Teeth Aren’t immediately Replaced With Dental Implants In Los Angeles, FL


If people don’t replace their missing teeth with dental implants, then the bone in their jaw could shrink over time. This is known as jawbone resorption and it can cause functional problems, such as having difficulty chewing and speaking properly. Not replacing missing teeth can also lead to a sunken-in facial appearance and an altered bite.

This is why people should replace their missing teeth with dental implants. Dental implants are surgically placed into the jawbone, where they act like artificial roots that stimulate the jawbone and become a permanent part of the patient’s mouth. Replacing missing teeth with dental implants is essential for maintaining healthy jawbone structure and function.


## How Are My Missing Teeth Replaced With Dental Implants In Los Angeles, FL?


Because every person’s smile is unique, dental implant procedures are customized to each patient’s specific smile. After the patient’s dental implant procedure is planned out in the consultation, the doctor will strategically and precisely place the dental implants into their jawbone.

The dental implants can then heal and integrate with the patient’s jawbone for several months. As the dental implants are fusing with the patient’s jawbone, a temporary crown or prosthesis can be placed on the dental implants so the patient can have a functioning and aesthetically pleasing smile while they wait for their final one.

When the final prosthesis is ready, it will be securely attached to the dental implants. With dental implants, patients will be able to eat, speak, and smile for years to come.
