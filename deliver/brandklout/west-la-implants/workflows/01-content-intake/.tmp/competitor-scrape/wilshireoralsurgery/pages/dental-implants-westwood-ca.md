## The Science of Dental Implants — What Westwood Patients Should Know


A dental implant is a titanium post that is surgically placed into the jawbone to serve as an artificial root. Once placed, it undergoes osseointegration, a biological process in which bone cells grow into and around the titanium surface, anchoring the implant permanently. A crown is then placed on top, restoring the tooth’s appearance and function. The implant does not rely on adjacent teeth for support, does not require adhesive, and does not need to be removed for cleaning.

Bone preservation is one of the most clinically significant advantages of implants over other tooth replacement options. When a tooth is lost, the bone that supported it begins to resorb in the absence of mechanical stimulation. An implant provides that stimulation, halting the resorption process that would otherwise continue under a bridge or denture. The National Library of Medicine documents dental implants as a well-established treatment modality in dentistry with clinical outcomes supported by decades of evidence.

Osseointegration

Bone cells grow into the titanium surface over three to six months, permanently anchoring the implant without cement, screws through adjacent teeth, or adhesive.

Bone Preservation

The implant provides mechanical stimulation to the jawbone, preventing the resorption that begins within months of tooth loss and continues progressively under conventional restorations.

Structural Independence

The implant is self-supporting and does not require reducing, crowning, or loading adjacent healthy teeth — a meaningful long-term advantage over bridges.

Long Clinical Track Record

Implant dentistry is supported by more than four decades of clinical research. Outcomes data from multicenter studies inform current surgical techniques and protocols.


## Technology-Driven Planning for Westwood Implant Patients


We use Cone Beam 3D imaging for every implant case. This technology creates a precise three-dimensional map of the surgical site, showing bone density, width, height, root positions, and the exact location of nerves and sinus anatomy. We use this data to plan each implant’s position, angle, and depth before any surgical procedure begins, reducing variability and improving predictability.

If bone volume at the implant site is insufficient due to prior tooth loss or atrophy, bone grafting can rebuild the site prior to or concurrent with implant placement. When appropriate, we also use platelet-rich fibrin to support healing at the surgical site. After integration, your referring general dentist or prosthodontist places the final crown, and we coordinate with your provider throughout the process. Our complete post-operative care instructions are provided at every stage.

Is implant surgery painful?

Most patients report less discomfort than they expected. The procedure is performed under local anesthesia or IV sedation, so the surgery itself is not painful. Post-operative discomfort is typically mild to moderate and well-managed with prescribed or over-the-counter pain management. Most patients return to normal activity within two to three days.

What is the success rate of dental implants?

Clinical research documents high long-term implant survival rates in healthy patients with adequate bone, though specific rates vary by study, implant system, location in the mouth, and patient health factors. At your consultation, we discuss your specific risk profile and what the clinical literature says about expected outcomes for your situation.

What happens if an implant fails?

Implant failure, while uncommon, does occur in a small percentage of cases. It most often presents as failure to osseointegrate or as peri-implantitis developing years after placement due to poor oral hygiene or systemic factors. We discuss the specific risk factors that apply to your case at your consultation and build a follow-up plan into your care from the start.

Can I get an implant if I have diabetes or another systemic condition?

Many patients with well-controlled systemic conditions, including diabetes, are suitable implant candidates. Poorly controlled blood sugar does increase the risk of complications, so we review your medical history carefully before making a recommendation. In some cases, coordination with your primary care physician is part of the pre-surgical workup.
