## What Mar Vista Parents Need to Know About Wisdom Teeth


Third molars typically begin developing in the late teens and are fully formed by the mid-twenties. The modern jaw frequently lacks space for all four, leading to impaction. The American Association of Oral and Maxillofacial Surgeons recommends evaluation in the late teens, ideally before roots are fully developed and bone has become denser. Earlier removal leads to shorter procedures, faster recovery, and fewer complications, which is why the coordination effort tends to be well worth it when handled during a school break.

For Mar Vista families scheduling around a student’s academic calendar, the summer window is the most forgiving. Recovery from a standard four-tooth extraction is typically three to seven days, which fits comfortably within a break between semesters. Winter and spring breaks are shorter but workable for straightforward cases. We advise scheduling the consultation first so we can evaluate the imaging and give you an accurate recovery estimate before committing to a specific window.

## Frequently Asked Questions — Wisdom Teeth Removal near Mar Vista


What X-rays should I bring from my child’s dentist?

A panoramic X-ray is the most useful to bring. If your dentist took a panoramic in the last six to twelve months, we can often work from that at the consultation. If the imaging is older or unavailable, we can take in-house Cone Beam 3D imaging on-site, which gives us even more detailed information about tooth position and nerve proximity.

My teenager is nervous about surgery — how do you handle anxious patients?

IV sedation is the most effective tool for anxious patients. Most sedated patients wake with no memory of the procedure and are surprised by how manageable the experience was. We also take time at the consultation to answer every question and explain what will happen step by step, which significantly reduces pre-procedure anxiety for both patients and parents.

Does my PPO dental insurance cover wisdom teeth removal?

Most PPO dental plans cover at least a portion of wisdom tooth removal, particularly when the teeth are impacted and the procedure is medically indicated. Coverage varies by plan. We contact your insurer before the procedure is scheduled to verify your specific benefits and provide you with an accurate cost estimate before anything is booked.

How do I know if my child’s wisdom teeth are actually impacted?

Impaction is determined through imaging, not symptoms alone. A tooth that has not erupted into its normal position in the mouth, or that is growing at an angle or blocked by bone, is classified as impacted. Your dentist or orthodontist may have already identified this on a panoramic X-ray. We confirm and characterize impaction using our in-house 3D imaging at the consultation.

How long until my child can drive after the procedure?

If IV sedation is used, the patient cannot drive on the day of surgery and must be transported by an adult. Most patients can resume driving the following day when they are no longer taking any prescription pain medication that impairs alertness. We provide full guidance on when it is safe to drive at discharge.

What foods can my child eat during recovery?

Soft foods and liquids are recommended for the first 24 to 48 hours, such as yogurt, applesauce, broth, and smoothies. Avoid hot liquids, straws, and anything hard or crunchy for the first week. Our post-operative care instructions include a detailed diet guide for the full recovery period.
