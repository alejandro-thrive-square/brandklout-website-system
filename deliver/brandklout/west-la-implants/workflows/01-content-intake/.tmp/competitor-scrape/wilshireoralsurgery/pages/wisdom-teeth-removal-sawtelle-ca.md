## What Parents and Patients in Sawtelle Should Know About Wisdom Teeth


The recommendation to remove wisdom teeth often comes from an orthodontist or general dentist at a routine visit. Third molars are the last teeth to develop, typically between ages 17 and 25, and the modern jaw usually lacks the space to accommodate them. When they become impacted, they can cause ongoing infection, crowding, cyst formation, and damage to the adjacent second molars.

For parents coordinating care for a son or daughter, the most common questions are about timing, recovery expectations, and whether the procedure can be done in one appointment. For the patient, the anxiety about the procedure itself is often the primary concern. At Wilshire Oral Surgery and Implant Center, we address both. The American Association of Oral and Maxillofacial Surgeons recommends early evaluation, typically in the late teens, to allow for removal before roots are fully developed and recovery becomes more complex. Earlier removal means shorter procedures, faster healing, and fewer complications.

For Teenagers and Young Adults

Removal in the late teens often means shorter roots, less dense bone, and a recovery that is more predictable and faster than waiting until the mid-twenties or beyond.

For College Students Home on Break

Sawtelle’s proximity to our office means a student can have their consultation and procedure handled efficiently during a two-to-three week break without extended travel.

For Working Adults

Wisdom tooth pain and infection are unpredictable. A working adult who defers care often ends up managing an acute situation on a bad timeline. Proactive removal is almost always more convenient.

For Bilingual Households

## Frequently Asked Questions — Wisdom Teeth Removal near Sawtelle


My teenager was referred for wisdom teeth removal — what is the first step?

The first step is a consultation at our office, which includes a review of any X-rays your general dentist or orthodontist has already taken, or new in-house 3D imaging if needed. We evaluate each tooth’s position and root development, explain what we find in plain language, and provide a clear recommendation and cost estimate before anything is scheduled.

Can a parent accompany a minor during the procedure?

A parent or legal guardian must be present for any minor patient and must provide consent for surgery. The parent or guardian is required to remain in the building during the procedure and to drive the patient home if IV sedation is used. Our staff will keep you informed and guide you through the process from arrival through discharge.

What is recovery like for a teenager compared to an adult?

Younger patients with less fully developed roots and less dense bone typically recover somewhat faster than older adults with fully mature wisdom teeth. Swelling and discomfort peak in the first 48 to 72 hours for both groups and then gradually improve. Most teenagers are significantly more comfortable by day three or four, similar to adult patients.

Do you offer bilingual consultations?

Yes. Our team communicates in English, Spanish, and Farsi/Persian. If you or your family member would prefer to receive pre-operative instructions, discuss the procedure, or ask post-operative questions in Spanish or Farsi, we can accommodate that throughout the entire care process.

Is your office actually in the Sawtelle neighborhood?

Yes. Our address is 12300 Wilshire Blvd #326, Los Angeles, CA 90025, which is in the 90025 zip code that covers the Sawtelle corridor. Patients from the residential streets near Sawtelle Avenue and Olympic Boulevard can walk or take a very short drive to reach us.

How do I know if wisdom teeth removal is actually necessary?

The recommendation is based on imaging, not just symptoms. An impacted or poorly positioned wisdom tooth that causes no current pain may still be developing a problem that will become more complex and more difficult to treat over time. We review your imaging at your consultation and give you a clear, honest assessment of what we see and what we recommend — and why.
