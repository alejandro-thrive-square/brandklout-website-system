## Frequently Asked Questions From Century City Patients


How far is Wilshire Oral Surgery from Century City?

Our office is at 12300 Wilshire Blvd #326, Los Angeles, CA 90025. From Century City, the drive west along Wilshire Boulevard or Santa Monica Boulevard takes approximately 5 to 10 minutes depending on traffic. No freeway is required — the route is a straightforward surface street drive from the neighborhood.

Do I need a referral to schedule a consultation?

No referral is needed. Century City patients can schedule directly with our office. If your general dentist has provided X-rays or records, bringing those to your first appointment can help us plan more efficiently — but it is not required to schedule.

What sedation options are available?

We offer local anesthesia, IV sedation, and general anesthesia, all administered in-office by our board-certified surgeons. IV sedation is commonly used for wisdom tooth removal, implant placement, and patients with dental anxiety. General anesthesia is available for more complex or multi-procedure cases. Your surgeon will recommend the appropriate option during your consultation.

How long is recovery after wisdom tooth removal?

Most patients recover within five to seven days, with peak swelling and discomfort in the first two to three days. Patients receiving IV sedation will need a driver home and should plan to rest that day. We provide complete post-operative instructions and are available by phone for any questions during recovery.

Does insurance cover dental implants or oral surgery?

Coverage depends on your specific plan. Many PPO dental plans provide partial coverage for medically necessary procedures such as wisdom tooth removal, bone grafting related to trauma, and biopsies. Dental implants may be partially covered depending on the reason for tooth loss. We review your plan before your appointment and provide a full cost breakdown in advance. Visit our insurance and financing page for more information.

What is the difference between an oral surgeon and a general dentist?

Oral and maxillofacial surgeons complete four to six years of accredited hospital-based surgical residency after dental school, with advanced training in anesthesia, complex extractions, implant surgery, bone grafting, jaw reconstruction, and facial trauma. This level of training is particularly important for patients with bone loss, anatomical complexity, or cases requiring sedation. General dentists can handle straightforward extractions, but more complex surgical cases are best managed by a board-certified specialist.
