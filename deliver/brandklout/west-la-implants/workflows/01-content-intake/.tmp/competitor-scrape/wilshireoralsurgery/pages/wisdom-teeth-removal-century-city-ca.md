## Why Wisdom Teeth Require an Oral Surgeon


General dentists perform routine extractions, but wisdom teeth, particularly impacted ones, involve surgical access, bone removal, tooth sectioning, and proximity to critical nerves that require the training of an oral and maxillofacial surgeon. The inferior alveolar nerve, which runs through the lower jaw, can be within millimeters of lower wisdom tooth roots. Cone Beam 3D imaging lets us measure this relationship precisely before any instrument is used.

The American Association of Oral and Maxillofacial Surgeons identifies impacted wisdom teeth as one of the most common oral surgery indications, particularly in younger adults. Board-certified surgeons like Dr. Shadi and Dr. Vahedi have completed hospital-based residency training that included high volumes of impacted wisdom tooth cases across varying levels of complexity. That experience translates directly to a more predictable outcome and a more comfortable patient experience.

Mesioangular Impaction

The most common type. The tooth is angled toward the front of the mouth, pressing against the second molar. May cause crowding or root resorption.

Horizontal Impaction

The tooth lies completely on its side, pointing directly into the second molar’s root. More complex to extract and typically requires sectioning.

Vertical Impaction

The tooth is in a normal vertical position but has not fully erupted into the mouth. May cause infection risk at the gum flap even when no pain is present.

Distoangular Impaction

The tooth angles backward toward the rear of the jaw. Among the more technically challenging cases due to limited surgical access and proximity to nerves.


## What to Expect on Procedure Day and During Recovery


For most patients, wisdom tooth surgery is an outpatient procedure completed in under an hour. If you choose IV sedation, you will need a driver for the return trip to Century City or elsewhere, and you should plan to rest for the remainder of the day. Most patients experience their peak swelling at 48 to 72 hours, after which it gradually decreases. By day three or four, most people feel significantly more comfortable and can return to desk work.

Our team provides complete post-operative care instructions covering diet, medication management, activity restrictions, and what to watch for during healing. We also provide our pre-operative guidelines and pre-anesthesia instructions in advance so there are no surprises on the morning of surgery. We remain available for follow-up questions throughout your recovery.

## Frequently Asked Questions — Wisdom Teeth Removal near Century City


How long is the drive from Century City to your office?

Most Century City patients arrive in five to ten minutes heading west on Wilshire Boulevard. Our address is 12300 Wilshire Blvd #326, Los Angeles, CA 90025, near Bundy Drive. No freeway required.

When is the best time to have wisdom teeth removed?

The optimal window is typically in the late teens or early twenties, when root development is not yet complete and the surrounding bone is more flexible. However, wisdom tooth removal is performed successfully at any age. The main factors are the clinical indication, the anatomy of the teeth, and the patient’s overall health. We evaluate all of this at your consultation.

Can I go back to work the next day?

It depends on the nature of your work. Many patients doing desk or remote work can return by day two or three, though day one post-surgery is generally not recommended for any work activity. If your job involves physical labor or strenuous activity, a longer recovery period applies. We give you realistic expectations based on your specific case at your consultation.

What is the risk of nerve damage with lower wisdom teeth?

The inferior alveolar nerve runs through the lower jaw in close proximity to some patients’ wisdom tooth roots. We use Cone Beam 3D imaging to measure this relationship precisely before surgery. In cases where roots are extremely close to the nerve, we discuss the associated risk transparently and can adjust the surgical plan accordingly, including techniques that reduce nerve exposure.

Do I need to have all four wisdom teeth out at once?

Not necessarily, though removing all four at once is the most common approach because it means a single recovery period and a single sedation event. If you have strong clinical reasons to remove only specific teeth, we can discuss a phased approach. The right plan depends on your imaging and individual clinical situation.

What causes dry socket and how do I avoid it?

Dry socket occurs when the blood clot that forms in the extraction socket is dislodged or dissolves before the site heals. It is more common in smokers and patients who drink through straws. Our post-operative instructions include specific guidance on avoiding dry socket, and we are available for prompt follow-up care if any complications arise during healing.
