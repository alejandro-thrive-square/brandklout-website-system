## Why Westwood Patients Choose to Act Now


Third molars are frequently identified during routine dental checkups at the student health center or a general dentist during a break, and the recommendation to see an oral surgeon often follows quickly. The question for most Westwood and UCLA patients is not whether to remove wisdom teeth but when. The answer, according to clinical guidance from the American Association of Oral and Maxillofacial Surgeons, is generally sooner rather than later. Earlier removal correlates with shorter root development, less dense bone, faster recovery, and reduced risk of the complications that come with fully mature, deeply impacted teeth.

For students who are home for the summer or between quarters, the timing window is valuable. Recovery from a standard four-tooth extraction is typically three to seven days, which is workable within most academic breaks. Missing two to three days of class during the semester is significantly more disruptive than scheduling around a break. Our team is accustomed to working with student and faculty schedules and makes every effort to accommodate the calendar realities of a university community.

Summer Break

The most popular window for students. Three to seven days of recovery is far more manageable during summer than mid-quarter. Book early since summer slots fill quickly.

Winter Recess

A shorter window, but sufficient for many straightforward cases. Some patients use winter break as an alternative when summer scheduling conflicts arise.

Spring Break

One week is typically enough for straightforward extractions. Recovery at home or on campus is manageable for most patients by day three or four.

Urgent Cases

When pain, swelling, or infection cannot wait for a scheduled break, we prioritize urgent cases and work to get you evaluated and treated promptly.


## Our Approach to Imaging and Surgical Planning


Every wisdom tooth case at our practice begins with a thorough evaluation using Cone Beam 3D imaging that reveals the precise position, angulation, and root anatomy of each tooth, as well as its relationship to the inferior alveolar nerve and other critical structures. For Westwood and UCLA patients who appreciate evidence-based care, this approach means surgical planning is grounded in precise data, not estimated on a two-dimensional film. We explain the imaging findings to you directly and discuss the specific factors that will shape your procedure before you commit to surgery.

We also provide our complete pre-operative guidelines and pre-anesthesia instructions well in advance so you arrive fully prepared. IV sedation is available for all wisdom tooth procedures and is administered by our board-certified surgeons.

## Frequently Asked Questions — Wisdom Teeth Removal near Westwood


How far is your office from UCLA?

We are approximately two miles east of UCLA’s main campus, accessible by heading east on Wilshire Boulevard or Sunset Boulevard. Our office is at 12300 Wilshire Blvd #326, Los Angeles, CA 90025, which is in the West Los Angeles corridor just east of Westwood.

Does UC SHIP dental cover wisdom teeth removal?

Coverage under UC SHIP or any dental plan depends on the specific plan and procedure. We verify your benefits before your appointment and give you a clear picture of what is covered and what you can expect to pay out of pocket. Contact our office with your insurance card and we can check coverage prior to your consultation.

How long should I wait after wisdom tooth removal before returning to class?

Most students feel ready to attend class within three to four days, assuming they are not in physical discomfort and can sit attentively for lecture or discussion. We recommend scheduling surgery so you have at least three full recovery days before any obligations resume. Strenuous physical activity, gym use, and sports should be avoided for the full first week.

What if my wisdom teeth are not causing pain — do I still need to remove them?

Not necessarily, but the absence of pain does not mean absence of a developing problem. Many impacted wisdom teeth cause no symptoms until they do — often in the form of an acute infection or damage to adjacent teeth that could have been avoided. We evaluate each case on its own imaging and recommend a course of action based on what we see, not on what you are currently feeling.

Can I study or use a computer during recovery?

Light screen use is generally fine after the first day, assuming your medication schedule and comfort level allow it. The first 24 hours post-surgery should be spent resting with your head elevated. Most students find they can manage light reading or remote coursework by day two or three, though extended focus periods may require short breaks due to residual soreness or medication effects.

Is IV sedation available for wisdom tooth removal?

Yes. IV sedation is available for all wisdom tooth procedures and is administered directly by our board-certified surgeons. Most patients who choose sedation have no memory of the procedure and recover comfortably at home. A responsible adult must drive you home and stay with you for the first few hours after your appointment.
