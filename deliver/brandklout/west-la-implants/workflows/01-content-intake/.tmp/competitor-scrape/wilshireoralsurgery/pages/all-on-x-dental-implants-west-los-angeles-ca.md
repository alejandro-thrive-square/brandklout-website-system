## Full-Arch Implant Restoration for West Los Angeles Patients


For patients in West Los Angeles who are missing most or all of their teeth in one arch, or who are facing imminent loss of failing dentition, full-arch implant restoration represents a fundamentally different category of treatment than individual implants. Using four to six titanium implants to support a fixed, full-arch prosthesis, this approach replaces an entire row of teeth in a single surgical phase. Patients sometimes hear this referred to as All-on-4, Teeth in a Day, or full-arch implants. Regardless of the name, the goal is the same: restoring full-arch function and appearance with a fixed, non-removable result supported by implants embedded in your own bone.

Wilshire Oral Surgery and Implant Center performs full-arch implant procedures from our office at 12300 Wilshire Blvd #326, Los Angeles, CA 90025, serving West LA patients who want board-certified surgical expertise and in-house imaging without traveling to a hospital or specialty center.


## Who Is a Candidate for Full-Arch Implant Restoration?


Full-arch implant restoration is designed for patients who have significant tooth loss, failing teeth that cannot be individually restored, or who are currently wearing or about to need a full denture. It is not appropriate for all patients, and candidacy depends on bone volume at the surgical sites, systemic health factors, and the patient’s specific goals and expectations.

We evaluate candidacy using Cone Beam 3D imaging to assess bone volume throughout the arch. Some patients require bone grafting in specific areas before implants can be placed. Others have sufficient bone for immediate placement. The consultation includes a thorough review of your imaging, dental history, medical history, and what the process will realistically involve for your specific case.

Failing Dentition

Patients with multiple teeth that cannot be individually restored and require full-arch extraction are strong candidates for same-phase implant placement.

Denture Dissatisfaction

Current denture wearers who experience poor fit, instability, or difficulty eating and speaking are frequently excellent candidates for implant-supported full-arch restoration.

Significant Bone Loss

Patients with significant bone resorption may still be candidates using angled implant placement to engage available bone volume, sometimes avoiding the need for extensive grafting.

Edentulous Patients

Patients who are already fully edentulous in one or both arches can proceed directly to implant evaluation without extraction planning.


## The Full-Arch Implant Process at Wilshire Oral Surgery and Implant Center


Full-arch restoration is a staged process, and understanding the timeline is important before you begin. In the surgical phase, any remaining failing teeth in the arch are extracted and four to six implants are placed. A temporary prosthesis is delivered on the same day or shortly after, allowing you to function with teeth during the healing period. This is where the “Teeth in a Day” description originates. The final, permanent prosthesis is fabricated after osseointegration is confirmed, typically several months later.

During the healing phase, patients follow a modified diet that avoids hard or chewy foods that could stress the implants before full integration. Our team provides detailed post-operative care instructions and monitoring appointments throughout the process. IV sedation or general anesthesia is used for the surgical phase to ensure patient comfort throughout what is a comprehensive procedure.

Comprehensive Evaluation

Full-arch 3D imaging, bone assessment, dental and medical history review, and candidacy determination. A clear timeline and cost estimate are provided.

Surgical Day

Extractions, implant placement, and temporary prosthesis delivery under IV sedation or general anesthesia. The surgical phase is completed in a single appointment.

Healing Phase

Osseointegration takes three to six months. You function on the temporary prosthesis during this period with a modified diet and scheduled follow-up appointments.

Final Prosthesis

After osseointegration is confirmed, the permanent prosthesis is fabricated and placed by your restorative provider, completing the full-arch restoration.

## Frequently Asked Questions — All-On-X Dental Implants near West Los Angeles


What is the difference between All-on-4 and All-on-X?

All-on-4 refers to a specific protocol using four implants to support a full-arch prosthesis. All-on-X is a broader term that encompasses variations using four, five, or six implants, depending on the patient’s bone volume and anatomy. The number of implants is determined based on your specific imaging and surgical plan, not a one-size-fits-all protocol.

Will I leave surgery with teeth?

In most cases, yes. A temporary fixed prosthesis is placed at the time of surgery or within a very short period afterward, allowing you to function with teeth during the osseointegration phase. This is what is meant by “Teeth in a Day.” The temporary prosthesis is later replaced with the final permanent prosthesis after healing is complete.

Is full-arch restoration the same as a denture?

No. A traditional full denture rests on the gum tissue and is removable. A full-arch implant restoration is fixed to titanium implants anchored in the bone. It does not shift, need adhesive, or come out for cleaning. It functions and feels significantly more like natural teeth than a removable denture.

How long does the full process take from consultation to final prosthesis?

The full process typically spans six to twelve months from consultation to final prosthesis delivery, depending on whether pre-surgical procedures such as bone grafting are needed and how quickly osseointegration progresses. We provide a realistic timeline at your consultation based on your specific imaging and treatment plan.

Can I finance full-arch implants?

Yes. We work with CareCredit, Alphaeon, and Cherry, all of which offer payment plans suited for high-value dental procedures. Extended low-interest or deferred-interest options are often available. We walk you through financing options at your consultation along with your insurance benefits verification and cost estimate.

What if I have been told I don’t have enough bone for implants?

Many patients who have been told they lack sufficient bone for implants are still candidates for full-arch restoration using angled implant placement techniques or bone grafting. We recommend a consultation and Cone Beam 3D imaging evaluation before accepting a general dentist’s conclusion that implants are not possible. The clinical picture is often more nuanced than a two-dimensional X-ray can reveal.
