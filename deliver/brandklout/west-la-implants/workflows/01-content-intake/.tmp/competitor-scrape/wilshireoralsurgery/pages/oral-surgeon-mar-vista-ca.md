## Frequently Asked Questions From Mar Vista Patients


How far is Wilshire Oral Surgery from Mar Vista?

Our office is located at 12300 Wilshire Blvd #326, Los Angeles, CA 90025. From central Mar Vista, the drive typically takes 10 to 15 minutes depending on traffic. The most direct routes are Centinela Avenue heading north to Wilshire Boulevard, Venice Boulevard east to Bundy Drive then north, or the 405 Freeway north to the Wilshire Boulevard exit.

Do I need a referral to see an oral surgeon?

No referral is required to schedule a consultation at Wilshire Oral Surgery and Implant Center. Many Mar Vista patients contact us directly after identifying a problem or receiving a recommendation from their general dentist. If your dentist has taken recent X-rays or provided records, bringing those to your appointment can help us plan more efficiently.

What sedation options are available for oral surgery?

We offer local anesthesia, IV sedation, and general anesthesia, all administered in-office by our surgeons. IV sedation and general anesthesia are appropriate for patients with dental anxiety, those undergoing multiple procedures in one visit, or complex cases requiring deeper anesthesia. Our team will discuss which option is safest and most appropriate for your specific procedure during your consultation.

How long does wisdom tooth removal recovery take?

Most patients recover from wisdom tooth removal within five to seven days, with the most noticeable swelling and discomfort occurring in the first two to three days. Patients who receive IV sedation will need a driver and should plan to rest the remainder of that day. We provide complete post-operative instructions and are available to answer recovery questions.

Does insurance cover dental implants or bone grafting?

Coverage varies by plan. Some PPO dental plans provide partial coverage for implant placement, particularly when tooth loss is the result of injury or a covered extraction. Bone grafting may be covered under medical insurance when it is linked to trauma or a medically necessary procedure. We review your specific plan before your appointment and provide a clear breakdown of covered versus out-of-pocket costs. See our insurance and financing page for more detail.

What makes an oral surgeon different from a general dentist for implants?

Oral and maxillofacial surgeons complete four to six years of accredited hospital surgical residency training after dental school, covering advanced anesthesia, complex implant cases, bone reconstruction, jaw surgery, and facial trauma. This depth of training is particularly important for patients with bone loss, anatomical challenges, or cases requiring sedation. General dentists can place implants in straightforward cases, but patients with more complex needs are typically best served by a board-certified oral surgeon. The American Association of Oral and Maxillofacial Surgeons offers additional information on implant procedures and what to expect from a specialist.
