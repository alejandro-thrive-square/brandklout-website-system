## Why Dental Implants Are the Right Long-Term Choice


Sawtelle patients who lose a tooth face the same decision as any patient: replace it with a bridge, a partial denture, or a dental implant. Of these options, a dental implant is the only one that replaces the tooth at the root level, stimulates the jawbone to prevent bone loss, and functions identically to a natural tooth without affecting adjacent healthy teeth. According to the National Library of Medicine, dental implants are among the most well-documented restorative options in dentistry, with a decades-long clinical record supporting their effectiveness.

Bridges replace the visible tooth but require reducing the adjacent teeth on either side to serve as anchors. Partial dentures restore function but are removable, can shift or require adhesive, and do nothing to preserve the underlying bone. For patients who want a solution they do not have to think about, clean any differently from natural teeth, or replace periodically, an implant is consistently the better long-term choice.

Preserves Neighboring Teeth

An implant stands independently. No adjacent teeth are reduced or used as anchors, meaning your healthy neighboring teeth remain completely intact.

Prevents Bone Loss

The titanium implant stimulates the jawbone through osseointegration, stopping the bone resorption that begins after tooth loss and accelerates under bridges and dentures.

No Maintenance Difference

An implant-supported crown is brushed and flossed like a natural tooth. No removals, no adhesives, no overnight soaking.

Designed to Last Decades

Implants are intended as a permanent solution. With proper care, the implant fixture itself rarely requires replacement, unlike a bridge that typically needs replacement eventually.


## The Implant Process at Wilshire Oral Surgery and Implant Center


The first step is a consultation that includes Cone Beam 3D imaging, which creates a precise three-dimensional map of the implant site, bone volume, nerve location, and surrounding anatomy. This imaging allows us to plan the implant’s position, depth, and angle with accuracy before any surgical step begins. We explain our findings clearly and answer your questions before you commit to any treatment.

If the implant site needs bone volume augmentation, we perform bone grafting as part of the process, sometimes on the same visit as implant placement depending on the case. IV sedation is available for patients who prefer it, administered by our board-certified surgeons in-office. After osseointegration, your general dentist or prosthodontist places the final crown. Our post-operative care instructions guide you through each recovery phase.

Consultation + 3D Imaging

We map your bone volume and anatomy precisely. A treatment plan with timeline and costs is provided before you commit to any procedure.

Grafting if Needed

When bone volume is insufficient, bone grafting rebuilds the site for a stable, long-term implant foundation.

Implant Placement

The titanium implant is placed under local anesthesia or IV sedation. Most patients return to normal activity within two to three days.

Final Crown

After osseointegration is confirmed, your restorative dentist places the crown. The implant is now permanently integrated.

## Frequently Asked Questions — Dental Implants near Sawtelle


Is your office walkable from Sawtelle Avenue?

Yes. Our office is at 12300 Wilshire Blvd #326, which is less than a ten-minute walk from the Sawtelle Japantown corridor along Sawtelle Avenue. For patients driving, building parking is available in our office complex.

Do you have staff who speak Spanish or Farsi?

Yes. Our team communicates in English, Spanish, and Farsi/Persian. If you or a family member would prefer to discuss your treatment plan, pre-operative instructions, or post-operative care in Spanish or Farsi, we can accommodate that. Clear communication throughout the implant process is important to us.

How many appointments does an implant require?

Typically, you will have a consultation and imaging appointment, an implant placement appointment, and follow-up visits during the osseointegration phase before your crown is placed by your general dentist. If bone grafting is required, that may be a separate appointment or combined with placement depending on the case. Being close to our office makes the multi-appointment process considerably more convenient.

Can I get a dental implant if I have gum disease?

Untreated gum disease is a contraindication to implant placement because periodontal bacteria increase the risk of peri-implantitis and implant failure. If you have active gum disease, it must be treated and brought under control before implant surgery is appropriate. We review your periodontal status at your consultation and coordinate with your general dentist as needed.

Do you accept IEHP or Medi-Cal for implants?

We accept commercial PPO dental insurance plans. We do not currently participate in Medi-Cal dental for implant procedures. For patients without PPO coverage, we offer financing through CareCredit, Alphaeon, and Cherry to make treatment more accessible. Contact us to discuss your specific situation.

Is the area around the implant site sensitive during osseointegration?

Some patients notice mild tenderness or sensitivity at the implant site during the early integration phase, which is normal and typically manageable with standard pain medication. If sensitivity becomes significant or is accompanied by swelling, unusual discharge, or looseness at the implant site, contact us promptly. Routine follow-up appointments during the healing phase allow us to monitor the implant site regularly.
