The QuestionWho is actually designing your new smile?

In many dental offices, the laboratory decides much of what your final teeth will look like. Implant patients may see one doctor for surgery, another for the restoration, and a lab taking instructions from both. Our philosophy is different.

Dr. Bensoussan personally develops the diagnosis, the treatment plan, the smile design, the bite, the implant positioning and the provisional restorations. The laboratory executes his design, not the other way round.

The DesignSeen before it exists

You wear the smile before it is made.

  1. Your smile is designed around your face, lips, tooth proportions, gum architecture, smile line, speech, bite and function.

  2. For many veneer and crown cases we 3D-print the design as provisional restorations. You wear the proposed smile while we refine shape, length, speech and bite.

  3. Only then are the final ceramics made. The laboratory receives the position, proportions, contours, shade and surface texture, and executes that design.

The PathWhat actually happens

Six steps, one doctor.

01/ 06

  1. Step 01

    Listen and diagnose

    We spend significant time diagnosing, planning and explaining before anything is treated. This is a high-touch specialty practice, not a high-volume implant center.

  2. Step 02

    Record in 3D

    3D CBCT imaging and TRIOS digital intraoral scanning capture your bone, teeth and bite, without impression trays.

  3. Step 03

    Design the teeth first

    With Digital Smile Design, the final teeth are designed first. Implants are then planned around the position that smile, bite and function require.

  4. Step 04

    Try it on

    Provisionals are 3D-printed in-office on SprintRay printers, so you experience the design. Appropriate full-arch patients leave with same-day fixed teeth.

  5. Step 05

    Surgery, same hands

    Surgery and restoration are managed by the same prosthodontist, guided by X-Guide dynamic 3D navigation and FastMap photogrammetry.

  6. Step 06

    Finish, and stand behind it

    Final ceramics come from laboratories chosen for their individual strengths, with a results-based warranty on qualifying surgical, cosmetic, and restorative treatment.

  7. “The technology itself is not the differentiator. How we use it to execute a carefully designed plan is what matters.”

A prosthodontist is a dentist who completed an additional, accredited specialty residency in restoring and replacing teeth. Dr. Bensoussan spent three more years in the UCSF Prosthodontics program after dental school, training in implant surgery and restoration, porcelain veneers and ceramics, full-mouth reconstruction and bite rehabilitation. A general dentist covers everyday care; a prosthodontist is the specialist for smile makeovers, dental implants and complex cases.
