BiofilmWhere the trouble starts

Biofilm is a sticky layer of bacteria that forms on teeth and gums. Left alone, it forms plaque. It is the root cause of cavities, gum disease and bad breath.

Your CleaningWhat happens in the chair

What happens at your cleaning.

  • Our well-trained hygienist gives you a gentle, thorough teeth cleaning.
  • Dr. Bensoussan tests your gums for any gum disease.
  • If there is any, a deep cleaning removes the calculus below the gum line that lets bacteria flourish.
  • We use warm water to make your experience comfortable.
  • Our hygienist shows you how to keep your teeth and gums in optimum condition at home.

How We CleanPlaque, tartar, bacteria

Each layer, removed its own way.

First · Guided Biofilm Therapy

An air polisher for biofilm

A specialized air polisher sprays a gentle stream of air, warm water and fine powder to remove the biofilm and plaque from tooth surfaces, gums and around implants.

It also makes your teeth brighter and smoother.

Then · Ultrasonic scaler

Tartar and stains, gently broken apart

Once the plaque is gone, an ultrasonic scaler removes stains and tartar (hardened plaque), and below the gum line if there is also gum disease.

Its vibrations break the deposits apart, and warm water flushes the debris and bacteria away.

A diagnostic tool · Saliva testing

Measuring the bacteria

The test measures the amount of toxic bacteria currently in your mouth, so Dr. Bensoussan can assess whether you are at high risk for gum disease and decay.

It also helps him assess whether existing gum damage or bone loss means gum disease is active.

Deep CleaningFor gum disease

When the gum pulls away.

We treat mild to moderate gum disease using an ultrasonic and a hand scaler to remove the tartar and plaque accumulated below the gum line.

How deep cleaning works

Plaque and tartar build-up cause the gum to move away from the tooth, creating pockets.

Both the buildup and the pockets can harbor harmful bacteria that, untreated, can cause inflammation leading to gum recession and bone loss.

Scaling removes the plaque and tartar. Planing smooths the root to prevent future bacteria growth.
