The CauseVery often, the bite

Pain in the jaw and jaw joint, as well as recurring headaches, are very often caused by a bad bite: how your teeth come together.

The SignsWhen the joint struggles

What a troubled jaw joint can feel like.

  • Clicking, popping or locking of the jaw.
  • Pain and inflammation in the joint itself.
  • Recurring headaches, and neck and face pain.
  • Muscle fatigue, spasms and pain in the head, neck or ear, as the muscles overcompensate to stabilize the joint.

Recurrent jaw pain or headaches? Come in for a full evaluation of your TMJ, your bite and any teeth grinding.

Three ReasonsWhy the joint suffers

The main reasons for TMJ problems.

Part of the TMJ is a small, flexible piece of cartilage between the skull and the jaw bone that cushions them. When it is displaced, the bones can rub together, leading to inflammation.

A bad bite

When the teeth don't align properly, the jaw can close unevenly, putting strain on the joint and the surrounding muscles. It can also shift the cushioning cartilage disc out of position.

Teeth grinding or clenching (bruxism)

Chronic clenching or grinding puts repetitive pressure on the TMJ, which can lead to strain, wear and displacement of the cushioning cartilage. A lot of bruxism occurs while a person sleeps.

Structural issues

A less common cause of TMJ pain: uneven jaw development, arthritis and trauma.

How It WorksDiagnosis, then treatment

From a full examination to your treatment.

Based on what he finds, Dr. Bensoussan prepares your treatment.

  1. Full diagnostic examination

    Your jaw joints and your bite

    A full clinical examination of the jaw joints and your bite, with digital x-rays and 3D x-rays. We also check whether you have been grinding or clenching your teeth.

  2. Study

    A simulation of your TMJ and bite

    Dr. Bensoussan studies the results and simulates what is happening with your TMJ and bite, and how to fix them.

  3. Diagnosis

    Bite, grinding, or the joint

    He establishes if the problem is primarily your bite, teeth grinding or clenching, or strictly a jaw joint problem.

  4. Treatment

    A special kind of mouthguard

    Typical treatment can include a mouthguard made to put your jaw in the correct position, or to prevent teeth grinding or clenching.

  5. For the bite

    Correcting how the teeth meet

    For issues with your bite, Dr. Bensoussan can correct it by repositioning your teeth with crowns or bridges, or with teeth shortening.
