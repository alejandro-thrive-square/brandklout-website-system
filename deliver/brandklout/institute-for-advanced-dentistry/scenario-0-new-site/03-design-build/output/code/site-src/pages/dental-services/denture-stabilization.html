Level 1 · Snap-in dentures

Small attachments that snap on

Two implants with little attachments that snap onto the denture: much stronger retention than a traditional denture, no adhesive in most cases, and easier eating and speaking.

In the softer bone of the upper jaw, four implants are needed for enough support.

Level 2 · Bar overdenture

A denture on a titanium bar

The denture attaches to a custom titanium bar joined to two or four implants. With two, it shifts less under pressure than snap-ins.

With four, it feels close to a fixed bridge: the upper palate can often be removed, chewing is significantly stronger and bone is preserved around the implants.

Level 3 · New Teeth in One Day

Fixed-in permanent teeth

For the ultimate in natural feel, function and strength: a new set of fixed teeth on four to five implants. No shifting, no adhesive ever, no false palate, full chewing power.

Who It's ForEvery denture wearer

Recommended for all denture wearers.

  • Loose or slipping dentures: stabilization makes a meaningful difference in comfort and function.
  • A denture that could fall out unexpectedly, now held firmly.
  • Weak chewing: retention and chewing power improve significantly.
  • Severe bone loss: a snap-in denture is often still possible, since two implants are typically enough.

Stabilizing a denture can also help slow bone loss. You can choose to stabilize only your lower denture.

Bone LossWhy dentures loosen

A denture loosens as the bone shrinks.

Denture stabilization can stop that process, and it is planned by a specialist in implants.

Bone loss and loose dentures

Too little stimulus, less bone

Long-time denture wearers often lose jawbone, because a denture gives the bone too little chewing stimulus. As the bone goes, the denture loosens.

Relining helps only for a short time. A traditional denture presses on the gums without stimulating the bone, and the lower face can cave in, giving a prematurely aged look.
