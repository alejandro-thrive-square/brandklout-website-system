The First Tooth

The American Academy of Pediatric Dentistry has found that your child should visit the dentist within six months after the presence of the first tooth or by his or her first birthday.

Early oral examination aids in the detection of the initial stages of tooth decay. Early detection is also essential to maintaining good oral health. Tooth decay has become the most common chronic childhood illness in the U.S.

That first visit is not a chair and a bright light. It happens on your lap, knee to knee with Dr. Nguyen, and it is over before anyone has had time to worry.

Before you book

When should my child first see a dentist?
Children should visit a pediatric dentist by age one or within six months of their first tooth. This early visit helps prevent dental issues and establishes a positive relationship with the dentist.
Can I stay with my child during the appointment?
Yes! Parents are always welcome to accompany their child if it helps them feel more comfortable.
How do I prepare a nervous child for the first visit?
Use positive language, read dental-themed books, and bring comfort items like a blanket or toy. Avoid words like “hurt” or “pain.”
Do you take my insurance?
Mason Park Dental accepts most PPO insurance plans and Medicaid for qualified patients. We also offer transparent billing and flexible financing. See insurance and financing for the in-network list.
