Is This You

Three reasons families book this visit.

Comprehensive pediatric dentistry is the exam, the cleaning and the conversation. It is where every other treatment on this site begins, and for most children it is all they ever need.

1

A first tooth, and no dentist yet.

The American Academy of Pediatric Dentistry has found that your child should visit the dentist within six months after the presence of the first tooth or by his or her first birthday.

2

It has been more than six months.

Regular cleanings and exams can help prevent tooth decay, gum disease, and other oral health issues. Tooth decay has become the most common chronic childhood illness in the U.S.

3

A child who has had a bad visit elsewhere.

We provide treatment in a fun, gentle, and relaxing environment. Infants and anxious toddlers are examined knee to knee, on your lap, and you stay in the room.

The Visit

What a visit includes.

Early detection

Early oral examination aids in the detection of the initial stages of tooth decay. Early detection is also essential to maintaining good oral health.

During each visit, we will gently clean your child’s teeth and remove any plaque or tartar. We will carefully examine their mouth and teeth for any potential issues.

Teaching, not telling

We will teach you and your child how to properly brush and floss their teeth as they grow. We will discuss dental hygiene habits and healthy food choices. We will teach your child to be our partner in keeping their mouth and teeth healthy.

Fluoride and sealants, when they help

Two things the practice may recommend

  • Fluoride. This mineral can strengthen tooth enamel, making teeth more resistant to decay. Fluoride may be applied in office or used in certain toothpastes and mouth rinses.
  • Sealants. A protective coating applied to the chewing surfaces of molars. Recent studies have found that sealants can be highly effective in preventing tooth decay.

What the exam checks

During your child’s examination, we will also

  • Evaluate their overall oral health
  • Discuss oral hygiene practices and habits
  • Check their bite and jaw for any issues
  • Evaluate their risk of tooth decay, gum disease, and oral cancer
  • Evaluate existing tooth restorations
  • Take dental x-rays if necessary
More on cleanings

Professional cleanings rid the teeth of harmful plaque and tartar build-up. This is important for preventing tooth decay, periodontal disease, and other oral health issues for your child. Additionally, a professional cleaning by our hygienist may remove some surface stains. This can help keep your child’s teeth looking whiter and brighter.

The Path

Five steps, about forty minutes.

1

Forms, before you arrive.

Our patient forms can be filled out online, or downloaded and filled out at home. If you cannot, arrive 20 minutes early and the front desk will help.

2

Your lap, or the chair.

Infants and anxious toddlers are examined knee to knee. Older children get the chair, the screen on the wall, and an explanation before anything happens.

3

The cleaning.

Gentle plaque removal, polishing, fluoride application, and an oral exam to check teeth, gums, and bite development. X-rays only if necessary.

4

The brushing lesson.

How to brush and floss at this age, what to do about bottles, pacifiers and snacks, and the answers to whatever you wrote down on the way in.

5

The plan, and the next date.

Anything the exam found, explained on the screen, with the price before you leave. Then the next six month visit goes in the diary.

Questions

What parents ask before the first visit.

From the practice’s own pediatric dentistry FAQ, word for word.

When should my child first visit the dentist?
Children should visit a pediatric dentist by age one or within six months of their first tooth. This early visit helps prevent dental issues and establishes a positive relationship with the dentist.
How often should kids come in?
Kids should come in every six months for cleanings and checkups. Some children may need more frequent visits depending on their oral health needs.
Why a pediatric dentist rather than a family dentist?
Pediatric dentists receive specialized training in child behavior, growth, development, and children’s oral health needs, making them the best choice for infants, kids, and teens.
What happens during a cleaning?
A cleaning includes gentle plaque removal, polishing, fluoride application, and an oral exam to check teeth, gums, and bite development.
Is fluoride safe for children?
Yes! Fluoride is safe and helps strengthen tooth enamel, making teeth more resistant to cavities. We customize fluoride use based on your child’s age and needs.
Do baby teeth really matter?
Absolutely. Baby teeth help with speech development, chewing, and guiding permanent teeth into proper alignment.
How do I prepare a nervous child?
Use positive language, read dental-themed books, and bring comfort items like a blanket or toy. Avoid words like “hurt” or “pain.”
