Restorative dentistry

Saving the tooth by treating what is inside it.

Pulp therapy treats the center, or pulp, of a tooth that is inflamed, infected or dead. The pulp is a soft substance consisting of nerves, blood vessels and connective tissue.

The Treatment

Two forms, and how to tell it is needed.

The symptoms

Symptoms of infection include visible injury or swelling, sensitivity to temperature, or pain in the tooth and gums. If your child is experiencing any of these symptoms, contact Mason Park Dental for a consultation.

Root canal therapy

There are two forms of pulp therapy. The first, root canal therapy, involves removing the pulp from the tooth chamber and root canals. We then clean the canals with a disinfecting solution. During root canal therapy, we may provide local anesthesia or sedation to ensure complete comfort.

Pulp therapy on a baby tooth

The second, pulp therapy, is performed on a primary (baby) tooth with extensive decay but no evidence of root involvement. Dr. Nguyen will remove the infected pulp from the tooth without touching the roots. The tooth is then treated with medication and packing is placed into the tooth.

Afterwards

The next few days.

After treatment, we may place a temporary filling to protect their tooth until the restoration is complete. Your child’s tooth may feel sensitive for the first few days. This discomfort may be relieved with over-the-counter or prescribed pain medications, as directed. Your child should avoid chewing on the treated tooth until they have their full restoration. If a problem occurs, contact Mason Park Dental.

Why it is worth doing

Pulp therapy can help preserve your child’s tooth for a lifetime.
