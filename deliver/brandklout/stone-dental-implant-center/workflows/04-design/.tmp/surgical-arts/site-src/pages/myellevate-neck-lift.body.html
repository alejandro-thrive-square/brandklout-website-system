01 · What it does

The suture does the lifting, so nothing is cut away.

Most people who come in about their neck are not asking for a facelift. They want the line under the chin back, the one that makes a jaw look like a jaw, and they cannot take three weeks off to get it. This is the procedure that sits in that gap.

Surface Skin Fat Muscle Deepest

The platysma, suspended

A light-guided suture is passed beneath the skin and used to suspend the platysma, the sheet of muscle that gives the neck its shape. Because the suture does the lifting, there is no external incision, and because nothing is cut away, the result is a repositioning of what you already have rather than a removal.

Where the muscle has separated and the skin has gone past holding, a suture is not enough. That is the surgical neck lift, and it is the longer, dearer conversation.

Submental fat, if it is there

Where fullness under the chin is fat rather than muscle, submental liposuction is done in the same visit through the same small entry point, so the line the suspension creates has a clean edge to sit against.

Fat, muscle, or both: which it is on you is measured at the consultation rather than guessed from a photograph.

The skin, left where it is

Nothing is removed. This procedure depends on skin that still has tone, because it redrapes over the suspended muscle rather than being trimmed. Where the skin needs help of its own, FaceTite tightens it by radiofrequency in the same visit, priced together at $7,800.

Skin with genuine excess will not take up over a suture, and that is the single test that decides between this and a neck lift.

02 · Who it suits

It works when the skin still takes up.

It works best where the skin still has reasonable tone and the problem is laxity in the muscle rather than a genuine excess of skin. In practice that tends to mean people in their late thirties to their fifties, and people of any age who have lost weight and been left with a slack neck.

03 · How it is done

A light-guided suture, through entry points you will struggle to find.

Under local anaesthetic, a suture is passed beneath the skin along a path lit from inside by a fibre-optic wand, so Dr. Stone can see exactly where it runs. The suture is looped around the platysma and drawn up to suspend it, restoring the angle under the chin and the border along the jaw.

There is no external incision along the neck. The entry points, typically under the chin and behind the ears, are small enough to settle to very little. Where fat needs removing or skin needs tightening, submental liposuction or FaceTite is done in the same sitting through the same points.

About ninety minutes in the chair. Under local anaesthetic alone the practice’s own guidance is that you can drive yourself home; if sedation is added, a responsible adult brings you and drives you home, as for any sedated procedure.

04 · Recovery

An afternoon, a week, and a season of settling.

The figures below are conventional ranges and they are what you should plan around. Dr. Stone will give you the numbers for your own neck at the consultation.

  1. The day

    About ninety minutes in the chair

    Local anaesthetic, with sedation if you want it. You go home the same afternoon, and if you were not sedated you can drive yourself.

  2. Days two and three

    A support garment, some tightness

    Swelling and a feeling of tightness under the chin, and very little pain in most cases. The garment does its work while you do very little.

  3. Inside a week

    Back at a desk

    Most people are back at a desk inside a week and comfortable in company shortly after. Plan for the week and come back early if it goes well.

  4. Weeks six to twelve

    The contour settles

    The line keeps refining as swelling resolves and the tissues soften around the suspension. Follow-ups are with Dr. Stone, not with a nurse you have not met.

07 · Questions

The six questions this procedure raises.

Will there be a visible scar?

There is no external incision along the neck. The suture is passed through small entry points, typically under the chin and behind the ears, which settle to be very difficult to find.

How long does it last?

The practice puts MyEllevate® at around five years, against up to ten for a full surgical neck lift. The suspension itself is permanent; what changes is that your neck keeps ageing around it. Skin quality, weight stability and genetics all move that number, and Dr. Stone will give you a realistic figure for your neck rather than the average.

Can I drive myself home afterwards?

Under local anaesthetic alone, yes: the practice’s own guidance is that you can drive yourself home and resume most activities straight away. If sedation is added, a responsible adult brings you, waits, and drives you home, as for a neck lift.

Is this the same as a thread lift?

No. A thread lift places barbed threads in the soft tissue and dissolves over months. MyEllevate® places a permanent suture around the platysma itself, under direct light guidance, and it is a surgical procedure performed by a surgeon.

Can I have it if I have already had a facelift?

Often, yes: it is a common way to refresh a neck without repeating a larger operation. Bring the operative details of the previous surgery to your consultation if you have them.

What does it cost, and can I finance it?
