Lifting a neck gives you back the angle between the chin and the throat. It cannot give you a jaw that the skeleton never built. Where the mandible itself lacks width or definition, no amount of suspension, liposuction or skin tightening will produce an edge, because there is nothing underneath for the soft tissue to drape over.

A jawline implant addresses the bone directly. It is a solid, precisely shaped augmentation placed along the mandibular border, and it creates the border the face is missing.

Who it suits

The clearest sign is a patient who is lean, has very little under the chin to remove, and still has no jawline. Subtraction has nothing to work with; the deficiency is structural.

  • A jaw that reads as soft or rounded in profile at any weight
  • A face that has been told it needs liposuction under the chin when there is barely any fat there
  • Asymmetry along the mandible that has been present since childhood
  • A wish for definition that filler along the jaw has never convincingly produced

This is the operation for the patient who has done everything else and still does not have a jawline. Dr. Stone is known online as @drjawline, and this is a good part of the reason.

Often combined with

The implant provides the frame; the soft tissue over it is a separate question:

  • MyEllevate® or a neck lift, where there is laxity as well as a deficient border.
  • Chin implant, where projection is needed at the front as well as along the sides. Frequently the same custom piece.
  • Submental liposuction, where a small amount of fat is blurring an edge the implant will create.

Custom, and reversible

A jawline implant is planned against your own skeletal anatomy rather than chosen from a handful of stock shapes. That is what separates a jaw that reads as yours from one that reads as hardware.

It is also removable. If it is ever not right, it comes out and the anatomy returns to where it started, which is a genuine advantage over permanent changes that cannot be undone.

Placing hardware on facial bone through the mouth is the centre of oral and maxillofacial surgery rather than an extension of it. Dr. Stone trained in that specialty across three Level 1 trauma centres.

The procedure

What the day actually looks like.

01

In theatre

Two to three hours under general anaesthetic. Access is through the mouth, so there is no external incision. You go home the same day with a responsible adult.

02

The first fortnight

Swelling is substantial for the first week and it is the part patients find hardest, because the jaw looks wider rather than sharper. Soft diet, careful oral hygiene, and most people are back at work at ten to fourteen days.

03

Settling

The definition emerges as swelling resolves over two to three months. Numbness of the lower lip and chin is common early and resolves as the nerve recovers.

Questions

What people ask before they book.

Why not jawline filler?

Filler is soft, it sits above the bone rather than replacing it, and on a jaw that lacks skeletal projection it tends to add width and heaviness rather than an edge. It also needs repeating indefinitely. An implant provides a hard border because a hard border is what is missing.

Can it be taken out?

Yes. It sits in a defined pocket and removal is a smaller procedure than placement. The anatomy returns to its starting point, which is not true of most permanent facial surgery.

Will it be obvious that I have had something done?

A well planned implant reads as bone, because it is doing the job bone would have done. The risk of an obvious result comes from oversizing, which is a planning decision made against your measurements before anything is ordered or placed.

Is there a scar?

Not on the face. The implant is placed through incisions inside the mouth, closed with dissolving sutures.

What happens at the consultation?

You and Dr. Stone go through your concerns and your goals, she takes a medical history and assesses your general health, and photographs and measurements are taken so the plan is drawn against your own anatomy. You leave knowing whether this is the right procedure for you, and if it is not, which one is.
