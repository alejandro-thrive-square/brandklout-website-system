The buccal fat pad is a defined structure sitting in the hollow of the cheek, separate from the fat immediately under the skin. In some faces it is large enough to blunt the line between cheekbone and jaw, producing fullness that has nothing to do with weight and does not respond to losing any.

Reducing it is a short procedure through a small incision inside the mouth. There is no external scar and the recovery is measured in days.

Who it suits

The candidate is someone with genuine buccal fullness and good underlying bone structure, where removing the pad reveals a cheekbone that is already there.

  • Fullness in the hollow of the cheek that has persisted at every weight
  • A mid-face that photographs wider than it appears in the mirror
  • A cheekbone whose line is blunted rather than absent

It is a poor choice for a thin face, an older face, or anyone whose fullness is skin and general soft tissue rather than the pad itself. Faces lose volume with age, and removing structural fat from a face that will thin anyway produces a gaunt, hollowed look a decade later that is very difficult to reverse.

The honest caveat

This procedure is popular for reasons that have more to do with photography than with anatomy, and it is permanent. Fat that is removed does not return, and a face that reads as sculpted at thirty can read as drawn at forty-five.

Dr. Stone declines this operation more often than she performs it, and will say so directly if your anatomy is not right for it. Where the fullness is soft tissue rather than the pad, or where the underlying structure is the actual deficiency, a cheek implant or facial liposuction addresses the real problem and leaves you with more, not less, as you age.

Often combined with

Removing fat reveals whatever structure is underneath, so it is frequently planned with something that improves that structure:

  • Cheek implants, to give the definition a platform rather than relying on subtraction alone.
  • Facial liposuction, where there is also fullness along the jaw and under the chin.

The procedure

What the day actually looks like.

01

In the chair

Around forty-five minutes under local anaesthetic with sedation. A small incision inside each cheek, the pad delivered and reduced, dissolving sutures. Home the same day.

02

The first week

Swelling makes the face look fuller rather than slimmer for the first week, which surprises people who were not warned. Soft diet and salt water rinses. Most are back at work in three to five days.

03

Settling

The contour emerges slowly and the final result is a three to six month proposition. Judging it at three weeks tells you very little.

Questions

What people ask before they book.

Will I look hollow when I am older?

That is the real risk of this operation and it is the reason for the conservative approach. Faces lose volume with age, and a pad removed in your twenties is not available in your fifties. The mitigation is careful patient selection and removing less than the maximum. If your face is likely to thin, Dr. Stone will tell you not to have it.

Is it permanent?

Yes. The buccal fat pad does not regenerate. This is the main reason it is planned conservatively: the operation can always be taken further at a later date, and it cannot be undone.

Will I have a scar?

Not externally. The incision is inside the mouth, closed with dissolving sutures, and heals within a couple of weeks.

Can I get the same effect without surgery?

Not from the buccal pad itself, which is too deep to be reached by anything topical or energy-based. If the fullness is superficial fat rather than the pad, other options exist, and establishing which one you have is the point of the assessment.

What happens at the consultation?

You and Dr. Stone go through your concerns and your goals, she takes a medical history and assesses your general health, and photographs and measurements are taken so the plan is drawn against your own anatomy. You leave knowing whether this is the right procedure for you, and if it is not, which one is.
