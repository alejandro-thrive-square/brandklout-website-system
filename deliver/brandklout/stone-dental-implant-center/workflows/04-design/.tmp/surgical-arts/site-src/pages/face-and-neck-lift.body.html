01 · What it does

A face lift is not about pulling skin tight.

Skin is the last layer, and treating it as the problem is how a face ends up looking operated on rather than rested. The work happens underneath, in the layer of muscle and fascia that has descended, and the skin is redraped over the result rather than dragged into position.

Surface Skin Fat SMAS Deepest

The SMAS, repositioned

The sheet of muscle and fascia under the skin of the face is what has descended, and it is what is lifted and fixed back where it sat. In the neck the same layer is the platysma, repaired in the midline where it has separated. This is the layer that holds, and it is why the result lasts.

Where the descent is real but the skin still has tone, the neck can sometimes be answered with a suture alone. That is the MyEllevate® conversation, and being pointed at it is a normal outcome here.

The fat, put back where it belongs

A jowl is fat that has slid below the jawline; a hollow cheek is fat that has left. Both are addressed with the deeper layer rather than treated as the problem on their own, and fullness under the chin is contoured through the same neck incision.

Where the mid-face has genuinely lost volume, fat transfer can be planned alongside. It is decided at the consultation, on your anatomy.

The skin, redraped

Once the deeper layer is where it belongs, the skin is laid back over it and only the genuine excess is removed. No tension is carried in the skin, which is the difference between a face that looks rested and one that looks pulled.

A lift restores position. It does not change the quality of the skin on top of it, which is what resurfacing is for, and it is often staged afterwards.

03 · How it is done

In front of the ear, around the lobe, and back into the hairline.

The incisions follow the natural creases in front of the ear, around the earlobe and back into the hairline behind it, with a small one under the chin for the neck. Through them the SMAS is lifted and fixed back where it sat, the platysma is repaired where it has separated, fat is contoured along the jaw and under the chin, and the skin is redraped over the result with only the genuine excess removed.

Four to six hours under general anaesthetic, depending on what is being combined. You are monitored afterwards and go home the same day with a responsible adult, or stay overnight if that is the safer plan for you.

Scars are placed where the anatomy conceals them and they mature over months from pink to pale. Scar care starts as soon as the wounds are closed and is part of the plan rather than an afterthought.

04 · Recovery

A day, a fortnight, and a year of settling.

The figures below are conventional ranges and they are what you should plan around. Dr. Stone will give you the numbers for your own face at the consultation.

  1. The day

    Four to six hours in theatre

    Under general anaesthetic. A responsible adult brings you, waits, and drives you home, or you stay the night if that is the safer plan. You do not drive for twenty-four hours afterwards.

  2. Day three

    Bruising and swelling peak, then fall away

    A support garment, tightness, and a face that looks worse than it feels. Day three is the worst of it and it improves quickly after that, which is worth knowing in advance.

  3. Week two

    Sutures out, presentable in company

    Sutures come out in stages across the first two weeks. Most people are presentable to people who are not looking for it at around two weeks, and back at a desk at two to three. No lifting or sharp head turns for six weeks.

  4. Months six to twelve

    The result settles

    It keeps refining as swelling resolves and scars mature. Follow-ups are with Dr. Stone, not with a nurse you have not met, and they go on for up to eighteen months.

07 · Questions

The six questions this operation raises.

Where do the scars go, and will people see them?

They follow the natural creases in front of the ear, around the earlobe and back into the hairline behind it. They are placed where the anatomy hides them and they mature over months from pink to pale. Scar care starts as soon as the wounds are closed and is part of your plan, not an afterthought.

How long will it last?

A well-executed lift resets the clock rather than stopping it. Most people look substantially better than their unoperated baseline for a decade or more, and you continue to age normally from the new starting point. Skin quality, weight stability, sun exposure and genetics all move that figure, and Dr. Stone will give you a realistic number for your face rather than an average.

Will I look like a different person?

No, and if a result looks like a different person then something has gone wrong with the planning. The aim is that people register that you look well rather than that you look altered. That is a matter of how much tension is placed on which layer, and it is decided in the operating theatre rather than promised beforehand.

Can I have just the neck?

Yes. The neck lift is quoted separately at $11,000, and for the right anatomy MyEllevate® at $6,000 achieves a great deal with no external incision. Which of the three you need is the substance of the consultation.

Can I combine it with eyelid surgery or resurfacing?

Often, and it is frequently the better plan. A lifted face with tired eyes reads as tired, so blepharoplasty is commonly planned alongside; laser resurfacing reaches the texture and pigment a lift cannot, and is usually staged rather than done the same day. Both are decided at the consultation, on your anatomy, and neither is sold as a package beforehand.
