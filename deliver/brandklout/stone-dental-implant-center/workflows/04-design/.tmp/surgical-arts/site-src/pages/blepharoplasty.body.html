People look at eyes. It is the first thing a face is read by, and it is where fatigue registers whether or not you are tired. Eyelid surgery removes or repositions the tissue that has made the eye look smaller and heavier than it is.

It is a short operation with a quick recovery and a very high satisfaction rate, which is why it is often the first procedure someone has and frequently the only one they need.

What this treats

Who it suits

Upper and lower lids are different problems and they are assessed separately, though both are commonly done in one sitting.

Where the real problem is a descended brow resting on the lid, the answer is a brow lift, and removing lid skin instead would be the wrong operation done well. Dr. Stone will hold your brow in position at the consultation and show you the difference.

  • 01

    Upper lid: skin that folds over the crease, sits on the lashes, or in marked cases begins to interfere with the outer field of vision

  • 02

    Lower lid: bags and puffiness that are present from the moment you wake, rather than after a bad night

  • 03

    Hollowing beneath the eye that makes the cheek and lid read as two separate structures

  • 04

    Makeup that will no longer sit on the upper lid because there is nowhere flat for it to go

Often combined with

Eyelid surgery addresses structure. It does not change the quality of the skin around it:

  • Brow lift, where both are contributing to the same heaviness.
  • Laser resurfacing of the lid skin, for the fine crepe that surgery cannot tighten.
  • Face and neck lift, where the eyes are being brought in line with a larger plan.

The part that is easy to get wrong

Lower lids punish over-resection. Take too much skin or too much fat and the lid pulls away from the eye, which is difficult to correct and immediately obvious. Modern technique is therefore conservative and increasingly about repositioning fat into the hollow beneath rather than removing it.

Dr. Stone trained in oral and maxillofacial surgery across three Level 1 trauma centres, which is where the orbit and the tissues around it are reconstructed after injury. It is a demanding way to learn an anatomy that leaves very little room for error.

The procedure

What the day actually looks like.

01

In the chair

One to two hours under local anaesthetic with sedation, depending on whether both upper and lower lids are being done. You go home the same day with someone to drive you.

02

The first week

Bruising is the main event and it is worst around days two and three. Cold compresses and sleeping propped up shorten it considerably. Fine sutures come out at around a week.

03

Settling

Most people are comfortable in company at ten days and in photographs at three weeks. The last of the swelling in the lid resolves over two to three months.

Questions

What people ask before they book.

Will the scars show?

On the upper lid the incision sits in the natural crease and disappears into it once healed. On the lower lid it is placed just beneath the lash line, or inside the lid entirely where only fat is being addressed, in which case there is no external scar at all.

How long does it last?

Upper eyelid surgery is generally a once in a lifetime operation for most patients. Lower lids are similarly durable. You continue to age, but the tissue that was removed does not come back.

Will my insurance cover it?

Occasionally, for upper lids only, and only where the excess skin is documented to obstruct the visual field. That requires formal visual field testing and photographs. The practice can tell you whether it is worth pursuing, but it is a question for your insurer and most cases are cosmetic.

Can filler or laser do this instead?

They address different things. Filler can soften a hollow under the eye and laser can improve the texture of thin lid skin, and for early changes either may be enough. Neither removes excess skin or repositions herniated fat, which is what makes a lid look heavy.

What happens at the consultation?

You and Dr. Stone go through your concerns and your goals, she takes a medical history and assesses your general health, and photographs and measurements are taken so the plan is drawn against your own anatomy. You leave knowing whether this is the right procedure for you, and if it is not, which one is.
