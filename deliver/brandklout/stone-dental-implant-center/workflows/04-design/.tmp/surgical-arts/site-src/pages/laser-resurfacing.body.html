Skin

Laser Resurfacing

Texture, fine lines and pigment in one treatment. The only thing on this list that changes the surface of the skin rather than what is underneath it.

Surgery repositions. Injectables fill and relax. Neither does anything about the actual quality of the skin: the fine crepe, the sun damage, the pigment, the texture that makes a face look weathered even when its structure is good.

Resurfacing removes a controlled depth of damaged skin and lets it heal from below with new collagen. It is the treatment that makes a good surgical result look genuinely refreshed rather than merely lifted.

Who it suits

Anyone whose complaint is about the surface rather than the shape. It is also the standard companion to facial surgery, staged rather than done the same day.

  • Fine lines around the mouth and eyes that do not respond to injectables
  • Sun damage, freckling and uneven pigment across the cheeks and forehead
  • Rough or crepey texture, particularly on thin eyelid and perioral skin
  • Acne scarring and enlarged pores

Depth is chosen against your skin and your tolerance for downtime, and it is the single decision that determines both the result and the recovery. There is no version of this that produces a dramatic change with no downtime, and anyone offering one is describing a different treatment.

The two depths

The practice prices these separately because they are genuinely different propositions:

  • Laser resurfacing, full face, $2,800. Texture, fine lines and pigment addressed in one treatment, with a recovery most people can plan around a long weekend and a quiet week.
  • CO2 laser, $2,000. The deeper option. Real downtime and real change, for skin where the damage has gone past what a lighter treatment will reach.

Which one you need is a clinical judgement about your skin, not a budget decision, and the cheaper of the two is the more aggressive.

What it will not do

Resurfacing does not lift. It tightens modestly through new collagen, and that is worth having, but a jowl is a jowl and no laser will reposition it. Where the complaint is descent, the honest answer is surgery, and resurfacing afterwards.

It also has to be respected in darker skin, where the risk of post-inflammatory pigment change is real and the settings and aftercare are different. That assessment happens before anything is booked.

The procedure

What the week actually looks like.

01

The appointment

Numbing cream for around an hour beforehand, then thirty to sixty minutes of treatment depending on the area and the depth. Deeper CO2 treatments are done with sedation.

02

The first week

The skin is red, hot and swollen, then peels. This is the treatment working and it is not optional. Five to ten days of social downtime depending on depth, with a strict regime of occlusive ointment and absolutely no sun.

03

Settling

Pinkness fades over several weeks. Collagen remodelling continues for three to six months, so the skin keeps improving well after it looks healed. Daily sun protection from then on is what protects the result.

Questions

What people ask before they book.

How much downtime, really?

Five to ten days where you would not want to be seen, depending on the depth chosen. Lighter fractional treatments sit at the short end, CO2 at the long end. Anyone who tells you a resurfacing treatment that genuinely changes your skin has no downtime is describing a facial.

How many treatments will I need?

Lighter resurfacing is usually a course of two or three spaced weeks apart. A deeper CO2 treatment is frequently a single event with results that last years. Which route suits you is decided against your skin and your calendar.

Is it safe for darker skin?

It requires more care. Darker skin types carry a real risk of post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation, which is manageable with the right settings, appropriate pre-treatment and disciplined aftercare, but it changes the plan. This is assessed honestly before anything is booked rather than discovered afterwards.

Can I have it at the same time as surgery?

Usually it is staged rather than combined, because both the skin and the surgical result need to heal without competing. The sequence is planned as part of your overall timeline.

What happens at the consultation?

You and Dr. Stone go through your concerns and your goals, she takes a medical history and assesses your general health, and photographs and measurements are taken so the plan is drawn against your own anatomy. You leave knowing whether this is the right procedure for you, and if it is not, which one is.
