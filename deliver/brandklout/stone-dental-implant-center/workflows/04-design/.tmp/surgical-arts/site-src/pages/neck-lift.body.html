01 · What it does

A neck lift is not a skin operation.

Three layers have changed and a result that lasts means addressing all three in the same sitting. The one people expect to hear about is the one nearest the surface, and it is the least of them.

Surface Skin Fat Muscle Deepest

The platysma

The sheet of muscle that gives the neck its shape, repaired where it has separated. This is the layer that holds, and it is the reason a neck lift lasts longer than anything that only tightens skin.

Where the muscle is lax but the skin still has tone, a suture suspension may be enough on its own. That is the MyEllevate® conversation, and it is a cheaper and shorter one.

Submental fat

Contoured where it has accumulated under the chin and along the jaw, so that the border the muscle repair creates has a clean line to sit against rather than a soft one.

Fullness under the chin is sometimes fat rather than muscle, and sometimes both. Which it is on you is measured at the consultation, not guessed at from a photograph.

Excess skin

Removed where it is genuinely in excess, rather than redraped over the problem. Treating the skin as the problem is how a neck ends up looking operated on rather than rested.

Skin is the last layer and the one everybody names first. A procedure that only pulls it tight is why work that has been done looks done.

02 · Who it suits

The test is whether the skin takes up when you lift your chin.

This is the operation when there is real skin excess. If the skin retracts when the chin is raised, a suspension procedure may still be enough. If it hangs, it needs removing.

03 · How it is done

Under the chin, then around and behind the ears.

A small incision under the chin gives access to the platysma, which is repaired in the midline where it has separated. Fat is contoured through the same opening. Incisions around and behind the ears follow the natural creases and are where the skin is redraped and the excess removed.

Scars are placed where the anatomy conceals them and they mature over months from pink to pale. Scar care starts as soon as the wounds are closed and is part of the plan rather than an afterthought.

A drain is used sometimes, for the first day or two, depending on how much work the neck needed. If one is used it comes out at the first post-operative visit.

04 · Recovery

A day, a fortnight, and six months of settling.

The figures below are the practice's conventional ranges and they are what you should plan around. Dr. Stone will give you the numbers for your own neck at the consultation.

  1. The day

    Two to four hours in theatre

    Under general anaesthetic or deep sedation. A responsible adult brings you, waits, and drives you home. You do not drive yourself for this one, and you should not for twenty-four hours afterwards.

  2. Day three

    Bruising peaks, then falls away

    A support garment, swelling and tightness. Day three is the worst of it and it improves quickly after that, which is worth knowing in advance rather than discovering.

  3. Week two

    Sutures out, back at a desk

    Sutures come out in stages across the first two weeks. Most people are back at a desk at two weeks and comfortable in company shortly after. Plan for the fortnight and come back early if it goes well.

  4. Months three to six

    The contour settles

    The result keeps refining as swelling resolves and the tissues soften. Follow-ups are with Dr. Stone, not with a nurse you have not met.

07 · Questions

The six questions this operation raises.

How is this different from MyEllevate®?

MyEllevate® suspends the platysma with a suture and removes nothing, so there is no external incision and the recovery is days rather than weeks. A neck lift removes skin and repairs muscle directly. The first is for laxity, the second for excess, and which you have is an anatomical fact rather than a preference.

Where do the scars go?

A small incision under the chin, and incisions around and behind the ears following the natural creases. They are placed where the anatomy conceals them and they mature over months from pink to pale.

How long does it last?

The practice puts a full surgical neck lift at up to ten years, against about five for MyEllevate®. The muscle repair is durable; what changes is that your neck keeps ageing around it. Skin quality, weight stability and genetics all move that number.

Will I need a drain?

Sometimes, for the first day or two, depending on how much work the neck needed. If one is used it is removed at your first post-operative visit and it is a great deal less unpleasant than it sounds.

What happens at the consultation?

You and Dr. Stone go through your concerns and your goals, she takes a medical history and assesses your general health, and photographs and measurements are taken so the plan is drawn against your own anatomy. You leave knowing whether this is the right procedure for you, and if it is not, which one is.

What does it cost, and can I finance it?

$11,000 for the neck alone, or $18,000 combined with the face. Financing runs through Sunbit, and membership credit can be applied to a surgical fee.
