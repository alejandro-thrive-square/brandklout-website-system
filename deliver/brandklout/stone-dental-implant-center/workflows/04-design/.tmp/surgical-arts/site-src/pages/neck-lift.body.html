The neck is where age shows first and hides least. It is also the practice signature: Dr. Stone is known online as @drjawline, and the neck is the work she is sought out for.

A neck lift addresses all three of the things that go wrong at once. The platysma, the sheet of muscle that gives the neck its shape, is repaired where it has separated. Fat is contoured where it has accumulated. Skin that is genuinely in excess is removed rather than redraped over the problem.

What this treats

Who it suits

This is the operation when there is real skin excess. The test is whether the skin retracts when you lift your chin: if it does, a suspension procedure may still be enough, and if it hangs, it needs removing.

Where the skin still has tone and the problem is muscle laxity, MyEllevate® achieves a great deal at $6,000 with no external incision and a far shorter recovery. Dr. Stone will recommend the smaller operation where it will do the job.

  • 01

  • 02

  • 03

  • 04

    Significant weight loss that has left the neck with more skin than it needs

Often combined with

A neck that has aged this far rarely has a face that has not:

  • Face and neck lift, quoted together at $18,000, where the jowl and the neck are one continuous problem.
  • Jawline implant, where the mandible itself lacks the projection to give the neck an angle to sit against.
  • Submental liposuction, where fullness under the chin is fat as well as muscle.

Which of these you need is decided on your anatomy at the consultation, not assembled into a package beforehand.

Why it matters who operates

The marginal mandibular nerve runs exactly where this operation works, and it is the nerve that moves the lower lip. A surgeon has to know that anatomy in three dimensions, in a particular neck, under tension, rather than as a diagram.

Dr. Stone trained in oral and maxillofacial surgery across three Level 1 trauma centres. She spent her residency rebuilding this anatomy after it had been broken, which is the background she brings to an elective neck.

The procedure

What the day actually looks like.

01

In theatre

Two to four hours, under general anaesthetic or deep sedation. A responsible adult brings you, waits, and drives you home. You do not drive yourself for this one.

02

The first fortnight

A support garment, swelling and tightness, and bruising that peaks around day three. Sutures come out in stages over two weeks. Most people are back at a desk at two weeks and comfortable in company shortly after.

03

Settling

The contour refines for three to six months as swelling resolves and the tissues soften. Follow-ups are with Dr. Stone, not with a nurse you have not met.

Questions

What people ask before they book.

How is this different from MyEllevate?

MyEllevate® suspends the platysma with a suture and removes nothing, so there is no external incision and the recovery is days rather than weeks. A neck lift removes skin and repairs muscle directly. The first is for laxity, the second for excess, and which you have is an anatomical fact rather than a preference.

Where do the scars go?

A small incision under the chin, and incisions around and behind the ears following the natural creases. They are placed where the anatomy conceals them and they mature over months from pink to pale.

How long does it last?

The practice puts a full surgical neck lift at up to ten years, against about five for MyEllevate®. The muscle repair is durable; what changes is that your neck keeps ageing around it. Skin quality, weight stability and genetics all move that number.

Will I need a drain?

Sometimes, for the first day or two, depending on how much work the neck needed. If one is used it is removed at your first post-operative visit and it is a great deal less unpleasant than it sounds.

What happens at the consultation?

You and Dr. Stone go through your concerns and your goals, she takes a medical history and assesses your general health, and photographs and measurements are taken so the plan is drawn against your own anatomy. You leave knowing whether this is the right procedure for you, and if it is not, which one is.
