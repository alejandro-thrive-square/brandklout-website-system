The practice

A surgeon's training. An artist's eye.

Stone Surgical Arts is a boutique facial and cosmetic surgery practice in Westlake, Texas. One surgeon, one address, and a plan that belongs to the person it was drawn for.

Oral & Maxillofacial Surgeon

Dr. Mona Stone, DDS

Meet your surgeon

She came to surgery through engineering, and it shows in the planning.

Before dental school, Dr. Stone took a degree in biomedical engineering. Then a Doctorate of Dental Surgery from the University of Missouri–Kansas City, then a full residency in Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery in Fort Lauderdale, across three Level 1 trauma centres, where the work is rebuilding faces that have been broken.

She is an attending surgeon at Baylor and Methodist, sits on Pinnacle faculty, and is certified in KLS Martin IPS® patient-specific implant planning: the workflow where an implant is designed to a particular skull from a particular scan, rather than fitted from a tray of stock sizes.

What that background produces is a surgeon who reads proportion the way a plastic surgeon does: the line of the jaw, the balance across the mid-face, how a feature will sit in ten years. She plans in three dimensions before touching anything.

Clinical oversight

Who is responsible for what happens to your face.

In aesthetics that question often has no clear answer. Here it has one.

01

A surgeon assesses you

Not a coordinator, not a treatment adviser. The consultation is with Dr. Stone, and so is the decision about what you should and should not have.

02

A surgeon operates

The person who planned your case performs it. There are no rotating providers and no handing a case to whoever is free that week.

03

A surgeon oversees the rest

Injectables and skin work are delivered by the practice's own team, under Dr. Stone's protocols, in her building, with her available.

Training

Where the hours were put in.

  1. Before dental school

    A degree in biomedical engineering, which is why she plans in three dimensions and thinks about load and structure before she thinks about skin.

  2. Doctorate of Dental Surgery

    University of Missouri–Kansas City.

  3. Internship

    A year at Truman Medical Center.

  4. Residency

    Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery at Broward General Medical Center in Fort Lauderdale, a Level 1 trauma centre where the work is rebuilding faces that have been broken rather than refining ones that have not.

  5. Full scope, since

    Maxillofacial pathology, dentoalveolar and implant surgery, maxillofacial reconstruction, microvascular surgery, trauma, and facial cosmetic surgery. Attending surgeon at Baylor Medical Center in Grapevine and Irving, and at Methodist.

Why a maxillofacial surgeon for cosmetic work. Reconstruction after trauma is the discipline of rebuilding a face to its own proportions from the bone outward. Cosmetic surgery asks the same question in a gentler register. It is the reason the implants here are planned to a scan rather than picked from a tray.

Outside the operating room

A public speaker, an activist, and a Formula 1 obsessive.

Dr. Stone cofounded Real Heroes Need Masks, a grassroots organisation that sourced PPE for medical staff through the pandemic, and the campaign is the reason her name turns up in a business-school newsletter as often as a medical one. She is a public speaker and a philanthropist.

She is also the mother of two small children, an avid supporter of motor sport, and the surgeon behind “Dr. Jawline”, a TikTok and Instagram following built on showing the work rather than talking about it.
