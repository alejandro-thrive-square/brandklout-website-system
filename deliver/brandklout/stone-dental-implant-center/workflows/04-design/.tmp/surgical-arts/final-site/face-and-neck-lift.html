A face lift is not about pulling skin tight. Skin is the last layer, and treating it as the problem is how a face ends up looking operated on rather than rested. The work happens underneath, in the layer of muscle and fascia that has descended, and the skin is redraped over the result rather than dragged into position.

Face and neck are quoted and performed together here. Lifting the face and leaving the neck produces a mismatch that most patients notice within a year, and the neck is the harder half of the operation, which is the half this practice is known for.

What this treats

Who it suits

This is the right operation when there is genuine descent and genuine skin excess, rather than early laxity that a suture suspension could still hold. It tends to be patients in their fifties and sixties, and younger patients who have lost a large amount of weight.

If your skin still has good tone and the problem is muscle rather than excess, Dr. Stone will tell you that and point you at MyEllevate® instead. Being talked out of the bigger operation is a normal outcome of a consultation here.

  • 04

Often combined with

The lift restores position. It does not change the quality of the skin on top of it, or reopen an eye that has hooded, so two things are commonly planned alongside it:

  • Blepharoplasty, because a lifted face with tired eyes reads as tired.
  • Laser resurfacing, for the texture and pigment a lift cannot reach. Often staged rather than done the same day.

Both are decided at the consultation, on your anatomy, and neither is sold as a package beforehand.

Why it matters who operates

The facial nerve branches run through the plane this operation works in. Knowing where they are on a diagram is not the same as knowing where they are in a particular face, at a particular depth, under tension. Dr. Stone trained in oral and maxillofacial surgery across three Level 1 trauma centres, rebuilding this anatomy after it had been broken, which is the least forgiving way to learn it.

She plans and performs every case herself. The surgeon who assesses you is the surgeon who operates on you and the surgeon you see at every follow-up.

The procedure

What the day actually looks like.

01

In theatre

Four to six hours under general anaesthetic, depending on what is being combined. You are monitored afterwards and go home the same day with a responsible adult, or stay overnight if that is the safer plan for you.

02

The first fortnight

Swelling and bruising peak around day three and fall away quickly after that. Sutures come out in stages across the first two weeks. Most people are presentable to people who are not looking for it at around two weeks.

03

Settling

The result keeps refining for six to twelve months as swelling resolves and scars mature. Follow-ups are with Dr. Stone, not with a nurse you have not met.

Questions

What people ask before they book.

Where do the scars go, and will people see them?

They follow the natural creases in front of the ear, around the earlobe and back into the hairline behind it. They are placed where the anatomy hides them and they mature over months from pink to pale. Scar care starts as soon as the wounds are closed and is part of your plan, not an afterthought.

How long will it last?

A well-executed lift resets the clock rather than stopping it. Most patients look substantially better than their unoperated baseline for a decade or more, and you continue to age normally from the new starting point. Skin quality, weight stability, sun exposure and genetics all move that figure, and Dr. Stone will give you a realistic number for your face rather than an average.

Will I look like a different person?

No, and if a result looks like a different person then something has gone wrong with the planning. The aim is that people register that you look well rather than that you look altered. That is a matter of how much tension is placed on which layer, and it is decided in the operating theatre rather than promised beforehand.

Can I have just the neck?

Yes. The neck lift is quoted separately at $11,000, and for the right anatomy MyEllevate® at $6,000 achieves a great deal with no external incision. Which of the three you need is the substance of the consultation.

What happens at the consultation?

You and Dr. Stone go through your concerns and your goals, she takes a medical history and assesses your general health, and photographs and measurements are taken so the plan is drawn against your own anatomy. You leave knowing whether this is the right procedure for you, and if it is not, which one is.
