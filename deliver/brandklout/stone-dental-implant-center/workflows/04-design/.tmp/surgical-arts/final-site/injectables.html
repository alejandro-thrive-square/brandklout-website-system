Non-Surgical

Injectables

Neuromodulators and filler, placed by the practice nurse injector, with Dr. Stone available for anything structural.

Injectables do two entirely different jobs that get spoken about as one. Neuromodulators relax the muscles that fold the skin. Fillers replace volume that has been lost or was never there. Confusing the two is how people end up with a face that is smooth and heavy at the same time.

Both are placed here by Brooke, the practice nurse injector. Anything structural, or any case that needs a surgical eye, is seen by Dr. Stone.

Neuromodulators

Botox, Jeuveau and Dysport all relax muscle. They are dosed and priced per unit, and the units are not interchangeable between brands, which is why comparing the unit price between them is meaningless.

  • Botox® and Jeuveau®, $12 per unit
  • Dysport®, $6 per unit, dosed differently, so compare the treatment rather than the unit
  • With Dr. Stone, for structural or corrective cases, quoted on assessment

They work on lines produced by movement. A line that is present when your face is completely still is an etched line, and it needs resurfacing or filler rather than a neuromodulator.

Fillers

Filler replaces volume. Where it is placed matters far more than how much of it is used, and the common failure of this treatment is chasing a hollow with more product until the face is heavy.

  • Dermal filler, $800 per syringe. The eight point lift, up to eight syringes, is $6,000.
  • Sculptra®, $900 per session, or $2,500 for three and $3,400 for four. A collagen stimulator rather than a filler: it builds volume gradually over months rather than placing it on the day.

Where the deficiency is skeletal rather than soft tissue, filler is the wrong tool and will keep being the wrong tool however many syringes are used. That is what implants are for, and Dr. Stone will tell you if that is what you are actually looking at.

Where injectables stop

This is a surgical practice, and the most useful thing it can tell you about injectables is when they have run out of road. Filler cannot lift a jowl, cannot shorten a long upper lip, and cannot build a jawline that the mandible does not support.

If the honest answer to your concern is a lip lift, a neck suspension or an implant, you will be told that here rather than sold another syringe.

The procedure

What the appointment actually looks like.

01

The assessment

Your face is looked at in movement and at rest before anything is drawn up, because that is what determines whether the answer is a neuromodulator, a filler, or neither.

02

The appointment

Fifteen to forty-five minutes depending on what is being treated. Topical numbing or a dental block for lips. You can drive yourself home and go back to work.

03

Settling

Neuromodulators begin working at three to five days and are fully in at two weeks, which is when any adjustment is made. Filler settles over two weeks. Sculptra builds over several months.

Questions

What people ask before they book.

Who actually injects me?

Brooke, the practice nurse injector, for routine treatment. Dr. Stone takes structural cases, corrective work and anything where a surgical assessment changes the plan. You are told which before you book, not on the day.

Why is Dysport cheaper per unit?

Because a Dysport unit is not equivalent to a Botox unit and the dosing is different. Comparing per unit prices between brands tells you nothing useful. Ask what the treatment costs for your face, which is the number that matters.

Can filler be dissolved if I do not like it?

Hyaluronic acid fillers can be dissolved with an enzyme, which is one of their genuine advantages. Sculptra cannot: it stimulates your own collagen over months and is not reversible, which is why it is dosed conservatively across sessions.

How often will I need it repeated?

Neuromodulators every three to four months. Hyaluronic acid filler generally lasts six to eighteen months depending on the product and the area. Sculptra is built over a course and then maintained annually.

What happens at the consultation?

You and Dr. Stone go through your concerns and your goals, she takes a medical history and assesses your general health, and photographs and measurements are taken so the plan is drawn against your own anatomy. You leave knowing whether this is the right procedure for you, and if it is not, which one is.

Is there a membership that makes this cheaper?

Yes. The practice runs a membership that banks credit toward treatment, including injectables, and unspent credit can be applied to a surgical fee. See Memberships.
