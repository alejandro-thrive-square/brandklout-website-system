What it is

Botox and Xeomin are both botulinum toxin type A: a purified protein that temporarily stops a specific muscle from contracting. Injected in very small units into the muscles of the upper face, they stop those muscles from folding the skin above them, and the lines that folding creates soften and, over repeated treatments, stop deepening.

They do not fill anything, tighten anything or remove anything. If a line is visible when your face is completely still, it is etched into the skin and a neuromodulator alone will improve it slowly if at all. That distinction is the single most common misunderstanding about this treatment, and it is the first thing Dr. Stone will check at your consultation.

What it does for you

Most people come in describing how they look rather than what they want done: tired, cross, older than they feel. In the upper face those three impressions are usually created by three specific muscles, and this is the treatment that addresses them.

  • The horizontal lines across your forehead soften, without dropping your brow.
  • The vertical crease between your eyebrows stops making you look annoyed.
  • The lines fanning from the outer corners of your eyes ease when you smile.
  • A heavy brow can be lifted a millimetre or two by relaxing what pulls it down.

Am I a candidate?

Most healthy adults are. The consultation exists to find the people who are not, and to work out how much you actually need, which for a first treatment is almost always less than you have in mind.

This is not for you if

You are pregnant or breastfeeding; you have a neuromuscular condition such as myasthenia gravis; you have an active infection at the injection site; or your lines are static — visible with your face at rest — in which case Dr. Stone will talk to you about resurfacing or filler instead, or about nothing at all.

Four products, and which one you get

All four are botulinum toxin type A and all four do the same thing. They differ in how quickly they start, how far they spread from the injection point, and how they behave in patients who have been treated for years. Dr. Stone keeps all four so the choice is clinical rather than whatever the practice stocks.

Why have it done here

Because the person injecting you operates on the facial skeleton for a living. An oral and maxillofacial surgeon has spent years working underneath the muscles she is now treating from above, and that changes two things: where the needle goes, and what she tells you when the honest answer is that this treatment will not fix what is bothering you.

It also means the conversation about what happens in ten years is a real one. Every appointment here is with Dr. Stone. There is no injector who sees you while she sees someone else.

Before and after your appointment

The week before

  1. Stop fish oil, vitamin E and high-dose NSAIDs for about a week if your doctor agrees — they thin the blood and cause bruising.
  2. Skip alcohol for 24 hours beforehand, for the same reason.
  3. Come with a clean face if you can. Makeup is removed before we start.
  4. Tell us about any recent dental work, vaccination or illness.

The 24 hours after

  1. Stay upright for four hours. No lying flat, no massage tables, no face-down naps.
  2. Do not rub, press or massage the treated areas. Product moves.
  3. No strenuous exercise, saunas or hot yoga for 24 hours.
  4. Small red bumps settle within an hour; a bruise, if you get one, takes a few days.
  5. Judge the result at two weeks, not at three days. We book your review then.

Questions

The ones we are asked most.

Does it hurt?

The needle is very fine and each injection takes about a second. Most patients describe a brief sting. No anaesthetic is needed for a standard upper-face treatment, and you can drive yourself home.

How soon will I see it?

Movement starts to soften at three to seven days and reaches full effect at about fourteen. Do not judge the result before two weeks — we book your review appointment at that point for exactly this reason.

Will I look frozen?

That is a dosing and placement decision, not a property of the drug. Dr. Stone treats the specific muscles that are folding your skin and leaves the rest working. A first treatment here is deliberately conservative — it is easy to add at your two-week review and impossible to remove.

What happens when it wears off?

Your muscles go back to doing what they did before, and so do your lines. Nothing gets worse than it was — the idea that stopping makes you look older than if you had never started is a myth. What is true is that people notice the return, because they have got used to the softer version.

Can I have it with something else the same day?

Often, yes. Botox and filler are routinely done in one visit. Combining it with a resurfacing treatment depends on the depth and the order, and Dr. Stone will sequence it at your consultation rather than fitting it around your calendar.

How many units will I need?

It depends on your muscle mass, which is why nobody can answer it over the phone. Men usually need more than women; a strong glabella needs more than a forehead. You are quoted your number after the assessment, before anything is injected.
