Questions

What people ask about paying.

Will my insurance cover any of this?

No. Cosmetic treatment is not a covered medical expense, and none of the seven treatments on our menu are billed to insurance. If a condition you have is genuinely medical rather than cosmetic, Dr. Stone will tell you and refer you appropriately — that is a different conversation and a different practice.

Can I use an HSA or FSA?

Generally not for cosmetic treatment. HSA and FSA funds are for qualified medical expenses and aesthetic injectables do not usually qualify. Your plan administrator is the authority on your specific account, not us.

Why are there no prices on this website?

Because a number without an assessment is misleading. Botox is priced per unit and the number of units depends on your muscle mass; filler is priced per syringe and how many you need depends on what has actually gone. You are quoted your figure at the consultation, before anything is done, and it does not change afterwards.

Is the consultation charged?

Ask when you book — this is one of the details the practice sets and it is not confirmed on this page. What is fixed is that you are told before you arrive, not at the desk on the way out.

Does the membership save money?

It is designed to, for someone who is already coming three or four times a year. If you have Botox once and are not sure you will repeat it, pay per visit — a membership that you do not use is not a saving. The two tiers are here.
