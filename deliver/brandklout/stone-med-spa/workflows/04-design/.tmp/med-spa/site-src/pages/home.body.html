Why here

Anatomy is the whole job.

A syringe is easy to hold. Knowing what is under the skin at that exact point, and what happens over the next ten years if you keep putting product there, is a different qualification entirely.

01

She trained on the skeleton first

An oral and maxillofacial surgeon spends years operating on the facial bones and the soft tissue over them. Filler placed by someone who has seen that anatomy from the inside goes on the bone that has flattened, not into the cheek that looks hollow.

02

Nothing is delegated

Every injection in this practice is placed by Dr. Stone. That is unusual, it limits how many patients we can take in a week, and it is the reason to come here rather than somewhere with more appointment slots.

03

She will tell you when the answer is surgery

There is a point past which filler stops helping and starts adding weight to a face that needs lifting. A practice that only sells injectables has no reason to say so. This one has a surgical practice next door and says it.
