# The homepage, rebuilt as a journey

**2026-09-01.** The team reviewed the 2026-08-31 staging build and called the design ugly and
simple. They were right, and the reason is structural rather than cosmetic:
[`DESIGN-STANDARD.md`](../../../docs/DESIGN-STANDARD.md) names the exact failure mode and the
2026-08-28 homepage was a textbook case of it.

**North star: [`drnahlah.com`](https://drnahlah.com), the build at
`bk-dr-nahlah/deliver/brandklout/dr-nahlah/workflows/04-design/.tmp/site-src/`.** Not to copy. To
understand. What makes that site work is four fundamentals, and none of them is a colour.

## What was wrong, measured

| | Old homepage (2026-08-28) | Reference (Dr. Nahlah) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Sections | 13 interchangeable bands | 10 named chapters in a fixed order |
| `data-chapter` | 0 | 9 |
| GSAP | **none at all** | 44 tweens, 19 ScrollTriggers |
| Scrubbed scenes | 0 | 12 |
| Pinned scenes | 0 | 2 |
| `IntersectionObserver` | 7 | **0, deliberately** |
| Largest type ratio | 2.5 : 1 | ~9 : 1 |
| Services | 14 identical bordered tiles, one grid | three scenes, each a different shape |
| Reviews | 6 equal boxes of 90-word text | one display quote plus a rail |
| Empty image slots | services, reviews, retention, FAQ | none |

The tell is in the class names. `section_hero`, `bg="paper"`, `bg="slate"`, `bg="ink"` all describe
**a strip with a background colour**. Swap any two and nothing breaks, because nothing was ever
connected. Scroll was pure transport: the page would read identically as a printed PDF.

## The four fundamentals taken from the reference

1. **Chapters, not bands.** A section is a *scene* with a name that says what it does to the
   reader. It arrives, it does something while it is on screen, and it departs.
2. **Scroll does work.** Not "an observer fires a class at 20% visibility". Scroll *position* is the
   interaction, so the reader can move through a moment at their own speed and back out of it.
3. **Type carries the hierarchy.** A 6.4rem display face against a 0.75rem tracked label. The old
   page set nearly everything at 17px, so nothing was louder than anything else.
4. **Atmosphere shifts.** Light, white, warm, ink, warm, ink. The page changes rooms.

**What was NOT taken:** the palette, the fonts, the specific sections, the prosthodontist framing.
Dr. Nahlah's story is "the specialist who fixes what others gave up on". Mason Park's is different
and the chapters had to be written for it.

## The spine: what Mason Park's story actually is

A general practice for adults and a **board certified pediatric practice** share one building on
Kingsland Boulevard. Dr. Nguyen's board certification is a three year specialty beyond dental
school, and having it *in the building* is the thing that collapses two trips into one. Every
chapter serves that one argument.

| # | Chapter | Job | Shape | Scroll |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 00 | **Arrival** | The promise, in one sentence | Full bleed, dark, the practice's own team photograph | split-line reveal, departure scrub |
| 01 | **The Relay** | Name the pain: two practices, two drives | Statement beside a sticky photograph | **scrubbed word by word** |
| 02 | **Under One Roof** | The answer, argued | Figures, an unequal pair, a comparison table, a marquee | counters, velocity-reactive marquee |
| 03 | **The Range** | 35 treatments, findable | Three alternating arcs, each a photograph plus a hairline index | staggered index, drift |
| 04 | **The People** | Continuity, the reason families stay | Tall grayscale plates on ink, then a full bleed team band | clip-path reveal |
| 05 | **The Path** | What actually happens, and when you learn the price | Horizontal track | **pinned and scrubbed** |
| 06 | **The Proof** | Real reviews, readable | One display quote plus a portrait, then a rail | **scrubbed quote, scrubbed rail** |
| 07 | **The Invitation** | Book, call, where, when, and the four objections | Split, ink, with the FAQ folded in | staggered entrance |

Financing is inside **step three of The Path**, where the objection actually lands, not in its own
band four screens further down. The FAQ is inside **The Invitation**, where a visitor is deciding.
That is the standard's rule and it is the right one.

## The rules the client set, and how they are honoured

### "Extract colour and font from the handoff. Elevate the rest."

Kept verbatim from the Figma handoff: the ten colour tokens (`#8C6349` brand, `#B78668` copper,
`#E2D5C5` beige, `#2E3538` ink, `#545F62` body, `#5D7A6B` sage, the three grounds) and the three
families (Newsreader / Nunito Sans / Karla). **Nothing else.** Three tokens are derived, all
documented in `theme/journey.css`: `--ink-deep` `#1E2426`, `--ink-mid` `#262D30`, `--paper`
`#F7F3EE`, plus `#D9B79C`, which is the brand hue taken light enough to clear AA on ink.

### "No em dashes in the copy."

**0 in the built homepage.** Verified with `grep -c "—" dist/index.html`. The eight in
`site/home.mjs` are all in code comments. Dr. Sung's credential line and the retention line, which
both carried them, are rewritten as clauses.

> **Still open:** 37 other pages carry 261 em dashes. Roughly 100 are in copy we authored and can
> be fixed; the rest are inside the client's own published sentences, which this build takes
> verbatim on purpose. Rewriting those is a client decision, not a find and replace.

### "No empty image slots."

Every image in the eight chapters resolves to a real file in the practice's own media library.
The **service imagery is read out of each service page's own `featured.src`**, so the pairing
between a treatment and its photograph is the client's, not ours, and a new service page brings its
own picture with it. `featured()` in `site/home.mjs` does the lookup and takes a fallback.

The invitation's map used to be the exception: a bare `<iframe>` is a blank white rectangle on an
ink ground for as long as it takes to arrive, and permanently blank wherever it is blocked. It now
sits **under** a photograph of the waiting room, which is the content; the map is wayfinding.

### The stone texture

The old site had a plaster texture behind its editorial bands and it is worth carrying forward. It
is **drawn, not downloaded**: two octaves of `feTurbulence` in an inline SVG data URI on `--stone`,
under 400 bytes, multiplied over the warm and ink grounds at 4.5% and 7%. Resolution independent,
no request, cannot 404, and no Magnific call. Widen the `baseFrequency` to coarsen it.

## The motion layer

`theme/journey.js`, shipped as a site-wide code asset beside `site.js`. It **runs only on a page
carrying `section[data-chapter]`**, so the other 73 pages pay one script tag and exit at line 27.
GSAP is **vendored, not CDN** (`vendor/gsap/`, 125KB, all plugins free since 3.13): a dental site
should not hand a third party the ability to run script on its pages.

`layout({ motion })` in `site/presentation.mjs` adds the four script tags and the `has-journey` body
class. `build.mjs` sets it from `body.includes("data-chapter=")` rather than a list of slugs, so a
page gains the motion layer the moment it is authored as chapters. Nothing to keep in sync.

**No ScrollSmoother.** It is in the reference and it is deliberately not here: it breaks full page
captures, and it flips ScrollTrigger's pin strategy from native `position:fixed` to a per-frame
`translateY` write, which is what the Dr. Nahlah client reported as flicker on a phone. Native
scroll, native pins.

### The chrome, and why it is not GSAP

The header floats over the arrival chapter and resolves to solid past it. That is a **layout**
decision, not a motion one, so `chrome()` is the first block in the file, runs before the reduced
motion and GSAP guards, and uses a plain scroll listener. Everything after it may not run; that
always does. `--jr-nav-h` is measured, never assumed, because it changes with the logo's intrinsic
size and the breakpoint, and the hero is pulled up by exactly that amount.

## Verification

`node verify-journey.mjs` drives the page over CDP and asserts behaviour. **21 checks, 0 failed.**

The one that matters is check 3: step the scroll to 10 / 50 / 90% of the trigger's **own** start and
end, and assert a different intermediate state at each. Chapter 01 reads **2 / 35, then 12 / 35,
then 23 / 35 words written**. A scene that snaps between two states is a triggered scene wearing a
costume, and this is the only check that catches it.

```
node build.mjs
node audit.mjs               # 0 blockers, 0 errors, 47 warnings (the pre-existing set)
node responsive.mjs          # 74 pages x 3 widths, 0 problems
node verify-journey.mjs      # 21 checks, 0 failed
node shot.mjs . --still      # LAYOUT captures. See below.
```

### `shot.mjs --still` exists because captures lie about motion

The capture pass scrolls the whole document to wake lazy images, which leaves every scrubbed
timeline wherever its scrub lag settled. The first capture of this build photographed the hero at
15% opacity, because its departure scrub had already run. `--still` emulates
`prefers-reduced-motion`, which puts the page in the state the guard defines: the finished layout
with no motion. **Use it for layout and responsive review. Never use a capture to judge motion.**

## Four bugs worth not rediscovering

1. **`.ch .wrap { margin: 0 auto }` ate every vertical gap on the page.** That rule is specificity
   (0,2,0) and the shorthand resets `margin-block` to 0, so every element carrying both `.wrap` and
   a block class lost its own `margin-top` / `margin-bottom` to it. The pull quote sat on top of its
   heading and the FAQ sat on top of the opening hours. Only the horizontal centring belongs in a
   shared rule: `margin-left: auto; margin-right: auto`.

2. **`.ch p { color: var(--fg-body) }` beat `.re-big { color: … }`,** (0,1,1) against (0,1,0), so the
   scrubbed statement rendered at full body colour and there was nothing left for the scrub to
   write. `verify-journey.mjs` caught it; three screenshots had not. A tween's *starting* state has
   to outrank the element rule underneath it.

3. **The counters shipped a literal `0` for the tween to replace.** With reduced motion on, or with
   JS failing, the page read "0 Doctors on staff". The final value is now in the HTML and the tween
   counts **up** to it. **A number a page states must be true before any script runs.**

4. **The reduced-motion guard was resetting `filter` on the doctor portraits and the index
   thumbnails.** Grayscale there is a design treatment, not motion, and stripping it made those
   images colour *only* for reduced-motion visitors. Only reset what a scroll class would otherwise
   have removed.

## Still open

1. **Apply the system to the other pages.** This is the canary, approved first, per the standard's
   Brand Authority and Canary Gate. The ten other core pages are next, then the 35 service pages.
2. **Nothing has shipped.** Local `dist/` only. Staging is
   `RAILWAY_ENV_NAME=staging`, run order in [`CORE-PAGES-2026-08-31.md`](./CORE-PAGES-2026-08-31.md).
   `import-styles.mjs` **must** run again: `site.css` grew a whole layer and the registered class
   list is a different set. `journey.js` and `vendor/gsap/*` are **new code assets** that have never
   been registered on Instatic, and the four new `<script>` tags are in the document head/body,
   which Instatic owns. **That is unsolved and is the first thing to work out before shipping.**
3. **The fluid root is not installed.** The standard asks for `rem` type off `scripts/fluid-root.mjs`
   and no `clamp()` font sizes. The journey layer uses the reference build's other approved
   mechanism, unitless multipliers stepped at px breakpoints, which is correct on its own but the
   rest of `site.css` still uses `clamp()`. Installing the root site-wide would rescale all 74
   pages, so it needs its own pass.
4. **Em dashes on 37 other pages** (see above).
5. Everything the 08-28 and 08-31 lists still carry: office hours, "Since 1997", a publishable
   rating, whether Dr. Sung stays, and the client's photo album (`photos.app.goo.gl/k9MzHrudUfh4QhpW9`),
   which still nothing uses.
