# The homepage, rebuilt as a journey

**2026-09-01.** The team reviewed the 2026-08-31 staging build and called the design ugly and
simple. They were right, and the reason is structural rather than cosmetic:
[`DESIGN-STANDARD.md`](../../../docs/DESIGN-STANDARD.md) names the exact failure mode and the
2026-08-28 homepage was a textbook case of it.

**North star: [`drnahlah.com`](https://drnahlah.com), the build at
`bk-dr-nahlah/deliver/brandklout/dr-nahlah/workflows/04-design/.tmp/site-src/`.** Not to copy. To
understand. What makes that site work is four fundamentals, and none of them is a colour.

## What was wrong, measured

| | Old homepage (2026-08-28) | Reference (Dr. Nahlah) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Sections | 13 interchangeable bands | 10 named chapters in a fixed order |
| `data-chapter` | 0 | 9 |
| GSAP | **none at all** | 44 tweens, 19 ScrollTriggers |
| Scrubbed scenes | 0 | 12 |
| Pinned scenes | 0 | 2 |
| `IntersectionObserver` | 7 | **0, deliberately** |
| Largest type ratio | 2.5 : 1 | ~9 : 1 |
| Services | 14 identical bordered tiles, one grid | three scenes, each a different shape |
| Reviews | 6 equal boxes of 90-word text | one display quote plus a rail |
| Empty image slots | services, reviews, retention, FAQ | none |

The tell is in the class names. `section_hero`, `bg="paper"`, `bg="slate"`, `bg="ink"` all describe
**a strip with a background colour**. Swap any two and nothing breaks, because nothing was ever
connected. Scroll was pure transport: the page would read identically as a printed PDF.

## The four fundamentals taken from the reference

1. **Chapters, not bands.** A section is a *scene* with a name that says what it does to the
   reader. It arrives, it does something while it is on screen, and it departs.
2. **Scroll does work.** Not "an observer fires a class at 20% visibility". Scroll *position* is the
   interaction, so the reader can move through a moment at their own speed and back out of it.
3. **Type carries the hierarchy.** A 6.4rem display face against a 0.75rem tracked label. The old
   page set nearly everything at 17px, so nothing was louder than anything else.
4. **Atmosphere shifts.** Light, white, warm, ink, warm, ink. The page changes rooms.

**What was NOT taken:** the palette, the fonts, the specific sections, the prosthodontist framing.
Dr. Nahlah's story is "the specialist who fixes what others gave up on". Mason Park's is different
and the chapters had to be written for it.

## The spine: what Mason Park's story actually is

A general practice for adults and a **board certified pediatric practice** share one building on
Kingsland Boulevard. Dr. Nguyen's board certification is a three year specialty beyond dental
school, and having it *in the building* is the thing that collapses two trips into one. Every
chapter serves that one argument.

Financing is inside **step three of The Path**, where the objection actually lands, not in its own
band four screens further down. The FAQ is inside **The Invitation**, where a visitor is deciding.
That is the standard's rule and it is the right one.

## The rules the client set, and how they are honoured

### "Extract colour and font from the handoff. Elevate the rest."

Kept verbatim from the Figma handoff: the ten colour tokens (`#8C6349` brand, `#B78668` copper,
`#E2D5C5` beige, `#2E3538` ink, `#545F62` body, `#5D7A6B` sage, the three grounds) and the three
families (Newsreader / Nunito Sans / Karla). **Nothing else.** Three tokens are derived, all
documented in `theme/journey.css`: `--ink-deep` `#1E2426`, `--ink-mid` `#262D30`, `--paper`
`#F7F3EE`, plus `#D9B79C`, which is the brand hue taken light enough to clear AA on ink.

### "No em dashes in the copy."

**0 in the built homepage.** Verified with `grep -c "—" dist/index.html`. The eight in
`site/home.mjs` are all in code comments. Dr. Sung's credential line and the retention line, which
both carried them, are rewritten as clauses.

> **Still open:** 37 other pages carry 261 em dashes. Roughly 100 are in copy we authored and can
> be fixed; the rest are inside the client's own published sentences, which this build takes
> verbatim on purpose. Rewriting those is a client decision, not a find and replace.

### "No empty image slots."

Every image in the eight chapters resolves to a real file in the practice's own media library.
The **service imagery is read out of each service page's own `featured.src`**, so the pairing
between a treatment and its photograph is the client's, not ours, and a new service page brings its
own picture with it. `featured()` in `site/home.mjs` does the lookup and takes a fallback.

The invitation's map used to be the exception: a bare `<iframe>` is a blank white rectangle on an
ink ground for as long as it takes to arrive, and permanently blank wherever it is blocked. It now
sits **under** a photograph of the waiting room, which is the content; the map is wayfinding.

### The stone texture

The old site had a plaster texture behind its editorial bands and it is worth carrying forward. It
is **drawn, not downloaded**: two octaves of `feTurbulence` in an inline SVG data URI on `--stone`,
under 400 bytes, multiplied over the warm and ink grounds at 4.5% and 7%. Resolution independent,
no request, cannot 404, and no Magnific call. Widen the `baseFrequency` to coarsen it.

## The motion layer

`theme/journey.js`, shipped as a site-wide code asset beside `site.js`. It **runs only on a page
carrying `section[data-chapter]`**, so the other 73 pages pay one script tag and exit at line 27.
GSAP is **vendored, not CDN** (`vendor/gsap/`, 125KB, all plugins free since 3.13): a dental site
should not hand a third party the ability to run script on its pages.

`layout({ motion })` in `site/presentation.mjs` adds the four script tags and the `has-journey` body
class. `build.mjs` sets it from `body.includes("data-chapter=")` rather than a list of slugs, so a
page gains the motion layer the moment it is authored as chapters. Nothing to keep in sync.

**No ScrollSmoother.** It is in the reference and it is deliberately not here: it breaks full page
captures, and it flips ScrollTrigger's pin strategy from native `position:fixed` to a per-frame
`translateY` write, which is what the Dr. Nahlah client reported as flicker on a phone. Native
scroll, native pins.

### The chrome, and why it is not GSAP

The header floats over the arrival chapter and resolves to solid past it. That is a **layout**
decision, not a motion one, so `chrome()` is the first block in the file, runs before the reduced
motion and GSAP guards, and uses a plain scroll listener. Everything after it may not run; that
always does. `--jr-nav-h` is measured, never assumed, because it changes with the logo's intrinsic
size and the breakpoint, and the hero is pulled up by exactly that amount.

## Verification

`node verify-journey.mjs` drives the page over CDP and asserts behaviour. **21 checks, 0 failed.**

The one that matters is check 3: step the scroll to 10 / 50 / 90% of the trigger's **own** start and
end, and assert a different intermediate state at each. Chapter 01 reads **2 / 35, then 12 / 35,
then 23 / 35 words written**. A scene that snaps between two states is a triggered scene wearing a
costume, and this is the only check that catches it.

```
node build.mjs
node audit.mjs               # 0 blockers, 0 errors, 47 warnings (the pre-existing set)
node responsive.mjs          # 74 pages x 3 widths, 0 problems
node verify-journey.mjs      # 21 checks, 0 failed
node shot.mjs . --still      # LAYOUT captures. See below.
```

### `shot.mjs --still` exists because captures lie about motion

The capture pass scrolls the whole document to wake lazy images, which leaves every scrubbed
timeline wherever its scrub lag settled. The first capture of this build photographed the hero at
15% opacity, because its departure scrub had already run. `--still` emulates
`prefers-reduced-motion`, which puts the page in the state the guard defines: the finished layout
with no motion. **Use it for layout and responsive review. Never use a capture to judge motion.**

### `shot.mjs --viewport` exists because captures also lie about sticky elements

The header floats over the hero and is `position: sticky`. In a `captureBeyondViewport` capture it
paints at a translated offset and leaves a band of the page background under it: at 1920 that read
as a **74px white gap between the utility bar and the hero photograph**, exactly like a broken
negative margin. Measuring it said `heroRect.y === navRect.y === 38`, which is no gap at all. The
same capture at a 0.5 scale also invented a solid white rectangle over the nav menu that no element
on the page was painting.

`--viewport` scrolls one viewport at a time and takes ordinary screenshots, which is the only way
to photograph a sticky header truthfully. It is for checking the chrome, not for reading a page:
the header repeats in every slice by definition.

**Two artifacts, one lesson. When a capture shows something structurally impossible, measure the
element before you fix it.**

## Four bugs worth not rediscovering

1. **`.ch .wrap { margin: 0 auto }` ate every vertical gap on the page.** That rule is specificity
   (0,2,0) and the shorthand resets `margin-block` to 0, so every element carrying both `.wrap` and
   a block class lost its own `margin-top` / `margin-bottom` to it. The pull quote sat on top of its
   heading and the FAQ sat on top of the opening hours. Only the horizontal centring belongs in a
   shared rule: `margin-left: auto; margin-right: auto`.

2. **`.ch p { color: var(--fg-body) }` beat `.re-big { color: … }`,** (0,1,1) against (0,1,0), so the
   scrubbed statement rendered at full body colour and there was nothing left for the scrub to
   write. `verify-journey.mjs` caught it; three screenshots had not. A tween's *starting* state has
   to outrank the element rule underneath it.

3. **The counters shipped a literal `0` for the tween to replace.** With reduced motion on, or with
   JS failing, the page read "0 Doctors on staff". The final value is now in the HTML and the tween
   counts **up** to it. **A number a page states must be true before any script runs.**

4. **The reduced-motion guard was resetting `filter` on the doctor portraits and the index
   thumbnails.** Grayscale there is a design treatment, not motion, and stripping it made those
   images colour *only* for reduced-motion visitors. Only reset what a scroll class would otherwise
   have removed.

## 2026-09-01, second pass: the fluid root, and Dr. Nahlah's proportions

The review note was: *responsiveness, the container seems too small, the animation should work when
they are in the viewport, this should have fluid responsive sizes, let's have the proportions we
have on the Nahlah site.* All four are the same root cause. The design was sized against a fixed
16px root and capped at 92rem, so on a large monitor it was a 1472px design stranded in the middle
of the screen, and on a small phone nothing shrank.

### The house fluid root is now installed, site wide

`theme/fluid-root.css`, emitted by `scripts/fluid-root.mjs`, concatenated **first**, before any
reset. `html { font-size }` now interpolates with viewport width: 12px at 320, flat 16px from 479
to 1440, 18px at 1920, 20px at 2400. Every `rem` in both stylesheets became viewport-relative for
free, which is what answers all four notes at once.

**The container.** `--maxw: 97.5rem` is 1560px at the flat root, which is Dr. Nahlah's `--maxw`
exactly. Because it is `rem`, it resolves to **1755px at 1920** and 1950px at 2400 instead of
staying at 1472px. The gutter is `--bleed: 4.5rem`, Dr. Nahlah's `--gutter` ceiling.

**The type scale is Dr. Nahlah's, measured out of `01-tokens.css` rather than re-invented:** `--d1`
6.66rem, `--d2` 4.68rem, `--h2` 3.24rem, `--h3` 1.71rem, body-lg 1.125rem, body .975rem, and the
same five multiplier groups (`--t-disp / -lg / -mid / -sm / -text`) stepped at Webflow's own
breakpoints, 1279 / 991 / 767 / 478. Labels are floored at .75rem, which is this build's own rule
and a deliberate departure from the reference's .702rem.

**479 to 1440 renders pixel-identical to the approved design**, because the root is flat across that
whole range. Only the two ends move, which is the entire point of the scale.

### What had to be fixed to make that safe

- **Seven `clamp()` font-sizes in `site.css`** were replaced. A `clamp()` with a live `vw` term
  scales a second time against a fluid root. Each was replaced by a plain `rem` times a multiplier
  (`--t-head`, `--t-sub`) fitted at the **midpoint of every band**, not just at 1440: preserving
  1440 alone is the trap the directive warns about, because the `vw` term was doing real work
  across the mid-range.
- **Twenty-eight small `rem` labels** in `site.css` were at .75 to .82rem. At 360 the root is 13.0px,
  so they would have rendered at 9.8px. They now carry `--t-ui`, raised to 1.24 below 479, which
  holds 12.1px at 360.
- **Body copy was `17px`,** a px font-size, which opts the whole document out of the mechanism. Now
  `calc(1.0625rem * var(--t-text))`, which is 17px at the flat root and 17.4px at 390.
- **The font `@import` moved to a `<link>` in the head.** An `@import` is only valid before any
  other rule in a sheet, and the fluid root now holds that position.

### Four bugs this pass, and the checks that now catch each

1. **`.ch .wrap`'s sibling: an unbalanced brace.** Splicing the new token block into `:root` dropped
   its closing brace. CSS error recovery then swallowed all four type tiers as if they were
   declarations, so **`--t-disp` stayed at 1 at every width**: the hero headline rendered at its
   1440 size on a 360 phone and ran off the screen. `audit.mjs` checks classes, `responsive.mjs`
   checks rendered text size, and **neither can see a stylesheet that parsed into the wrong shape.**
   `build.mjs` now counts braces and fails the build, and `verify-journey.mjs` asserts the tier
   reaches the glyph at 360 / 768 / 1440.

2. **Two files owned the same tier.** `journey.css` re-declared `--t-text: 1` in its own `:root`,
   later in the concatenated sheet at equal specificity, so it beat `site.css`'s phone tier at every
   width and disabled it across the whole site. **One tier, one owner.**

3. **The pinned chapter stopped fitting the viewport.** At 1920 the root is 18px, everything in the
   scene grew 12.5%, and it measured **1239px inside a 1000px window** — pinned, that puts the
   progress bar and the bottom of every card permanently off screen. That is precisely what "the
   animation should work when they are in the viewport" describes. A scene that is `100svh` tall
   always reports `scrollHeight === clientHeight`, so the obvious check proves nothing; the new
   pin-fit check measures the contents against the box. The scene is now sized in `svh`, so it is
   bound to the window rather than to the type scale.

4. **`fluid-root.mjs --check` reported 0/75 pages carrying a root they all carried.** The gate
   resolved a `<link href="/assets/site.css">` against the page's own directory, which for a
   root-absolute href yields the filesystem root. Every site we deploy links its stylesheet that
   way, so **the gate was failing open for every client**. Fixed in the shared script: an absolute
   href now resolves against the document root, meaning whichever `--check` target contains the file.

### The animations were fine

Worth recording, because the fix was not where the note pointed. A new check parks every chapter in
the middle of the viewport and asserts that each element the timeline touches has reached its
resting state **there**. It passed on the first run. The reveals were never the problem; the pinned
chapter overflowing the window was.

### Third pass: the mid range, and a gutter that scrolled itself away

768 / 899 / 900 / 1024 / 1279 / 1280 had never been looked at. Three things came out of it.

- **`scroll-snap-align: start` cancels a scroll container's own gutter.** It aligns the first item to
  the SCROLLPORT edge rather than the content box, so the browser scrolls the container by exactly
  its padding on load: measured `railScroll 56, chipX 0` on 56px of `padding-left`. The review rail
  and the mobile treatment path both sat flush against the window edge while every other section was
  inset. `scroll-padding-left` is the property that tells a snap container where its start edge
  actually is. Both now hold their gutter, and `verify-journey.mjs` asserts it at 390 and 1024.
- **Under One Roof ends on the full bleed marquee**, so its bottom padding was stacking with The
  Range's top padding: 208px of nothing between a rule and a heading at 900. Same treatment The
  People and The Proof already had.
- **The four figures** paired up below 900 rather than 768. Four of them in a 700px row put every
  label on two lines.

The overflow sweep now covers **360 / 390 / 768 / 899 / 900 / 1024 / 1279 / 1280 / 1440** — the
layout boundary and the type-tier boundary as well as the device widths, because a break happens at
a boundary and nobody photographs 899.

`probe.mjs` was added while chasing the gutter: one expression against the built site at any width.
`PROBE_W=1024 PROBE_STILL=1 node probe.mjs "<expr>"`. It is how the rail was diagnosed in one run
instead of by reading screenshots.

**The hero lede was left alone.** It reads small against a 120px headline at 1920, but 1.125rem is
Dr. Nahlah's `--body-lg` and her `.ar-lead` uses exactly that. Matching her proportions was the
instruction, so the ratio stands.

### Fourth pass: the drawer, driven

**The header was trapping its own close button.** The drawer is `position: fixed; z-index: 85` and
the toggle was static, and a positioned element paints above a non-positioned one whatever the DOM
order. The drawer's 6.5rem of top padding is exactly the strip the toggle sits in, so with the menu
open, the point at the centre of the button hit-tested to a **`.navbar_link`**: tapping to close the
drawer *navigated*. Both live inside the header's stacking context, so one z-index above the drawer
is the whole fix.

No screenshot can show this. The button is drawn correctly; it is simply not on top. It took
driving the drawer and calling `elementFromPoint` on the button's own centre. `verify-journey.mjs`
now opens the drawer, checks the header inverts back to ink for it, hit-tests the toggle, closes it
by clicking whatever is actually there, and asserts the page did not navigate.

**Three doctors in three columns needed more width than the 900 to 1023 band has** — each bio got a
33 character measure, which is a column of confetti rather than a paragraph. From 700 to 1023 each
doctor is now a **row**: portrait left at 13rem, credential and bio right at about 62 characters.
Card height went from 534px to 260px. Three across returns at 1024.

Two things that cost a cycle there, both worth remembering:

- **Five children auto-placed into a two column grid interleave.** The bio landed in the portrait's
  208px column while the 630px column held the role. Placement is explicit now.
- **A later declaration of equal specificity beats one inside a media query.** `.pe-plate {
  margin-bottom: 1.5rem }` sat after the band block and undid its `margin-bottom: 0`. The block was
  moved below every `.pe-*` base rule. This is the same shape as the `.ch .wrap` bug and the
  `.ch p` bug: **in this stylesheet, order is the thing that bites, not specificity.**

### Fifth pass: one stack breakpoint, and the shorthand bug's other half

**The page was turning at three different widths.** 900 for the relay, the pair and the arcs; 960
for both chapter heads, the pull quote and the invitation; 1024 for the doctors. Dragging a window
across that range made the layout reassemble in three separate jolts, which is a good part of what
"responsiveness" reads as when somebody resizes a page to test it. Everything two-column now turns
at **900**. The doctors keep 1024, because theirs is a different question: not one column against
two, but two against three.

**The `padding` shorthand had exactly the bug the `margin` shorthand had.** `.ch .wrap` is (0,2,0)
and `padding: 0 var(--bleed)` resets `padding-block` to 0, so `.iv-top { padding-bottom: 4.5rem }`
on an element carrying both classes was silently dropped and the FAQ heading sat **14px** under the
opening hours. The margin half of this was found and fixed on the first pass; the padding half sat
beside it untouched for four passes. Both are now longhand.

**The address wrapped onto four lines at 900** because `.iv-facts` was three columns from 700px of
*viewport*, inside a copy column that is 393px wide there. It is `repeat(auto-fit, minmax(11rem,
1fr))` now, so it sizes against the space it actually has: two columns at 900, three at 1440.

**`--f-body` was declared in the documented scale and used nowhere**, while eight rules hand-rolled
a value 2.6% away from it. A token nothing uses is a lie about the scale, so the rules adopted it.

Both remaining interactions are now driven rather than photographed. The FAQ is asserted by the
**section growing** and `checkVisibility()` per answer, because a closed `<details>` child still
reports a bounding box in Chrome and measuring the answer proves nothing. The treatment path is
asserted under real touch emulation at 390 and 1000: it branches on `(hover:none) and
(pointer:coarse)`, not on width, so **that branch cannot be reached by resizing** and it is the one
most of this practice's traffic gets.

### Sixth pass: what it costs a visitor, and who can reach it

**Fifty two focusable links, off screen, ahead of the page.** The mobile drawer was pushed aside
with a `transform`, and a drawer pushed off screen with a transform is still in the accessibility
tree and still focusable. A keyboard or switch user tabbing off the logo at 390 walked through all
52 of them, sitting at `x=415`, before reaching any page content. `visibility: hidden` takes them
out of the tab order; transitioning it alongside the transform keeps them present for the whole
slide, which is the mechanism `.navbar_scrim` in the same file already used. Nothing visual shows
this and no screenshot ever could. It was **pre-existing on all 74 pages**, not new.

**Weight, measured rather than assumed.** A phone fetches **371 KB uncompressed** for the homepage
across 10 requests: CSS 97, JS 147, HTML 58, images 68. The dev server sends no compression, so on a
real host the text drops to roughly 14 / 48 / 12 and the total lands near **140 KB**. The hero
correctly resolves to `g7-410x273.jpg` at 58 KB rather than the 1536px original, and `weigh.mjs`
puts the homepage at **0.17 MB on load** against 0.15 MB for the band design it replaced. GSAP and
`journey.js` load on the homepage only, all four `defer`, none render blocking.

> `site.css` is 97 KB and all 74 pages fetch it, including the 42 KB journey layer that only the
> homepage uses. It is one cached file for the whole site so the cost is paid once, and Instatic
> takes a single site-wide code asset, so splitting it is not free. Worth revisiting only if the
> core-pages pass leaves most pages still not using it.

**2400px closes the fluid root at both ends.** Root 20px, container 1950px, headline 133px, lede at
55 characters, the pinned chapter fitting the window exactly at 900 = 900, no overflow.

### Seventh pass: chapters depart, and the pin was mismeasuring everything below it

`DESIGN-STANDARD.md` item 4 of the Definition of Done asks that a chapter both **arrive and
depart**. Arrival is the easy half and the one everybody ships; only the hero had an exit. The other
seven have one now.

**What departs is the head, not the chapter.** The reference scrubs its whole scene out on
`start:'top top', end:'bottom top'`, which is right for a scene exactly one viewport tall. These are
not: The Range runs three screens. Fading a whole chapter from the moment its top touches the
viewport top dims the paragraph somebody is still reading several hundred pixels lower. So the tag
and the headline recede as **they** pass the top edge, which is the part of the scene that is
actually leaving, and the media keeps its own `[data-drift]` parallax underneath.

The trigger is the head's own box rather than the section, for the same reason the pin needs it: in
The Path the section is held at the viewport top for the whole scene, so a section-anchored exit
would fade the heading on arrival and hold it faded until the pin released.

**And the departures exposed a latent bug in everything below Chapter 05.** The Proof's heading read
**0.05 opacity while still half a screen from the top edge** — its departure had already run.
Pinning inserts a spacer, so every element below the pin moves down by the length of the pin, and a
trigger refreshed *before* the pin measures a document that does not yet contain it. The pin now
carries `refreshPriority: 1` so it is measured first. **Every trigger below Chapter 05 had the same
error**; the departures merely made one of them visible. The arrival reveals of The Proof and The
Invitation had been firing at the wrong scroll positions all along.

One check had to be corrected rather than the code: the reveal check was flagging the invitation's
headline at `y=-152` with 4px still showing. An element whose top edge has passed the viewport top
is in its departure, and a departure is a fade by definition. **Arrival and departure are different
assertions**; check 9 now judges only what is arriving and 9a covers the rest.

### Eighth pass: the system is frozen

`DESIGN-STANDARD.md`'s Canary Gate: *"finish one representative canary page first ... freeze the
approved token set, shell, type scale, and interaction runtime for that batch before copying the
system across the other pages."* The homepage was the canary. **`site/chapters.mjs` is the freeze.**

Eighteen named parts, all moved verbatim out of `site/home.mjs`: `chapter` and `tag` (the shell and
the continuity device), `arrival`, `figures`, `versus`, `marquee`, `arc`, `plate` / `plates`,
`band`, `path`, `quote`, `quoteRail`, `badges`, `facts`, `faqList`, plus `button`, `linkArrow` and
`wrap`. The homepage now composes them; an inner page gets the same vocabulary without copying it,
and a change to a chapter's shape happens in one place.

**The extraction is provably a refactor.** The built page was snapshotted first and diffed after
every group, tag by tag with whitespace normalised. **Zero lines of difference** through all four
groups. That is the only way to move this much markup and be able to say the design did not move
with it; do the same if it is refactored again.

One thing genuinely changed, and it was a bug the extraction surfaced: `versus` had been building
its `aria-label` by gluing the two column headers together, which announces *"At Mason Park compared
with Two separate practices"* to a screen reader. The visible headers are column labels; an
accessible name is a sentence. It is an authored parameter now.

`audit.mjs` is what keeps the library honest: it fails the build on a class the stylesheet does not
carry, so `chapters.mjs` and `theme/journey.css` cannot drift apart silently.

### Ninth pass: the first core page, and what it cost

`/our-doctors/` is the second page on the chapter system. It was three alternating split sections
and a booking band — the same architecture the homepage was rebuilt out of, and the same failure:
swapping two doctors changed nothing, because nothing connected them.

**The order is an argument now.** Dr. Nguyen leads *alone*, in her own chapter, because a board
certified pediatric dentist in the building is the thing that makes this practice different from
every other one in Katy. The two general dentists follow as a pair, because their story is the range
they cover between them. Then what all three hold in common, then the invitation.

**What the page cost:** two new library parts (`arrival({compact})` and `spread`), about 60 lines of
CSS, and a rewrite of one template function. **No new chapter mechanics, no new motion, no new
tokens.** That is the number worth having: the remaining nine core pages are template rewrites, not
designs.

The page found one content bug the old layout hid. The crawler returns Dr. Nguyen's personal line
and her philosophy statement as **one paragraph run together with no space** (`...national
parks.“My philosophy focuses on...`). `fixRunOn` cannot repair it because the sentence boundary has
a quotation mark in it, and setting ninety words as a single display-size aside is a wall. They are
split at the opening quote now and set as an aside and a pull quote, which is what they are.

#### The harness was flattering itself, and that is the real lesson

The first run reported **53 of 53 on both pages**, which should have been impossible: `/our-doctors/`
has no relay, no pinned path, no figures row and no FAQ. Git Bash had rewritten the route argument
into a Windows path, the strip left a bare name, and the server's directory fallback **quietly served
the homepage twice**. The runbook already records this trap for `shot.mjs`; `verify-journey.mjs` had
it too and nobody had noticed because it had only ever been pointed at one page.

Six harness defects came out of pointing it at a second page, none of them defects in the page:

1. The route is forced back to a leading and trailing slash, so a mangled argument cannot silently
   fall through to `/`.
2. Homepage-only checks now **SKIP, announced**, rather than fail. A part a page does not have is
   not a failure and it is not a pass; the summary counts them separately so "37 checks" on a
   content page cannot read as a clean sweep of the homepage's 53.
3. The pin is asserted only where a treatment path exists. Forcing every page to pin something would
   be motion for its own sake. What stays unconditional is the other half of the requirement: scroll
   has to **drive** content in at least three places, which a content page meets through scrubbed
   departures and drifting media.
4. The drawer check compared `location.pathname` against a hardcoded `"/"`.
5. Guards have to sit **inside** the page expression: a throw there makes `Runtime.evaluate` return
   `undefined`, and the Node-side guard then throws on the undefined instead of reporting a missing
   part.
6. `process.exit()` truncates pending stdout on a pipe and had been eating the summary line.
   `process.exitCode` lets the process drain.

**A verification harness pointed at one page is a harness that has never been tested.**

### Tenth pass: `/our-services/`, and a duplicate the old layout hid

Third page on the system. It was a hero, a grid of icon tiles, a grid of link cards and a booking
band. Now: arrival, **The Range** (the three arcs, the scannable version), **Everything** (the
complete A to Z), **The Cost**, **The Invitation**.

**The treatment taxonomy moved to `site/catalogue.mjs`.** Two pages need the three arcs and neither
owns them: the homepage shows them as the index of what is offered, `/our-services/` shows the same
three and then everything else beneath. The homepage's built output was diffed after the move and
came back **byte-identical**, as it should for a pure extraction.

**Nothing on the index is hand-typed.** The groups are read off the catalogue and the remainder is
whatever service route the build renders that the arcs do not already carry, so **adding a service
page cannot silently fail to appear**. One new library part, `columnIndex`: cards make you read a
box per item, and a column of hairlines lets the eye run down a list the way it does in a book,
which is what a "show me everything" page owes a visitor.

#### The duplicate, and why the first fix was wrong

The complete index surfaced something the card grid had hidden: the site carries **two implant
pages**, `/services/dental-implants/` and `/services/dental-implant-placement/`, which both reduce
to the label **"Dental Implants"** and **both have zero content blocks**. Listing the same words
twice against two URLs is indistinguishable to a reader.

The first fix was to dedupe by label. `audit.mjs` immediately caught what that actually did:
`orphan: nothing on the site links here`. **Dropping a duplicate orphans a page**, which is worse
than the duplicate.

The fix belongs upstream. Each arc item is now named for the page it actually opens, so the arc
reads "Dental Implant Placement" and the index lists "Dental Implants" separately. No duplicate, no
orphan, and no invented label. A collision now **throws at build time** with a message saying to
name the arc item rather than drop the page, so the wrong fix cannot be reached again.

> **Client question, not a build problem:** two implant service pages with near-identical names and
> no content on either. Which one is meant to exist? Same for whether `/services/dental-implants/`
> should be an overview above the specific procedure pages.
