The Treatment

What it is, and what it involves.

What is root canal therapy?

The infected pulp is taken out of the tooth, the canals inside are cleaned and shaped, and the tooth is sealed. The pain goes with the infection, and the tooth stays in your mouth.

In the practice’s words

Root canal therapy is a dental procedure designed to treat an infected, decayed, or injured tooth. Inside every tooth is a soft tissue called pulp, containing nerves and blood vessels. When the pulp becomes infected due to deep decay, trauma, or cracks, it can cause severe pain and lead to tooth loss if left untreated. Endodontic treatment involves removing the infected pulp, cleaning the inner canals, and sealing the tooth to restore its function and appearance.

The dental pulp, located in the center of your tooth, is vital during your tooth’s developmental years. It contains blood vessels, nerves, and connective tissue. Once your tooth is fully mature, it can survive without the pulp. When bacteria invade the pulp due to decay, cracks, or trauma, the infection can spread beyond the root, causing severe damage.

The technology the practice uses for it

  • Digital x‑raysAn accurate diagnosis before anything is opened.
  • Rotary endodonticsFaster cleaning and shaping of the canals.
  • Gutta‑perchaA biocompatible filling that seals the canals against re-infection.
  • A crown afterHigh-quality restorative crowns are part of why these last.

Not needing one at all

Prevention is cheaper than every treatment on this page, and this list is the whole of it.

  • Brush and flossDaily, without exception. Most root canals start as a cavity.
  • Come inFor exams and cleanings, so decay is found while it is still small.
  • Act earlyAddress cavities and dental injuries promptly, not when they start hurting.
  • Wear a guardA mouth guard during sport protects against the cracks that reach the pulp.
  • Do not chewIce, pens or anything hard enough to crack a tooth.

Questions

Questions patients ask before they decide.

Is a root canal painful?

With modern technology and anesthetics, root canal therapy is virtually painless. It relieves the pain you already have — that is what it is for.

Would it not be simpler to pull the tooth?

Extraction should be the last resort. A root canal saves your natural tooth, maintaining your bite alignment and jawbone integrity. Extraction often leads to the need for an implant or a bridge, which is more expensive and more time-consuming. Keeping your natural teeth means better oral function and appearance.

Do root canals cause illness?

No. This outdated theory has been debunked by scientific research. Root canal therapy is safe and effective for treating infection. What does affect your health is leaving the infection untreated: bacteria can enter the bloodstream and contribute to heart infections, respiratory issues and a weakened immune system.

What happens if I put it off?

The infection can spread to other teeth or to the jawbone. An abscess can form, causing severe facial swelling. The tooth can be lost, which then needs an implant or a bridge. And dental infections can affect your general health.

Will I need a crown afterwards?

Usually. A crown protects the treated tooth from fracture, restores normal chewing and speaking, and improves the tooth’s appearance. A treated tooth is brittle without one.

How long will the tooth last?

Modern root canal treatment has a success rate of over 95%, and the treated tooth can last a lifetime with proper care. Precision cleaning and sealing of the canals, a high-quality restorative crown, and your own commitment to oral hygiene are what get it there.
