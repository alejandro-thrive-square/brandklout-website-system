Is this you

Three ages, three different problems.

The same restriction causes something different at every stage of life, which is why it is so often missed after infancy.

01

Infants

Difficulty latching, poor milk intake, slow weight gain, feeds that go on and on. And for the mother, significant nipple pain or clogged ducts.

02

Children

Speech development, eating solid food, or keeping teeth clean. It can also interfere with tooth alignment and jaw development.

03

Adults

Often never diagnosed as a child. Chronic discomfort, oral hygiene difficulty, speech issues, and periodontal problems such as gum recession.

The two conditions, and what each one restricts

  • Tongue‑tieAnkyloglossia. The lingual frenum — the fold under the tongue — is too short or tight, restricting tongue movement. Speech impediments, difficulty swallowing, and complications with breastfeeding.
  • Lip‑tieThe labial frenum, joining the upper lip to the gum above the front teeth, is overly restrictive. It prevents proper lip movement, can contribute to a gap between the front teeth, and makes brushing and flossing harder.

The Treatment

What it is, and what it involves.

What is a frenectomy?

A frenum is a small fold of connective tissue in the mouth. When one is too short or too tight it restricts movement. A frenectomy removes or modifies it, and the restriction goes with it.

In the practice’s words

A frenectomy is a minimally invasive dental procedure that involves the removal or modification of a frenum — a small fold of connective tissue located in the mouth. The lingual frenum connects the underside of the tongue to the floor of the mouth; the labial frenum connects the inside of the upper lip to the gum above the front teeth.

Both conditions are more than just physical restrictions — they can impact emotional well-being, social interactions, and everyday comfort. The goal is to restore mobility and eliminate these restrictions, improving oral function and overall comfort.

Why it is done with a laser here, and not a scalpel

  • PrecisionOnly the necessary tissue is removed, leaving what is around it untouched. Minimal trauma.
  • Minimal bleedingThe laser cauterises blood vessels as it works.
  • No stitchesIt seals the tissue, so sutures are rarely needed. That matters most for the youngest patients.
  • Less swellingLess damage to adjacent tissue means less inflammation afterwards.
  • Faster recoveryMost patients return to eating and speaking within hours.
  • Lower infection riskThe laser’s sterilising effect on the site.

Why This One

What it does for you.

A tongue-tie sounds like a small thing until you are three weeks into a feed that is not working.

Feeding, almost immediately

For infants, improvements in feeding are often immediate. It changes the mother’s experience as much as the baby’s.

Speech and articulation

In children, restoring full mobility often improves pronunciation and articulation.

Dental spacing

A restrictive labial frenum can hold a gap open between the front teeth.

You can clean your teeth properly

Brushing and flossing become possible where the lip or tongue would not move out of the way.

Less strain, for adults

Improved tongue range of motion and reduced muscular strain, decades after it should have been caught.

Gum recession slows

A pulling frenum is a periodontal problem as well as a functional one.

The Path

What happens, step by step.

01

Comprehensive consultation

A detailed examination of the oral tissues. For infants and children we may observe feeding or speech patterns; for adults, speech, hygiene and physical limitation.

02

Diagnosis and planning

Whether there is a tie and how severe it is, then the procedure explained in full — benefits, aftercare and expected outcome — before anything is agreed.

03

The laser procedure

The tight frenum is gently released. Usually complete in under 15 minutes, with no sutures and no extended downtime.

04

Aftercare instructions

Oral stretches to ensure proper healing and prevent the tissue reattaching. This is the part that decides whether it worked.

05

Follow-up

Healing and function checked. Where feeding or speech is involved we may work alongside your lactation consultant or speech therapist.

Afterwards, and the one thing not to skip

Recovery is quick and uncomplicated. The stretches are not optional — released tissue can reattach.

  • Daily stretchesThe oral exercises Dr. Sung sets, done as instructed. This is what prevents reattachment.
  • InfantsContinue breastfeeding or bottle-feeding as usual, straight away.
  • Children and adultsMaintain good oral hygiene, and avoid hard or spicy food for a short while.
  • Follow-upsAttend all of them. Healing and function are checked, not assumed.
  • ExpectMinor discomfort only. Swelling or mild bleeding resolves within 24 to 48 hours.

Questions

Questions patients ask before they decide.

How long does it take?

The procedure itself is usually complete in under 15 minutes, and requires no sutures or extended downtime.

Does it hurt?

Patients report little to no discomfort during or after, thanks to the laser. Infants, children and adults all benefit from that. Minor swelling or mild bleeding resolves within 24 to 48 hours.

Can my baby feed straight afterwards?

Yes. Continue breastfeeding or bottle-feeding as usual, and improvements in feeding are often immediate.

Will the tissue grow back?

It can reattach, which is why the daily oral stretches Dr. Sung prescribes are the most important part of aftercare. Do them as instructed and attend the follow-up.

I am an adult. Is it too late?

No. Some tongue-tie and lip-tie conditions persist into adulthood, causing hygiene difficulty, chronic discomfort, speech issues or gum recession. A laser frenectomy improves tongue range of motion, reduces muscular strain and supports better dental health at any age.

Do you work with our lactation consultant?

Yes. In cases involving feeding habits or speech development we work collaboratively with lactation consultants and speech therapists. Bring their notes to the consultation.
