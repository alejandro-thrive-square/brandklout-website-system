Is this you

Is this the treatment you need?

The practice’s own list. The first one is the one nearly everybody has and nearly everybody ignores.

01

Bleeding when you brush or floss

This is not normal and it is not because you brushed too hard. It is the first sign.

02

Chronic bad breath

Bacteria living in pockets below the gumline, where a brush cannot reach them.

03

Red, swollen or tender gums

Inflammation, which is the body responding to an infection it cannot clear.

04

Receding gumlines

Teeth looking longer than they used to. The gum is pulling away from them.

05

Visible plaque and tartar

Hardened deposit at the gumline that brushing no longer removes.

06

Pockets, or loose teeth

A gap opening between tooth and gum, or a tooth that has begun to shift. This is the late stage.

And who is most at risk of needing one

  • HygienePoor habits, or a long stretch where oral health slipped down the list.
  • TobaccoSmoking or any tobacco use.
  • Family historyGum disease runs in families more than most people expect.
  • Systemic conditionsDiabetes especially, which works in both directions.
  • Hormonal changePregnancy and menopause both raise the risk.
  • Dry mouthUsually a side effect of medication, and it removes your natural defence.

The Treatment

What it is, and what it involves.

What is scaling and root planing?

Plaque, tartar and bacteria removed from below the gumline, then the tooth roots smoothed so bacteria have nothing to hold onto. A regular cleaning works on the tooth surfaces; this works under the gum.

In the practice’s words

A deep cleaning, also known as scaling and root planing, is a specialized treatment that removes plaque, tartar, and bacteria from below the gumline. While a regular cleaning targets the surfaces of your teeth, a periodontal cleaning goes much deeper to stop gum disease in its tracks.

Without scaling and root planing, harmful bacteria thrive in deep pockets, leading to persistent gum infections, gum recession, bone deterioration around teeth, and eventual tooth loss.

How Dr. Sung decides whether you need one

  • Pocket depthsMeasured with a gentle probe around each tooth. Deeper pockets signal infection.
  • Plaque and tartarSpecifically what has built up below the gumline, not what is visible above it.
  • InflammationRedness, swelling and bleeding, recorded rather than eyeballed.
  • Tooth mobilityAny looseness, which indicates bone loss has already started.
  • Digital x‑raysFor the bone level, which is the part none of the above can show directly.

Why This One

What it does for you.

Gum disease is the leading cause of adult tooth loss, and it is almost entirely silent until the bone has gone.

Early gum disease reverses

Gingivitis caught here goes away. Past a certain point it stops being reversible.

The bacteria are removed

From the pockets under the gum, which is the only place they are actually living.

Bone loss stops

The bone around a tooth is what holds it in. Once it goes it does not come back.

Smooth roots stay clean

Planing the root surface leaves nothing for plaque to accumulate on.

Your breath changes

The unglamorous benefit, and often the one patients notice first.

Lower systemic risk

Gum infection is linked to heart disease, stroke, diabetes control, respiratory illness and Alzheimer’s.

What untreated gum disease is linked to, beyond the mouth

Medical research links periodontal disease to a range of serious health conditions:

  • Heart disease. Bacteria from infected gums can contribute to arterial plaque buildup.
  • Stroke. Inflammation caused by gum disease may increase stroke risk.
  • Diabetes complications. Infections worsen blood sugar control, and poor control worsens the infection.
  • Respiratory illness. Oral bacteria can be aspirated into the lungs.
  • Alzheimer’s disease. Studies suggest gum disease bacteria have been found in the brain tissue of Alzheimer’s patients.

Choosing a deep cleaning now supports a healthier future not just for your mouth but for your whole body.

The Path

What happens, step by step.

01

Examination

Dr. Sung examines the gums and measures the pocket depth around every tooth. That map is the treatment plan.

02

Scaling

Plaque and tartar removed from beneath the gumline with instruments made for it.

03

Root planing

The root surfaces are smoothed, so bacteria have nothing rough to recolonise.

04

Antibiotic therapy, if needed

Localised antibiotics placed in the pockets to help the tissue heal. Not every case.

05

Follow-up and maintenance

Healing checked, pockets re-measured, and a maintenance plan set. This step is what makes the first four hold.

The Alternatives

How it compares.

A deep cleaning, against a regular one.

Deep cleaning vs Regular cleaning
Below the gumline. The tooth surfaces above it.
Treats an existing infection. Prevents one starting.
The roots are planed smooth. The crowns are polished.
Local anaesthetic, usually. None needed.
Then maintenance every 3–4 months. Every 6 months.

These are not alternatives you choose between. Which one you need is decided by your pocket depths, and Dr. Sung will show you the numbers. If your gums are healthy, you want the regular one.

Afterwards, and why it is every three months

Completing the scaling is the beginning. Gum disease returns if the maintenance does not.

  • Cleanings every 3–4 monthsRather than six. The pockets recolonise faster than healthy gums do.
  • Medicated rinsesWhere the tissue needs help settling down.
  • Antibacterial treatmentApplied during maintenance visits, not only at the first one.
  • Periodontal evaluationsPockets re-measured, so healing is a number and not an impression.

Alongside it: the technique work that keeps the pockets clean between visits, and laser treatment where the tissue needs more than scaling.

Questions

Questions patients ask before they decide.

Is a deep cleaning painful?

This is the commonest worry and the practice addresses it directly: with anaesthesia and gentle care, most patients experience minimal discomfort. The area is numbed before the scaling starts.

Do I really need this, or is it an upsell?

A fair question, and the answer is measurable rather than a matter of opinion. Dr. Sung measures the pocket depth around each tooth, records inflammation and mobility, and takes digital x‑rays for the bone level. If the numbers do not indicate gum disease, you want a regular cleaning instead, and he will say so.

What is the difference from a regular cleaning?

A regular cleaning focuses on the tooth surfaces above the gumline and is part of a routine checkup. A deep cleaning targets plaque and tartar beneath the gumline and is necessary for patients with gum disease.

Will my gums grow back?

Inflammation settles and pockets shrink, and early gum disease can be reversed. Gum tissue and bone that have already been lost do not regenerate on their own, which is why the timing of this treatment matters so much.

Why do I need cleanings every three months afterwards?

Because the pockets recolonise faster than healthy gums do. The maintenance programme — cleanings every 3 to 4 months, medicated rinses, antibacterial treatment and periodontal evaluations — is what prevents the disease returning.

Can gum disease really affect the rest of my health?

Medical research links periodontal disease to heart disease, stroke, diabetes complications, respiratory illness, and Alzheimer’s disease. An untreated gum infection is a chronic bacterial infection with a blood supply.
