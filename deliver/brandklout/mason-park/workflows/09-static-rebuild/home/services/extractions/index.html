When a tooth has to come out.

Dr. Sung preserves natural teeth wherever it is possible. These five are the situations where leaving one in does more harm than taking it out.

01

Decay past saving

No filling or crown will hold it. Left in, it harbours bacteria that reach the teeth beside it.

02

Advanced gum disease

The structures holding the tooth have gone and it can no longer be stabilised.

03

Impacted wisdom teeth

Trapped under the gum, causing pain, infection, cysts, or damage to the tooth in front.

04

Making room

Where crowding means braces or aligners have nowhere to move the teeth to.

05

A fracture below the gum

Cracked under the gumline or split vertically, after a fall or a sports injury.

The Treatment

What it is, and what it involves.

Simple, or surgical?

A simple extraction is for a tooth you can see and reach: numbed, loosened, lifted out. A surgical one is for a tooth broken at the gumline, not fully erupted, or with long or curved roots — a small incision, sometimes a little bone, then careful closure.

In the practice’s words

Simple tooth extractions are the most common type and are typically needed when a tooth is visible, intact, and easily accessible above the gumline. Local anesthesia is administered to numb the area completely, Dr. William Sung gently loosens the tooth using specialized dental instruments, and the tooth is carefully removed with minimal pressure and discomfort.

Surgical tooth extractions involve making a small incision into the gum tissue to access the tooth, removing bone if necessary to safely extract it, and careful closure of the area to promote smooth healing. Dr. Sung uses advanced surgical techniques to minimize trauma, reduce discomfort, and support faster recovery.

Four kinds, and what each one is for

  • SimpleSeverely decayed teeth, broken teeth above the gumline, non-impacted teeth.
  • SurgicalImpacted teeth, broken roots, complex extractions.
  • Wisdom teethPreventing or treating overcrowding, infection and impaction. Planned on digital imaging, with sedation offered.
  • EmergencyUrgent tooth pain, infection, dental trauma. Same-day appointments wherever possible.

Why This One

What it does for you.

Nobody wants an extraction. These are the practice’s own five reasons it is sometimes the healthiest thing available.

The pain ends

A severely damaged or infected tooth is the source of it. Removing it removes the pain.

The infection stops spreading

It does not travel into the healthy teeth and gums around it.

The bite can be corrected

Space, where crowding was preventing orthodontic treatment from working.

The jawbone is protected

An infected tooth left in place damages the bone that holds the rest of them.

You can eat and speak again

And the replacement conversation can finally start.

Sedation, if you need it

Oral sedation, nitrous oxide, or IV sedation for complex cases and high anxiety.

Dr. William Sung, DMD

The three sedation options, in the practice’s words
  • Oral sedation. A simple pill helps you feel calm before and during your procedure.
  • Nitrous oxide (laughing gas). A light sedative that wears off quickly after treatment.
  • IV sedation. For more complex cases or high anxiety levels, deeper sedation ensures maximum comfort.

Ask about sedation when you schedule your consultation. We understand that dental procedures can be daunting, and this is not something you have to get through unaided.

The full detail of each stage

Thorough evaluation. Using advanced digital imaging and diagnostic tools, Dr. William Sung thoroughly examines the affected tooth, its surrounding gum tissues, and jawbone structure. This comprehensive assessment ensures we can plan the safest, most effective approach while protecting your overall oral health.

Customized treatment plan. After the evaluation, we take the time to discuss your specific case, address any concerns, and create a customized treatment plan. If you experience dental anxiety or prefer additional relaxation support, we offer sedation options. You will know exactly what to expect at every step.

Anesthesia administration. Before beginning, we apply a local anesthetic to completely numb the treatment area, ensuring a pain-free experience. For patients who need additional support, sedation helps you relax deeply while remaining safely monitored by our team.

Careful removal with advanced techniques. Dr. Sung uses advanced extraction techniques designed to minimize trauma to the surrounding tissues. Whether it’s a simple extraction or a more complex surgical removal, the focus is on protecting your oral health and reducing discomfort, and on preserving the surrounding bone and gums as much as possible.

Post-extraction care and healing support. We provide personalized post-operative instructions covering swelling, pain control, safe eating and oral hygiene, and we schedule follow-up visits to monitor your healing.

The Alternatives

What we try first.

Three things that save a tooth an extraction would take.

The alternative for The problem it solves
Root canal therapy Infected pulp. The tooth is kept and the infection removed.
A crown A severely damaged tooth that still has a root. Strength and function restored.
Periodontal therapy Gum disease, before it loosens the teeth past stabilising.
Extraction When none of the three above is still viable.

And after one: an implant, a bridge, or a partial denture. The gap is not meant to stay a gap.

The first 24 hours

Almost everything that goes wrong after an extraction goes wrong here, and almost all of it is avoidable.

  • Cold compressTo bring the swelling down.
  • No smoking, no strawsFor at least 24 hours. Suction pulls the clot out, and that is dry socket.
  • Soft foodYogurt, mashed potato, smoothies.
  • Take the medicationAs Dr. Sung prescribed it, not only when it starts hurting.
  • Clean, but not thereKeep up your hygiene and leave the extraction site alone at first.

Questions

Questions patients ask before they decide.

Will it hurt?

A local anesthetic numbs the treatment area completely before anything begins, which makes the procedure itself pain-free. If you are anxious, oral sedation, nitrous oxide or IV sedation are all available — ask when you schedule.

Could my tooth be saved instead?

Possibly, and we look at that first. Root canal therapy preserves a tooth with infected pulp, a crown restores a severely damaged one, and periodontal therapy stabilises teeth loosened by gum disease. Extraction is what happens when none of those is viable any more.

What is dry socket, and how do I avoid it?

It is what happens when the blood clot over the socket is dislodged before it has healed. Avoid smoking and drinking through a straw for at least 24 hours — both create suction — and leave the site alone while you brush.

When is an extraction an emergency?

Contact the practice immediately if you have a severe, persistent toothache, swelling or an abscess in the gums, a broken tooth with the root exposed, or trauma from an accident or a fall. Emergency extractions are prioritised.

What can I eat afterwards?

Soft foods for the first days — yogurt, mashed potatoes, smoothies. Use a cold compress for swelling, take the medication as prescribed, and keep up your oral hygiene while avoiding the extraction site itself at first.

What replaces the tooth?

A dental implant, a bridge, or a partial denture, depending on where the gap is and what the bone will take. Dr. Sung discusses replacement at the same appointment as the extraction, not months later.
