The Treatment

What it is, and what it treats.

What is laser dentistry?

A focused beam of light energy that cuts or vaporises tissue on contact, or strengthens the bond between a tooth and a filling. It reaches only what it is aimed at, and it sterilises as it goes.

In the practice’s words

Laser dentistry is the use of focused light beams to treat various dental conditions with extraordinary precision. Unlike traditional methods that often involve scalpels and drills, lasers allow us to treat the soft tissues and hard structures of the mouth more gently.

Dental lasers allow Dr. William Sung to target only the affected tissue while leaving healthy areas untouched. This precise control results in better outcomes and helps preserve as much of the natural tooth or gum structure as possible. Moreover, the laser’s sterilizing effect reduces the risk of infection significantly.

Five things the practice uses it for

  • Gum diseaseInfected gum tissue and bacteria removed with laser energy, encouraging healthy tissue to regrow. More comfortable than surgery, and an alternative to a traditional deep cleaning in some cases.
  • FrenectomyTongue-tie and lip-tie released gently, with minimal bleeding and no stitches. Faster healing, and often immediate improvement in function.
  • Cavity detection and removalDecay identified at its earliest stages, and removed without drilling. More of the natural tooth is preserved.
  • Gum contouring and gingivectomyThe gum line sculpted, for a gummy smile or gums overgrown through medication, with no cutting and no stitching.
  • Cold sores and ulcersApplied to a developing cold sore it can often stop it progressing, reducing symptoms and speeding healing.

Why This One

The practice’s own four advantages, and the two things the laser does that a blade simply cannot.

A more comfortable visit

Faster healing

What the beam actually does, procedure by procedure

The laser device emits a highly focused beam of light energy which interacts with your oral tissues in a controlled and specific way. Depending on the treatment, it may be used to:

  • Gently cut or remove soft tissue.
  • Eliminate decay from teeth without traditional drilling.
  • Reshape or contour the gums for better appearance and health.
  • Sterilize the treatment area to reduce the risk of infection.
  • Assist in whitening treatments by activating special whitening agents.

Your visit starts with a thorough evaluation: your dental history reviewed, a detailed examination, and an assessment of your current oral health. If you are a good candidate, Dr. Sung explains the recommended procedure, the benefits of the technology, and what to expect before, during and after.

The Alternatives

How it compares.

The laser, against the drill and the scalpel.

Laser vs Traditional
Often little or no anaesthetic. Local anaesthetic as standard.
No vibration, and no drill noise. Both, and both are what people dread.
Minimal bleeding; usually no sutures. Bleeding, and stitches on soft tissue.
Sterilises the site as it works. The site is cleaned separately.
Shorter recovery. Longer, with more swelling.

The laser is not a replacement for everything. A tooth needing a crown still needs a crown, and an extraction is still an extraction. It changes how the soft-tissue work is done.

Where it fits with everything else

Laser is a tool inside other treatments more often than it is a treatment of its own.

  • Gum diseaseAlongside or instead of scaling and root planing, depending on the tissue.
  • Tongue and lip tieThe frenectomy page covers this one in full.

Questions

Questions patients ask before they decide.

Will I need an injection?

In many cases the laser’s precision eliminates the need for local anaesthesia, which is a large part of why anxious patients prefer it. Dr. Sung will tell you which category your procedure falls into before you agree to it.

Does it hurt?

Dental lasers minimise or eliminate the need for drills and sutures, so there is less vibration, less pressure and less anxiety during treatment. Most patients describe it as easier and faster than the conventional version of the same work.

Will I need stitches?

Usually not for soft-tissue work. A laser frenectomy, for example, releases the restrictive tissue with minimal bleeding and no need for stitches.

How long does healing take?

Shorter than conventional methods. Because laser treatment causes less trauma to the tissue, patients usually experience minimal bleeding and swelling and recover faster.

Can a laser really remove a cavity without drilling?

Yes, in suitable cases. The laser targets the decayed area precisely, which preserves more of the natural tooth. It is also used to detect decay at its earliest stages, before there is much to remove.

Can everything be done with a laser?

No, and it would be misleading to suggest so. The practice uses it for gum disease treatment, frenectomy, cavity detection and removal, gum contouring, and cold sores and ulcers. Other treatments still use conventional instruments, and Dr. Sung will say which is which.
