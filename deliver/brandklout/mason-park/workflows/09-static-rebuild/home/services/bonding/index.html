Is this the treatment you need?

Bonding is for small flaws, and it is genuinely excellent at them. For anything structural it is the wrong answer, and Dr. Sung will say so.

01

The most common one, and the one bonding was made for.

Front teeth that have worn down slightly out of line with each other.

04

Small ones can be closed with resin instead of with aligners.

05

Discolouration whitening will not shift

Enamel that stays stained after bleaching can be covered instead.

06

Exposed roots

Where gum recession has left root surface uncovered and sensitive.

And when bonding is not the answer

  • Severe damageA tooth with structural loss needs a crown, which covers the whole tooth rather than patching it.
  • A whole-smile changeModerate to major cosmetic work is veneer territory, or a smile makeover.
  • A bite problemBonding assumes healthy enamel and a normal bite. If either is off, that gets addressed first.

The Treatment

What it is, and what it involves.

What is dental bonding?

A durable, tooth-coloured resin applied to the tooth and hardened under a curing light, then shaped and polished to match the teeth around it.

In the practice’s words

Dental bonding is a cosmetic dental procedure in which a durable, tooth-colored resin is applied to your teeth and hardened with a special curing light. This resin is shaped and polished to match the rest of your smile, resulting in a natural-looking restoration.

Because the procedure is non-invasive, dental bonding is ideal for patients who want immediate results without the commitment of more extensive treatments like veneers or crowns. It’s not just about applying resin — Dr. William Sung takes into account the shade, shape, and translucency of your existing teeth to ensure a flawless match. Each layer of the resin is meticulously sculpted and polished so that it reflects light like natural enamel.

Five reasons patients pick it over the alternatives

  • Minimally invasiveNo drilling and no anaesthetic, in most cases. Nothing is removed from the tooth.
  • One visitMost bonding is finished in a single appointment, often under an hour per tooth.
  • AffordableOne of the most cost-effective cosmetic treatments there is.
  • It matchesThe resin is colour-matched to your own enamel, shade and translucency included.
  • You see it immediatelyNo laboratory, no temporary, no second appointment. You leave with the result.

Why This One

What it does for you.

The case for bonding is that it is the smallest intervention that solves the problem. That is a real virtue and not a compromise.

Your enamel stays

Veneers may require enamel removal. Bonding adds material rather than taking it away, and enamel does not grow back.

Same-day result

Popular with people preparing for a specific event, for exactly this reason.

It is reversible in practice

Because nothing was cut away, a bonded tooth can later take a veneer or a crown if you want one.

Safe for children and teens

Conservative enough for a playground chip on a developing tooth, without compromising it.

It looks like the tooth

Layered and polished so it reflects light the way enamel does, not as a flat patch.

Three to ten years

Depending on where the tooth sits and how you treat it. Front teeth last longer.

The Path

What happens, step by step.

01

Evaluation

Your goals, your teeth, and whether bonding is genuinely the right treatment for what you want changed.

02

Tooth preparation

A gentle etch roughens the surface so the resin grips. No drilling, and nothing removed.

03

Application

Tooth-coloured resin applied and shaped to the contour the tooth should have.

04

Curing

A UV light hardens the resin in seconds. This is the whole of the “procedure” part.

05

Polishing

Refined until the bonded area reflects light like the enamel beside it. This is the step that decides whether anyone can tell.

This table is the practice’s own, including the durability row. Bonding does not last as long, and the page is not going to pretend otherwise.

Making it last the ten rather than the three

Three factors decide where in that range you land, and two of them are entirely within your control.

  • HygieneBrushing and flossing as normal. Resin does not decay, but the tooth under and around it does.
  • Where the tooth isFront teeth last longer, because they take less chewing force.
  • HabitsBiting nails, chewing pens, opening things with your teeth. Each of those is what chips a bond.
  • CheckupsRegular visits catch a bond starting to lift, while a touch-up is all it needs.

Questions

Questions patients ask before they decide.

How long does dental bonding last?

With proper care, 3 to 10 years. Where you land in that range depends on your oral hygiene, where the bonded tooth sits — front teeth last longer — and whether you bite your nails, chew on pens, or use your teeth as tools.

Does it hurt?

No drilling and no needles in most cases. The tooth is etched, the resin is applied and cured, and the area is polished. It is one of the few genuinely comfortable procedures in dentistry.

Should I have bonding or veneers?

Bonding is cheaper, takes one visit, is minimally invasive, and lasts 3–7 years — it suits minor flaws. Veneers cost more, take multiple visits, may require enamel removal, and last 10–15 years or more — they suit moderate to major cosmetic change. Dr. Sung will look at your teeth and tell you which case yours is.

Is bonding safe for children and teens?

Yes. It is a safe, conservative treatment for younger patients, especially for minor chips from playground injuries or small gaps in a growing smile. Because it is non-invasive and requires no enamel removal, it is often recommended until a more permanent solution is appropriate.

Will it match my other teeth?

That is the whole craft of it. Dr. Sung matches the shade, shape and translucency of your existing teeth, and each layer of resin is sculpted and polished so it reflects light the way natural enamel does.

Can bonded teeth be whitened later?

The resin will not lighten with whitening the way enamel does, so if you are planning to whiten, do that first and have the bonding matched to the new shade. Mention it at the consultation.
