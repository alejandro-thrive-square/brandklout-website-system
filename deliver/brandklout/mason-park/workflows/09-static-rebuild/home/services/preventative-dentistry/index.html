The Treatment

What it is, and what it involves.

What is preventative dentistry?

Finding a problem while it is still cheap and small. A cleaning, an examination, digital x‑rays that see under the surface, and a conversation about the habits that put you here.

In the practice’s words

Preventative dental care is your first line of defense against cavities, gum disease, enamel erosion, and tooth loss. It involves identifying and addressing oral health concerns early before they become major issues. With regular dental cleanings and exams, potential problems can be spotted early, allowing for timely treatment and prevention.

Beyond preserving oral health, preventative dentistry plays a crucial role in your overall well-being. Studies have shown strong links between oral health and systemic health conditions, including heart disease, diabetes, and respiratory illness. By prioritizing prevention, you’re not just caring for your smile — you’re taking steps to protect your entire body.

Our mission is simple: prevent problems before they start. This means more than just cleaning teeth — we assess your lifestyle, dental habits, and medical history to personalize your care.

Five things in the practice’s preventative programme

  • Exams and cleaningsPlaque, tartar and bacteria off; cavities, gum disease, jaw issues and soft-tissue changes looked for. Digital x‑rays, at less radiation, for what is under the surface.
  • FluorideA varnish after the cleaning. It hardens enamel, reduces sensitivity and can reverse the earliest weakening.
  • SealantsResin painted into the grooves of the molars, where a brush cannot reach. For children, teens, and adults whose molars are still intact.
  • Gum disease preventionPeriodontal charting, and scaling and root planing where the pockets call for it.
  • Oral cancer screeningLips, tongue, throat, cheeks and gums, at every exam. It is quick, and oral cancer is usually painless early.

Why This One

What it does for you.

The practice’s own five benefits. The fourth is the one that surprises people: a dental exam finds things that are not about teeth.

Fewer cavities and infections

Regular cleanings take the bacteria and plaque out before they become decay.

Gum disease caught early

Gingivitis is reversible. Periodontitis, which takes gum and bone with it, is not.

It costs less than the alternative

Prevention is always cheaper than the restorative work that replaces it.

It finds things beyond teeth

An oral exam can show signs of diabetes, nutritional deficiency, and osteoporosis.

Fresher breath, whiter teeth

The unglamorous benefit that is actually why most people book.

A plan built for you

More frequent cleanings if you build plaque quickly, custom fluoride for dry mouth, advice pitched at your actual habits.

Preventative care at four stages of a life
  • Children. Gentle cleanings, fluoride treatments to strengthen developing enamel, dental sealants for the hard-to-reach back teeth, and habit counselling to curb thumb-sucking and pacifier use early. We create a safe, playful environment where kids feel confident in the dental chair.
  • Teens. Cavity prevention strategies tailored for those with braces or aligners, nutrition and hygiene advice for developing smiles, and wisdom tooth monitoring through late adolescence.
  • Adults. Thorough exams that go beyond surface-level checks, professional cleanings, gum health maintenance to prevent or manage periodontal disease, and early intervention on minor issues before they become costly ones.
  • Seniors. Monitoring for tooth wear and enamel erosion, management of dry mouth (often caused by medications), oral cancer screenings at every check-up, and care for dentures or restorations to keep the comfort and fit right.

Whether it’s your child’s first cleaning, your teen’s Invisalign checkup, or your own biannual visit, everyone receives age-appropriate care in one location. Dr. Tam Nguyen, the pediatric dentist, practises across the hall.

Questions

Questions patients ask before they decide.

How often should I visit for preventative care?

Most patients benefit from visits every six months, though Dr. Dave Cho may recommend more frequent care based on your specific needs. A history of gum disease, smoking, diabetes or dental appliances can all mean quarterly checkups.

Are fluoride treatments safe?

Yes. Fluoride treatments are safe, fast, and effective. They help strengthen enamel and are especially helpful for children or patients prone to cavities or sensitivity.

It has been years. Is that a problem?

It is a reason to come, not a reason to stay away. The team is described by its own patients as friendly and non-judgmental, and the visit starts where you are rather than where you should have been.

What does the exam actually check?

Dr. Cho examines your mouth for signs of cavities, gum disease, jaw issues and abnormalities in the soft tissues, and includes a visual and tactile oral cancer screening of the lips, tongue, throat, cheeks and gums. Digital x‑rays show what is below the surface: hidden decay, bone loss and impacted teeth.

Are sealants only for children?

No. Sealants are most often recommended for children and teens, but adults without fillings or decay in their molars benefit from them too. They are a non-invasive, affordable way to lower cavity risk.

Can a dental visit tell me anything about my general health?

It can. Oral exams can reveal signs of health problems including diabetes, nutritional deficiencies and osteoporosis, and there are strong documented links between oral health and heart disease, diabetes and respiratory illness.
