The Treatment

What it is, and what it involves.

What is crown lengthening?

A surgical procedure that reshapes the gum tissue — and sometimes the bone underneath it — to expose more of the tooth. The bone part is what separates it from simple gum reshaping.

In the practice’s words

Crown lengthening is a dental surgical procedure that reshapes the gum tissue — and sometimes underlying bone — to expose more of a tooth’s structure. It’s commonly performed to prepare a tooth for a crown or bridge, to improve the esthetics of a gummy smile, and to assist with other restorative dental treatments.

Restorative procedures like dental crowns, bridges, or veneers rely on sufficient tooth structure. Crown lengthening ensures that restorations fit securely and perform well long-term. By removing a small portion of the gum and bone, Dr. William Sung creates the perfect environment for durable and beautiful dental restorations.

Crown lengthening, against gum contouring

  • Gum contouringReshapes the gums only, for cosmetic improvement. No bone is touched, and it heals faster.
  • Crown lengtheningMay involve bone adjustment, to create space for a restoration and improve how the tooth functions.
  • Which one you needDecided by whether the goal is appearance alone or a foundation for something. Dr. Sung will tell you which yours is.

The risks, stated plainly

Crown lengthening is generally safe, and like any procedure it carries minor risks:

  • Temporary tooth sensitivity. The newly exposed surface was under the gum until now.
  • Slight changes in smile symmetry where multiple teeth are involved. This is why the planning matters.
  • Teeth appearing longer. Listed as a risk, though in cosmetic cases it is the entire point — worth knowing which category you are in before you agree.

Dr. Sung takes every precaution to minimise the risks and maximise the result.

Questions

Questions patients ask before they decide.

What is the difference from gum contouring?

Gum contouring reshapes the gums only, purely for cosmetic improvement. Crown lengthening may also involve adjusting the bone, to create space for a restoration and improve how the tooth functions. Dr. Sung will tell you which your case needs.

Will my teeth look longer afterwards?

Yes, and that is usually the intention — particularly in cosmetic cases where a gummy smile is the reason for the procedure. It is worth being clear before you agree whether you are in the functional or the cosmetic category.

Is it painful?

The procedure is done under local anaesthetic, and the practice uses minimally invasive tissue and bone reshaping specifically to promote faster healing and minimal discomfort. Temporary sensitivity afterwards is the commonest complaint.

Why do I need this before a crown?

Because a crown needs enough tooth structure above the gum line to hold onto. Where a tooth has fractured below the gum, or too little is exposed, the restoration will not fit securely or last. This creates that foundation.

What should I do beforehand?

Dr. Sung may recommend a deep cleaning to remove plaque and tartar, discontinuing certain medications that could interfere with healing, stopping smoking, and discussing any health conditions that may affect recovery. Good preparation means faster healing and a better outcome.

Are there risks?

Minor ones: temporary tooth sensitivity, and slight changes in smile symmetry where several teeth are involved. Both are planned around, and the practice states them openly rather than after the fact.
