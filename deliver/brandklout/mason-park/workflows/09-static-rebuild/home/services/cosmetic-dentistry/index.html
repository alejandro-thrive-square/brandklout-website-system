The Treatment

Five treatments, and how they combine.

What is a smile makeover?

Not a treatment — a plan. Several cosmetic treatments combined and sequenced to reach a result, designed around your facial features, your tooth proportions and your natural smile line.

In the practice’s words

A smile makeover is a customized plan that combines multiple cosmetic treatments to achieve your ideal appearance. Depending on your goals, this may include teeth whitening, porcelain veneers, dental crowns, and Invisalign to correct both the appearance and alignment of your teeth.

No two smiles are the same — and neither are our treatment plans. Dr. Dave Cho takes the time to carefully assess your current oral health, listen to your goals, and evaluate your unique facial features, tooth proportions, and natural smile line. Whether you’re aiming for subtle enhancements or a dramatic transformation, we design a customized plan tailored to your vision.

The five, and which problem each one solves

  • WhiteningIn-office for one visit, or custom take-home trays at your own pace. Lifts years of stain from coffee, wine, smoking and age.
  • Porcelain veneersUltra-thin ceramic shells bonded to the front of the teeth. Chips, cracks, stubborn discolouration, small gaps and minor misalignment, all at once.
  • BondingTooth-coloured resin sculpted onto small imperfections. Minimally invasive, one appointment, and the cheapest of the five.
  • CrownsWhere a tooth needs strength as well as appearance. A cosmetic result on a structural problem.
  • Invisalign®Crowding, gaps and minor bite issues, moved rather than covered. Often the right first step, because straightening changes what else is needed.

The Alternatives

Which of the five is yours.

The three that change how a tooth looks.

If the problem is then The treatment is usually
Colour, on otherwise sound teeth. Whitening — in-office or take-home.
A small chip, crack or uneven edge. Bonding. One visit, and nothing removed.
Several front teeth at once. Porcelain veneers.
A tooth that is weak as well as unsightly. A crown, which does both jobs.
Crowding, gaps, or the bite. Invisalign®, moving the teeth rather than covering them.

Most patients arrive wanting veneers and leave with a plan involving fewer, cheaper treatments. That is a good outcome, not a downgrade.

Why the order matters

A makeover done in the wrong sequence costs more and looks worse. Three rules decide it.

  • Health firstDecay, gum disease and a failing restoration are all treated before cosmetic work goes over them.
  • Position before colourStraightening changes which teeth show. Do it before deciding what needs covering.
  • Colour before matchingWhiten first. Resin and porcelain do not lighten later, so anything bonded or fitted is matched to the shade you have settled on.

Questions

Questions patients ask before they decide.

Will it look obviously done?

The stated goal is the opposite: a smile that looks naturally beautiful and feels authentically yours. The plan is built around your facial features, tooth proportions and natural smile line rather than to a standard shape.

Do I need all five treatments?

Almost certainly not. A makeover combines whichever treatments your goals actually require, and many patients need one or two. Arriving wanting veneers and leaving with a plan for whitening and bonding is a common and good outcome.

What is the difference between veneers and bonding?

Veneers are custom-crafted ceramic shells bonded to the front of the teeth, stain-resistant and long-lasting, suited to several teeth or a dramatic change. Bonding is resin sculpted directly onto the tooth in one visit, minimally invasive and far cheaper, suited to small imperfections.

Should I whiten before or after other work?

Before. Resin and porcelain do not lighten with whitening the way enamel does, so anything bonded or fitted is matched to the shade you have settled on. Mention your whitening plans at the consultation.

Can cosmetic work be done if I have decay or gum problems?

Those are treated first. Cosmetic work placed over active decay or gum disease fails, and the health assessment is part of the consultation for exactly this reason.

How long does a smile makeover take?

It depends entirely on the plan. Whitening or bonding can be a single appointment. A plan involving Invisalign followed by veneers runs for months, because the teeth have to be moved before anything is matched to them.
