The Treatment

What it is, and what it involves.

What is a composite filling?

Plastic resin blended with finely ground glass, in a shade matched to your own enamel. It bonds to the tooth rather than sitting inside it, which is why less of the tooth has to go.

In the practice’s words

Composite fillings are made from a blend of plastic resin and finely ground glass particles. This combination produces a material that closely matches the color and translucency of natural teeth. These tooth-colored fillings offer a discreet way to restore decayed, chipped, or broken teeth without leaving noticeable signs of dental work.

Unlike older silver or metal fillings, composite fillings bond directly to the tooth structure. This bonding process not only strengthens the tooth but also requires less removal of healthy enamel, helping to preserve as much of your natural tooth as possible.

The full detail of each stage

1. Comprehensive examination. Dr. William Sung will carefully examine the affected tooth or teeth, looking for signs of decay, cracks, or structural damage. To ensure an accurate diagnosis, we may take digital X-rays for a clearer, more detailed view of the internal structures. The goals at this stage are identifying all areas of concern, early detection of hidden cavities, and a customized plan tailored to your needs.

2. Gentle preparation. We gently administer local anesthesia to numb the area, then carefully remove the decayed or damaged parts of the tooth. Our technique is minimally invasive, preserving as much healthy tooth structure as possible for better long-term strength and support.

3. Layered filling placement. The composite material is placed in carefully controlled layers, each thin layer applied and then hardened using a dental curing light. Layering strengthens the filling and lets Dr. Sung shape the material with precision. It provides a strong bond, reduces the chance of gaps or leaks, and ensures an even distribution of material. The shade is selected to match your natural teeth.

4. Shaping and polishing. Dr. Sung sculpts and contours the filling to match the natural anatomy of your tooth so the bite feels comfortable, then polishes it to a glossy finish, which improves appearance and prevents staining and plaque buildup.

Making one last

Five to ten years is the average. Size, hygiene and grinding are what move it either way.

  • BrushTwice a day, with a fluoride toothpaste.
  • FlossDaily, to clear plaque between the teeth and at the gumline.
  • AvoidBiting ice, hard candy and popcorn kernels, especially on a fresh restoration.
  • LimitCoffee, tea and red wine, which stain resin over time.
  • ReturnFor checkups, so a filling is monitored before it fails.

Questions

Questions patients ask before they decide.

What are composite fillings made of?

Composite fillings are crafted from a combination of plastic resin and finely ground glass particles. This mixture produces a durable, tooth-colored material that bonds tightly to your natural tooth structure for a seamless repair.

Is getting a composite filling painful?

The process is generally painless. Local anesthesia is used to numb the area completely before treatment begins. Most patients experience little to no discomfort during or after the procedure.

Can I replace my old metal fillings with composite fillings?

Yes. Many patients choose to replace old silver amalgam fillings with tooth-colored fillings for a more natural appearance. Dr. William Sung can assess your current restorations and recommend safe replacement options if appropriate.

How do I care for my composite fillings after placement?

Brush and floss daily, avoid excessive biting on hard objects, and keep up regular dental checkups. Good oral hygiene protects both your natural teeth and your restorations.

Do composite fillings stain over time?

While composite fillings are resistant to stains, they can occasionally pick up discoloration from foods and beverages like coffee, tea, and red wine. Regular cleanings help minimize staining and keep your fillings looking good.

How long do they last?

On average, resin fillings last between 5 and 10 years. With excellent care and routine dental visits they can last longer. The size and location of the filling, your oral hygiene habits, and how much wear comes from chewing or grinding are what decide it. We monitor every restoration during your exams.
