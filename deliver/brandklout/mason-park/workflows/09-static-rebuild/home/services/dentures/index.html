The Treatment

What it is, and what it involves.

What are dentures?

Removable appliances that replace missing teeth and the tissue around them, made to the measurements of your own mouth. They restore what the teeth did, and what the face looked like with them.

In the practice’s words

Dentures are removable dental appliances used to replace missing teeth and surrounding tissue. They help restore oral functionality and the appearance of a full smile. Whether you need full dentures or partial dentures, our options offer a reliable, customized solution.

At Mason Park Dental of Katy, we believe everyone deserves to smile confidently. Whether you’ve lost teeth due to injury, decay, or age, dentures offer an effective solution to restore function, beauty, and self-esteem. Led by Dr. Dave Cho, we specialize in providing dentures in Katy, TX, crafted for exceptional comfort, fit, and esthetics.

Four kinds, and who each one is for

  • FullAll the teeth in the upper or lower jaw are gone. The denture rests on the gums.
  • PartialSome natural teeth remain. It fills the gaps and stops the rest from shifting.
  • ImmediateFitted the same day as the extractions, so you do not leave without teeth.
  • Implant‑supportedAnchored on implants. No slippage, and more to chew with.

Living With Them

The first weeks, and every week after.

Dentures, against implants.

Dentures vs Implants
Removable, for cleaning and overnight. Fixed in the jaw, permanently.
No surgery. Surgical placement, then healing.
The most cost-effective full-arch option. A larger investment up front.
An adjustment period for eating. Chewing close to natural teeth.
Five to ten years, then replaced. Designed to be permanent.

The two are not exclusive: implants can anchor a denture, which is the fourth type above.

Eating with them, the first month

Your mouth and the muscles in it need time to adapt. Four habits make that faster.

  • Start softMashed potato, well-cooked vegetables, yogurt, eggs, oatmeal. Nothing that needs force.
  • Small bitesCut food up, chew slowly, and bite with the side teeth rather than the front ones.
  • Both sidesChew evenly. A denture has no roots, so balance is what holds it still.
  • Stay hydratedDentures can reduce saliva. Water through the day, and less caffeine and alcohol.
Caring for them, and when to come back

Clean them daily. Use a soft-bristle toothbrush designed for dentures and a non-abrasive denture cleaner — regular toothpaste is too harsh. Rinse thoroughly after eating to remove debris and prevent stains.

Soak them overnight. Dentures need to stay moist to retain their shape. Lukewarm water or a denture-cleaning solution, never hot water, which warps them. If yours have metal components, ask which solution to use.

Handle them with care. Clean them over a soft towel or a basin of water, hold them gently, and never force them into place.

Keep up your own hygiene. Brush your gums, tongue and the roof of your mouth daily. With partial dentures, care for the natural teeth as advised.

Come back if the fit changes. Looseness or shifting while speaking or eating, gum irritation or sores, changes in facial appearance, or persistent discomfort all mean the denture needs adjusting or replacing.

Questions

Questions patients ask before they decide.

What are dentures?

Dentures are removable dental appliances designed to replace missing teeth and help restore oral functionality and esthetics.

How long do dentures last?

With proper care, dentures typically last between 5 and 10 years. Regular dental visits can extend their life.

Are dentures comfortable to wear?

Modern dentures are more comfortable than ever. With a precise denture fitting, most patients adapt quickly.

What is the cost of dentures in Katy, TX?

Prices vary by type — full, partial, or implant-supported — and by how much customisation is needed. We give you clear pricing during the consultation.

Can I sleep with my dentures in?

We generally recommend removing dentures overnight to allow your gums to rest and to properly clean the appliance.

How will I know I need new ones?

Even the best dentures need replacing eventually. Contact the practice if you notice looseness or shifting while speaking or eating, gum irritation or sores, changes in your facial appearance, or persistent discomfort.
