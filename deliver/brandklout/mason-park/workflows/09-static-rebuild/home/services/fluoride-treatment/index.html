The Treatment

What it is, and what it actually does.

What is a fluoride treatment?

A concentrated form of a natural mineral, applied directly to the teeth. Fluoride strengthens enamel — the protective outer layer — so it resists the acids and bacteria that cause decay.

In the practice’s words

Fluoride treatment involves applying a concentrated form of fluoride directly to your teeth. Fluoride is a natural mineral that strengthens tooth enamel — the protective outer layer of your teeth. When enamel is fortified, your teeth become more resistant to decay and erosion caused by acids and bacteria.

At Mason Park Dental, we provide professional fluoride applications during routine dental visits or whenever patients need an extra boost for enamel protection. Daily brushing and flossing are important, but sometimes your teeth need extra help.

Four things it does, in the practice’s own order

  • RemineralisationIt repairs the earliest stages of enamel damage by rebuilding weakened tooth surfaces. This is the one that surprises people: some early decay can be reversed.
  • Decay resistanceIt makes the enamel itself harder to dissolve in the acids that cause cavities.
  • Fewer bacteriaIt inhibits the ability of harmful bacteria to produce acid in the first place.
  • Protection for dental workIt shields fillings, crowns and veneers against future decay around their edges.

Questions

Questions patients ask before they decide.

Are fluoride treatments safe?

Yes. They are safe, fast and effective. Fluoride varnish in particular dries quickly and carries minimal ingestion risk, which is why it is the form used for young patients.

Can fluoride actually reverse a cavity?

It can reverse the earliest stage, before a hole has formed. Fluoride remineralises weakened enamel — it rebuilds the surface. Once decay has broken through into the tooth, it needs a filling instead.

How long does it take?

Minutes. It is brushed on, or held in a tray for a few minutes, usually at the end of a cleaning. You do not need a separate appointment.

Can I eat afterwards?

Not for at least 30 minutes, so the fluoride has time to work on the enamel. Stick to soft foods for the rest of the day if you notice any minor sensitivity.

I am an adult. Is this not a children's treatment?

No. Adults with fillings or crowns benefit from it protecting that work, patients with dry mouth benefit because reduced saliva means higher cavity risk, and seniors benefit because aging enamel thins.

How often should I have it?

It depends on your risk rather than on a schedule. Dr. Sung recommends a frequency based on your cavity history, your medications, and what the exam finds.
