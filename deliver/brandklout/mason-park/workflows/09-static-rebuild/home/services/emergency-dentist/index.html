Right now

What to do while you are on your way.

Call (281) 769-8878 first. Then find your situation below. With a knocked-out tooth, minutes matter.

Knocked outGo now

Hold it by the crown, never the root. Rinse gently if dirty — do not scrub. Put it in milk or your own saliva and come straight in.

◆

Toothache

Rinse with warm water. Floss gently to clear anything trapped between the teeth. Do not put aspirin on the gum — it burns the tissue.

◆

Broken or chipped

Save any pieces you can find. Rinse with warm water, and hold a cold compress against the outside to keep the swelling down.

◆

Lost filling or crown

Dental cement or a little toothpaste in the cavity will do temporarily. Bring the crown with you if you still have it.

And the seven signs that mean call today, not next week

  • PainSevere and constant, rather than coming and going.
  • SwellingIn the face or the jaw. This one can become serious quickly.
  • BleedingThat does not stop.
  • A knocked-out toothThe most time-critical thing on this list.
  • BreaksBroken teeth, or large cracks.
  • Loose restorationsA crown or filling that has come out or is moving.
  • InfectionPus, fever, or swollen glands. Do not wait on this one.

In the practice’s words

Dental emergencies don’t make appointments — they strike suddenly, leaving you in pain, discomfort, and uncertainty. Whether you’ve cracked a tooth, lost a filling, or have a severe toothache that won’t go away, our office is fully equipped to treat dental emergencies quickly and professionally.

Choosing the right emergency dentist can make all the difference between saving or losing a tooth. We offer same-day emergency appointments, walk-in visits, advanced diagnostic tools, immediate pain relief options, transparent pricing and insurance help, and compassionate personalized care.

The five emergencies seen most often here

  • Severe toothache or abscessSudden intense pain usually signals an underlying infection. Immediate relief first, then saving the tooth wherever that is still possible.
  • Broken, chipped or knocked-out teethTime is critical. The sooner you are in the chair, the better the chance of keeping the natural tooth.
  • Lost fillings or crownsThe tooth underneath is exposed and vulnerable. Same-day restoration to protect it.
  • Bleeding or swollen gumsFrom infection, trauma or periodontal disease. Addressed quickly to stop the bleeding.
  • Soft tissue injuriesTongue, cheeks or lips. These bleed a great deal and need proper management to heal without infection.

Reading your own pain: which kinds mean act fast

Dental pain is your body signalling that something isn’t right. Some types are urgent and some can wait, and knowing the difference matters.

  • Sharp, sudden pain. Stabbing pain that comes and goes suddenly could be a cracked tooth, an exposed nerve, or a deep cavity reaching the inner pulp. This requires immediate attention to avoid worsening damage.
  • Constant, severe pain. Usually an infection, and usually getting worse rather than better on its own.
  • Pain with swelling or fever. The combination is the one to take most seriously. Ignoring these symptoms can lead to more extensive damage, higher costs, and even life-threatening infections.

Where To Go

If you are not sure, call and describe it.

Everything in the list above is what this practice handles. Some situations belong in an emergency room instead — difficulty breathing or swallowing, a suspected broken jaw, bleeding that will not stop, or a head injury alongside the dental one. If any of those apply, seek emergency medical care rather than a dental appointment. If you are unsure which you are dealing with, phoning the practice on (281) 769-8878 costs a minute and the team will tell you.

Most emergencies were preventable

Not said to make anyone feel worse — said because the way out of the cycle is the boring appointment, not the urgent one.

  • Regular checkupsA cavity found at a routine visit never becomes a 2am abscess.
  • A mouth guardA sports guard is the single best protection against a knocked-out tooth.
  • A night guardGrinding cracks teeth slowly. A night guard stops that.
  • Do not chew iceOr pens, or anything hard. It is the commonest cause of a cracked molar.
  • Act on small thingsA twinge, a rough edge, a filling that feels odd. These are cheap now.

Questions

Questions patients ask before they decide.

Can I just walk in?

Yes, walk-in visits are welcome. Calling first is better if you can — the team can give you guidance over the phone and have things ready when you arrive.

My tooth was knocked out. What do I do?

Handle it by the crown, not the root. Rinse it gently if it is dirty but do not scrub it. Place it in milk or your own saliva and get to the practice immediately. Time is the single biggest factor in whether it can be saved.

The pain stopped. Do I still need to come in?

Yes. Pain from an infected tooth often stops when the nerve dies, which means the infection is still there and now has nothing warning you about it. Ignoring symptoms can lead to more extensive damage, higher costs, and even life-threatening infections.

Should I put aspirin on the sore tooth?

No. Placing aspirin directly on the gum causes chemical burns to the tissue. Rinse with warm water, floss gently to clear trapped food, and take pain relief the normal way.

Will you have to take the tooth out?

Only if it genuinely cannot be saved. An emergency root canal, a filling, or a crown repair are all more likely. Where an extraction is the right answer, it is performed carefully and sedation is available.

I am terrified of the dentist and now I am in pain.

Say that when you call. Sedation is available for emergency treatment, including for extractions, and the team is used to patients arriving frightened as well as in pain.
