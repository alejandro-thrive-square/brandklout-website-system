The Treatment

What it is, and what it involves.

What is a dental implant?

A biocompatible titanium post placed in the jawbone, which the bone grows onto over a few months — osseointegration. The visible tooth is a crown, a bridge or a denture fixed on top of it.

In the practice’s words

When it comes to tooth replacement, dental implants stand out as the gold standard for restoring both function and esthetics. Whether you’re replacing a single tooth, multiple teeth, or an entire arch, implants provide a permanent, reliable, and natural-looking solution.

The implant post is made of biocompatible titanium, which fuses with the jawbone through a process called osseointegration, making it incredibly stable and long-lasting. Studies show that dental implants have a success rate of over 95%, making them one of the most reliable dental treatments available.

Five things the practice does with implants

  • Single toothOne implant and a custom crown, for one gap.
  • Implant‑supported bridgeFor several missing teeth. Stronger support than a traditional bridge, and the neighbouring teeth are left alone.
  • All‑on‑4® and All‑on‑6®A full arch on a minimal number of implants. Faster recovery, immediate function, nothing removable.
  • Implant‑supported denturesStability and comfort a resting denture cannot give. No slippage and no irritation.
  • Bone grafting firstWhere the jawbone has already deteriorated, a graft builds the foundation the implant needs.

The Path

What happens, step by step.

01

Consultation and planning

Your oral health is evaluated and a personalised implant plan is built around it.

02

Implant placement

The titanium post is placed surgically into the jawbone, under anaesthetic or sedation.

03

Healing and osseointegration

Over a few months the bone grows onto the post. This is the part that cannot be hurried.

04

Abutment placement

A small connector is attached to the implant, ready to carry the restoration.

05

The final restoration

A custom crown, bridge or denture is placed on top, matched to the teeth around it.

The Alternatives

How it compares.

An implant, against a denture.

Implant vs Denture
Anchored in the jawbone. Rests on the gums.
No adhesives, ever. Adhesives, soaking and adjustments.
Nearly full bite strength. Some foods stay difficult.
Stimulates the bone, so it is kept. The bone underneath continues to shrink.
Surgery, and months of healing. No surgery, and a faster result.

The two combine: an implant-supported denture is the fourth service above.

Keeping one, once it is in

An implant cannot decay. The gum around it can, and peri-implantitis is what fails implants.

  • Clean it dailySoft brush and low-abrasive paste around the crown and gumline; floss, a water flosser or implant floss below it.
  • RinseA non-alcoholic antibacterial mouthwash. With a full arch, interdental brushes or super floss.
  • Every six monthsCheckups, professional cleaning with instruments that will not damage the restoration, and x‑rays of the bone.
  • Nothing hard or stickyIce, hard candy, caramel and toffee. Chew on both sides, and cut hard food up.
  • Guard itA night guard if you grind, a mouth guard for sport, and never your teeth as tools.
Smoking, alcohol and why they matter more here

Smoking and excessive alcohol consumption can dramatically increase the risk of dental implant failure. The chemicals in tobacco products interfere with the body’s natural healing process, making it harder for the implant post to properly fuse with the jawbone.

If you smoke, it’s recommended to quit at least two weeks before your implant procedure to improve healing, and to avoid smoking during recovery — nicotine restricts blood flow to the gums, increasing the risk of infection, gum recession, and implant failure. Alcohol slows down the body’s ability to heal and may irritate the gums. Studies show that dental implant failure rates are significantly higher in smokers compared to non-smokers.

Questions

Questions patients ask before they decide.

How long do dental implants last?

With proper care, dental implants can last a lifetime. The implant post is made of titanium, which integrates with the jawbone, making it a durable, long-term solution.

Is getting dental implants painful?

No. The procedure is performed under local anesthesia or sedation for maximum comfort. Most patients experience minimal discomfort afterward.

How much do dental implants cost in Katy, TX?

The cost varies based on the number of implants, the complexity of the case, and insurance coverage. We offer flexible financing options.

Can I get dental implants if I have bone loss?

Yes. If you have bone loss, we may recommend bone grafting to create a stable foundation for the implant. At the consultation Dr. William Sung will evaluate your jawbone health and discuss the options.

Are dental implants covered by insurance?

Some insurance plans cover a portion of implant treatment. Our team will help you navigate your benefits and explore financing options.

What is the difference between placement and restoration?

Placement is the surgical half — putting the titanium post into the jawbone, which has its own page. Restoration is what goes on top of it once the bone has fused: the abutment and then the crown, bridge or denture. Both happen here.
