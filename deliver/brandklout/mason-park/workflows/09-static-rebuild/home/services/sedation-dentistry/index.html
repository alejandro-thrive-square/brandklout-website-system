The Treatment

Three levels, and how to tell which is yours.

What is sedation dentistry?

Carefully administered medication that lets you relax through treatment. Not being knocked out — in most cases you stay awake, and simply do not mind.

In the practice’s words

Sedation dentistry uses carefully administered medications to help patients relax during dental treatment. From mild sedation to deeper relaxation, sedation dental care makes even the most complex procedures feel effortless.

Each method is safe, effective, and tailored to your individual comfort and needs. Dr. Dave Cho, DMD, holds a sedation certification, your vital signs are continuously monitored, and sedation levels are adjusted as needed.

The three, lightest first

  • Nitrous oxideLaughing gas, inhaled through a small mask over the nose. Euphoric and relaxed within minutes, and it wears off quickly — you can usually drive yourself home.
  • Oral sedationA prescribed pill about an hour before. You stay awake but deeply relaxed, and may not remember much. For moderate anxiety, a sensitive gag reflex, or longer appointments.
  • IV sedationDelivered into the bloodstream, so it works immediately and the level can be controlled through the appointment. For severe anxiety, complex surgery, or long visits with extensive work.

The Path

What happens, step by step.

01

Consultation

Your medical history, your current medications, and how bad the anxiety actually is. Then a recommendation: gas, pill or IV.

02

Preparation

For oral or IV sedation you may be asked to fast for a few hours, and you will need someone to drive you home. Arrange that in advance.

03

Sedation and treatment

The sedation is administered, the procedure begins, and you are monitored throughout.

04

Recovery

Nitrous oxide wears off in minutes. Oral and IV sedation take longer, and you go home with someone else driving.

The Alternatives

How the three compare.

Nitrous oxide, against IV sedation.

Inhaled through a small nose mask. Into a vein, so it acts immediately.
Light and adjustable. The deepest level offered here.
Wears off within minutes. A longer recovery afterwards.

Oral sedation sits between the two: a pill an hour before, awake but deeply relaxed, and little memory of it afterwards.

What it is used alongside

Sedation is not a treatment on its own. It is how another treatment becomes possible for you.

  • ExtractionsEspecially surgical ones, and wisdom teeth.
  • Implant placementAnd bone grafting, where the surgery is longer.
  • Root canal therapyThe treatment with the worst reputation and the least deserved one.
  • Even a cleaningIf a routine visit is what you have been avoiding, sedation is a legitimate way back in.

Questions

Questions patients ask before they decide.

Is sedation dentistry safe for nervous patients?

Yes. When administered by trained professionals it is extremely safe, and the team monitors your vital signs continuously, adjusting the level as needed. Before anything is scheduled there is a thorough pre-sedation consultation, your medical history and medications are reviewed, only the safest methods and doses are used, and emergency equipment is available on site.

Will I be unconscious?

Not with nitrous oxide or oral sedation — you stay awake, just deeply relaxed. Oral sedation often leaves little memory of the procedure. IV sedation is deeper, and its level is controlled through the appointment.

Can I drive myself home?

After nitrous oxide, usually yes — the effects wear off quickly. After oral or IV sedation, no. Arrange transportation before the appointment.

Do I need to fast beforehand?

For oral sedation or IV sedation you may be asked to fast for a few hours. You will be told exactly what is needed at the consultation.

I never get properly numb. Does that change anything?

It is one of the recognised reasons for sedation. Some patients genuinely have difficulty getting numb with local anaesthetic, and sedation addresses the experience rather than fighting the anaesthetic.

Can I have several treatments done at once?

Yes, and that is often the point. Sedation allows multiple treatments to be performed in fewer appointments, which suits patients facing extensive work.
