Is this you

Is this the treatment you need?

Most patients arrive here having been told an implant is not possible yet. This is what makes it possible.

01

A tooth has been missing a while

The bone that held it stops being stimulated and begins to shrink.

02

Periodontal disease

Advanced gum disease takes bone as well as gum.

03

Trauma or infection

An injury or an abscess that reached the bone before it was treated.

04

An extraction, just now

Bone deteriorates fast in an empty socket, which is why a graft can go in on the same day.

What the graft is actually for

  • SupportDental implants, which need bone to fuse to.
  • PreserveThe integrity of the jawbone that is still there.
  • PreventFurther tooth loss as the ridge collapses.
  • ImproveFacial structure and oral health.

The Treatment

What it is, and what it involves.

What is a bone graft?

Bone material — your own, a donor’s, animal-derived or synthetic — placed where the jaw has thinned, and held under a membrane. Over months your body replaces it with your own bone.

In the practice’s words

Bone grafting is a surgical procedure where natural or synthetic bone material is placed in the jaw to rebuild areas with insufficient bone. Over time, the graft integrates with your existing bone, creating a strong foundation for dental restorations like implants or bridges.

Stimulation: the graft material encourages new bone growth around the placement site. Osteoconduction: the graft acts as a scaffold for bone-forming cells to grow upon. Osteoinduction: certain grafts release proteins that stimulate your body’s own bone growth. Integration: over time, your natural bone replaces the graft material, creating solid, healthy bone tissue.

Five procedures, and when each one is used

  • Socket preservationA graft placed straight into the socket after an extraction, before the bone can go.
  • Sinus liftFor the upper jaw, where the sinus cavity leaves the bone too shallow. The membrane is raised and material added beneath it.
  • Ridge augmentationWhere severe loss has collapsed the jaw ridge. It rebuilds the natural contour.
  • AutogenousYour own bone, usually from the chin or the back of the jaw. The best compatibility and healing there is.
  • Allograft and xenograftDonor or animal-derived material, for large areas or when you would rather not use your own bone.

Why This One

What it does for you.

A graft is rarely the treatment anyone came in for. It is what makes the treatment they came in for possible.

The implant succeeds

A higher success rate, because there is something for the post to fuse to.

Facial symmetry returns

A collapsed ridge shows in the face. Rebuilding it puts the volume back.

Bite and chewing improve

Once the restoration on top of it is in and stable.

The teeth around it are supported

Bone loss is not confined to the gap. Its neighbours are standing in the same jaw.

Further deterioration stops

The graft breaks the cycle rather than pausing it.

A full arch becomes possible

Full-mouth restoration usually needs this before the implants or dentures can go in.

Four things people believe about bone grafts that are not true
  • “It is extremely painful.” Modern techniques, sedation options, and pain management medications make the procedure surprisingly comfortable.
  • “Recovery takes forever.” Most patients return to their normal activities within a few days. Full integration takes a few months, but day-to-day recovery is quick.
  • “Grafts always fail.” With experienced care, bone grafts have high success rates and significantly improve implant outcomes.
  • “Only older patients need them.” Bone loss can happen at any age through injury, disease, or extraction. This is suitable for adults of many ages.

The Path

What happens, step by step.

01

Consultation

Gums, teeth and jawbone assessed on digital x‑rays and 3D scans, so the loss is measured rather than estimated.

02

Treatment planning

Which graft material, whether you want sedation, and how long integration will take in your case.

03

Surgery day

The site is numbed, the material placed and secured under a membrane. Most procedures take less than 90 minutes.

04

Healing and integration

Several months while your own bone grows around the graft and takes it over. Normal activity resumes within days.

Questions

Questions patients ask before they decide.

Is bone grafting painful?

Modern techniques, sedation options and pain management medications make it surprisingly comfortable. The site is numbed with a local anaesthetic, and sedation is available if you would rather be sedated.

How long does recovery take?

Most patients return to normal activities within a few days. Full integration — your own bone growing around and replacing the graft — takes several months, but day-to-day recovery is quick.

Do bone grafts fail?

With experienced care, bone grafts have high success rates and significantly improve implant outcomes. Smoking is the single biggest thing that puts one at risk, which is why we ask you to stop before and after.

Am I too young for this?

No. Bone loss can happen at any age through injury, disease, or tooth extraction, and bone grafting is suitable for adults of many ages.

Where does the bone come from?

Four options. Autogenous grafts use your own bone, typically from the chin or the back of the jaw, and give the best compatibility and healing. Allografts use donor bone, xenografts are animal-derived, and synthetic grafts use manufactured material. Dr. Sung will recommend one and explain why.

How long is the procedure itself?

Most bone grafting procedures take less than 90 minutes. Arrange transport home if you are having sedation.
