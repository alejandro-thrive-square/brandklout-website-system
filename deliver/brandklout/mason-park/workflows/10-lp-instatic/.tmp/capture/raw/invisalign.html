Everything Patients Ask Before Booking

Am I a candidate for Invisalign?
+

Most crowding, spacing and mild-to-moderate bite issues can be treated with clear aligners. Severe rotations, significant jaw discrepancies and some bite corrections still do better with braces or a specialist referral. A scan and exam tell you definitively, and if you are not a candidate, you will be told at the consultation rather than twelve trays in.

How long will Invisalign take?
+

Shorter cases often finish in a few months; comprehensive cases typically run twelve to eighteen. Your digital plan shows the projected number of stages before you start, so you know the timeline before you commit to it.

Will my smile look natural?
+

That is the goal. Your plan is designed around your bite, your facial proportions and how your teeth show when you speak and smile, so the finished alignment looks like it belongs to you rather than a generic set of teeth.

Can I preview the result before treatment?
+

Yes. Your digital plan shows the projected movement stage by stage, so you see the intended result and the number of trays it will take before your first aligner goes in.

Does Invisalign hurt?
+

Expect pressure and tenderness for a day or two after each new tray; that pressure is the movement happening. Most patients describe it as noticeable rather than painful, and it settles as the tray beds in.

What does Invisalign cost at Mason Park Dental?
+

$1,000 off brings all-inclusive Invisalign® treatment from the regular $4,800 fee to $3,800, with a further $500 off when you prepay in full out of pocket. In-office and third-party payment plans are available, and insurance payments are reimbursed back to you. Your exact plan is confirmed at your consultation.

Does insurance cover Invisalign?
+

Many plans include an orthodontic benefit that applies to clear aligners, often as a lifetime maximum rather than an annual one. Our team will check your benefits for you. If you do not have insurance, ask about the Mason Park Dental Savings Plan: no annual maximum, no deductible, and no waiting period.
