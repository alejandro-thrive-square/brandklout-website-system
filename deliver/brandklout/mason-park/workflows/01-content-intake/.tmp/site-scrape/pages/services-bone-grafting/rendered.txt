What Is Bone Grafting?

Bone grafting is a surgical procedure where natural or synthetic bone material is placed in the jaw to rebuild areas with insufficient bone. Over time, the graft integrates with your existing bone, creating a strong foundation for dental restorations like implants or bridges.

We perform bone grafting for dental implants and other restorative purposes using several different grafting options:

Autogenous grafts (from your own bone)
Allografts (donor bone)
Xenografts (animal-derived)
Synthetic grafts

Our Bone Grafting Procedures
1. Socket Preservation

After tooth extraction, bone in that area may begin to deteriorate quickly. We place a bone graft into the socket to preserve the structure and prevent bone loss, making it easier to place implants later.

2. Sinus Lift Surgery

When placing implants in the upper jaw, the bone may be too shallow due to the sinus cavity. A sinus lift raises the sinus membrane and allows us to add grafting material to create sufficient depth for implant placement.

3. Ridge Augmentation

Severe bone loss can cause the jaw ridge to collapse. Ridge augmentation rebuilds the natural contour of the jaw, allowing for better dental implant placement and improved facial esthetics.

4. Autogenous Bone Grafting

We may use bone from your own body—typically from the chin or back of the jaw—to provide the best compatibility and healing for jawbone restoration.

5. Allograft and Xenograft Options

Donor or animal-derived graft materials are often used when large areas need rebuilding or when the patient prefers not to use their own bone.

What to Expect During the Procedure
Step 1: Consultation

You’ll meet with Dr. William Sung for a comprehensive evaluation. We’ll assess the health of your gums, teeth, and jawbone using advanced imaging tools such as digital X-rays and 3D scans.

Step 2: Treatment Planning

Based on your specific needs, we’ll design a customized bone grafting treatment plan. This may include recommendations for the type of grafting material, sedation options, and integration timelines.

Step 3: Surgery Day

The area is numbed with a local anesthetic. We then place the bone grafting material into the site and secure it using a membrane or barrier. Most procedures take less than 90 minutes.

Step 4: Healing & Recovery

The healing process, called osseointegration, can take several months. During this time, your natural bone grows around the graft, solidifying it as part of your jaw structure.

The Science Behind Bone Regeneration

Bone grafting harnesses your body’s natural healing powers. Here’s how the process works:

Stimulation: The graft material encourages new bone growth around the placement site.

Osteoconduction: The graft acts as a scaffold for bone-forming cells to grow upon.

Osteoinduction: Certain grafts release proteins that stimulate your body’s own bone growth.

Integration: Over time, your natural bone replaces the graft material, creating solid, healthy bone tissue.

By combining surgical skill with biological science, Mason Park Dental of Katy ensures every patient has the best chance at a strong, lasting result.

Understanding Bone Loss After Tooth Loss

When a tooth is lost, the jawbone beneath it no longer receives stimulation from chewing forces. Without that stimulation:

The bone begins to resorb (shrink) over time

The jaw can lose up to 25% of its width within the first year

This leads to changes in facial appearance, bite alignment, and oral health

Bone grafting proactively addresses these issues, restoring lost bone volume and allowing for stable, long-lasting dental restorations.

Different Materials Used in Bone Grafting

At Mason Park Dental of Katy, we carefully select the ideal material based on your individual needs. Different types of bone grafting materials offer various advantages:

Autograft: Your own bone harvested from another area (like the chin or hip). Provides the highest success rate due to complete biocompatibility.

Allograft: Processed bone from a human donor, often used when autograft harvesting is not ideal.

Xenograft: Bone material derived from animal sources, such as bovine bone, treated to ensure safety and compatibility.

Alloplast (Synthetic Grafts): Lab-made materials designed to promote bone growth without donor material.

Every material option is designed to encourage your body’s natural ability to regenerate bone, ensuring success with bone grafting in Katy, TX.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

1. What is bone grafting?
Bone grafting is a procedure that replaces or adds bone to the jaw to support future dental restorations like implants.

2. Is bone grafting painful?
The procedure is performed under local anesthesia or sedation to keep you comfortable. Mild swelling or soreness afterward is normal and manageable with medication.

3. How long does bone grafting take to heal?
Healing typically takes between 3 to 6 months, depending on the size and location of the graft and your overall health.

4. What are the types of bone grafts used?
Options include autografts (your own bone), allografts (human donor), xenografts (animal), or synthetic materials. We’ll help choose the right type based on your needs.

5. Can I get implants immediately after bone grafting?
In some cases, yes. However, many patients will need to wait several months until the graft has fully integrated before receiving implants.
