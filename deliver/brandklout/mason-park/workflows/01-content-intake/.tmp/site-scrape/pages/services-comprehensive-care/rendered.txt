What Is a Dental Deep Cleaning?

A deep cleaning, also known as scaling and root planing, is a specialized treatment that removes plaque, tartar, and bacteria from below the gumline. While a regular cleaning targets the surfaces of your teeth, a periodontal cleaning goes much deeper to stop gum disease in its tracks.

Dr. William Sung will carefully evaluate your gums during your consultation and recommend a deep dental cleaning in Katy, TX if you show signs of early gum disease (gingivitis) or more advanced periodontal issues.

Steps in a Deep Cleaning Procedure

Here’s what you can expect during your deep cleaning at Mason Park Dental of Katy:

Examination: Dr. William Sung will examine your gums and measure pocket depths.
Scaling: Special instruments are used to remove plaque and tartar beneath the gumline.
Root Planning: The roots of your teeth are smoothed to discourage future bacterial buildup.

Follow-up Visit: We monitor healing and recommend a maintenance plan.

Scaling and root planning are highly effective in preventing the progression of gum disease and preserving your natural teeth.

Deep Cleaning vs. Regular Cleaning: What’s the Difference?
Regular Cleaning: Focuses on the tooth surfaces above the gumline; performed during routine checkups.
Deep Cleaning (Scaling and Root Planing): Targets plaque and tartar beneath the gumline; necessary for patients with gum disease.

The Hidden Dangers of Untreated Gum Disease

Gum disease doesn’t just threaten your mouth — it can impact your entire body.

Medical research links periodontal disease to a wide range of serious health conditions, including:

Heart disease: Bacteria from infected gums can contribute to arterial plaque buildup.

Stroke: Inflammation caused by gum disease may increase stroke risk.

Diabetes complications: Infections worsen blood sugar control.

Respiratory illnesses: Oral bacteria can be aspirated into the lungs, leading to infections.

Alzheimer’s disease: Studies suggest gum disease bacteria may be found in the brain tissues of Alzheimer’s patients.

Choosing a deep cleaning now can support a healthier future not just for your mouth, but for your whole body.
