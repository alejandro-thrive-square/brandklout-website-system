Bruxism and Restorative Challenges

Bruxism, or chronic teeth grinding, can wear down enamel, fracture dental work, and lead to a host of oral health problems. Patients with a history of bruxism often need restorative dentistry not just to fix damage, but also to protect their teeth going forward. We approach these cases by restoring worn teeth with durable materials that can withstand pressure while also addressing the underlying causes of grinding. Custom occlusal guards may be recommended to prevent future wear. These restorations help realign the bite and relieve symptoms like jaw pain, headaches, and tooth sensitivity. By treating the damage and preventing recurrence, we help patients preserve their investment in their dental health.

Restorative Dentistry vs. Cosmetic Dentistry: What’s the Difference?

Though they sometimes overlap, restorative dentistry focuses on function, while cosmetic dentistry emphasizes appearance. For example:

A dental crown can restore bite strength (restorative) and improve appearance (cosmetic).

A filling restores tooth structure (restorative) but can be tooth-colored (cosmetic).

We often combine both to deliver results that are beautiful and healthy.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

1. What is restorative dentistry?
Restorative dentistry involves treatments that repair or replace teeth to restore your smile’s function and appearance, such as crowns, bridges, and fillings.

2. Is restorative dentistry covered by insurance?
Most PPO dental insurance plans cover a portion of restorative dental care, especially medically necessary procedures. We’ll help verify your benefits.

3. How long do dental crowns and bridges last?
With good hygiene, crowns and bridges can last 10–15 years or more. We’ll provide tips to maintain your restorations long-term.

4. Are tooth-colored fillings as durable as metal ones?
Yes! Today’s composite fillings are strong, durable, and safer than metal—making them ideal for both front and back teeth.

5. Can I combine cosmetic and restorative treatments?
Absolutely. We often blend cosmetic and restorative dentistry to improve both function and esthetics, giving you the best of both worlds.

6. What Is Root Canal Therapy and When Is It Needed?
Root canal therapy comes into play when a cavity, crack, or trauma allows bacteria to reach the inner pulp of your tooth, causing infection, pain, or sensitivity. If left untreated, this infection can jeopardize both your comfort and the health of your entire mouth.

During a root canal, Dr. Dave Cho carefully removes the damaged pulp (along with the tiny nerves and blood vessels inside your tooth), then meticulously cleans and disinfects the area. Once the canals are spotless, we fill them with a biocompatible material to seal things up tight and finish with a durable filling to keep bacteria out.

Many times, we’ll also recommend a crown after your root canal. This custom-crafted cap adds extra protection and helps restore your tooth’s natural strength—so you can smile, chew, and talk with confidence again.

7. What is the typical recovery time after a restorative dentistry procedure?
Recovery time varies depending on the treatment type and how many teeth are involved. Most patients who receive small fillings or a single crown can return to their daily activities right away, often with little to no discomfort. More involved procedures—like dental implants or multiple extractions—may require a day or two of rest, plus some temporary dietary tweaks. Our team always provides detailed aftercare instructions and informs you of what to expect, ensuring your healing is as smooth and comfortable as possible.

8. What are the risks associated with restorative dentistry?
Restorative dental treatments are generally low-risk and designed with your comfort and health in mind. That said, as with any dental procedure, a few potential risks may occur:

Sensitivity or Mild Discomfort: It’s common to experience a bit of sensitivity or soreness around treated teeth, especially right after your procedure. This usually fades within a few days as your mouth adjusts to the new shape.
Chipping or Loosening: Crowns, bridges, or fillings can occasionally chip or come loose over time—especially if exposed to very hard foods or teeth grinding. Regular checkups help catch and address these issues early.
Rare Complications: Infections or allergic reactions to dental materials are extremely rare, especially with today’s advanced, biocompatible restorations like zirconia or porcelain.
Delayed Treatment Risks: If cavities or tooth damage aren’t treated promptly, more extensive procedures (such as root canals or extractions) may eventually become necessary.

Our team at Mason Park Dental uses only top-rated materials and proven techniques to minimize risks and keep your smile healthy. If you notice unusual pain, swelling, or changes after a procedure, let us know right away so we can help.

9. What are inlays and onlays, and when are they recommended?
If you have a tooth with damage that’s more extensive than what a regular filling can repair—but not quite severe enough to need a full crown—inlays and onlays are often the ideal solution. Think of them as custom-crafted puzzle pieces designed just for your tooth. Inlays fit snugly within the grooves of your tooth, while onlays extend to cover one or more of the tooth’s cusps for extra reinforcement.

We recommend inlays and onlays when you need to restore strength and function to your tooth without removing as much healthy structure as a crown would require. Our digital technology ensures a precise fit, and once bonded in place, these restorations blend seamlessly with your natural enamel for a durable, nearly invisible result.
