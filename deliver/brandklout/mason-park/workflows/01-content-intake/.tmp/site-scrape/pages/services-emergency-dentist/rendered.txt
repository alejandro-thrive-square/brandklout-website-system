Steps to Take During a Dental Emergency

While you wait to see our emergency dentist in Katy, TX, here’s what you can do to minimize pain and protect your oral health:

For Toothaches:
Rinse your mouth with warm water.
Gently use dental floss to remove any food caught between teeth.
Avoid placing aspirin directly on the gum—it can cause burns.
For Knocked-Out Teeth:
Handle the tooth by the crown, not the root.
Rinse it gently if dirty, but do not scrub.
Place the tooth in milk or saliva and get to the dentist immediately.
For Broken or Chipped Teeth:
Save any pieces you can find.
Rinse your mouth with warm water.
Apply a cold compress to reduce swelling.
For Lost Fillings or Crowns:
Try placing dental cement or toothpaste in the cavity temporarily.
Bring the crown with you to your appointment if possible.

Understanding Different Types of Dental Pain: When to Act Fast

Dental pain is your body’s way of signaling that something isn’t right. Some types of pain are urgent, while others can wait. At Mason Park Dental of Katy, Dr. William Sung stresses the importance of understanding the warning signs.

Sharp, Sudden Pain

If you experience stabbing pain that comes and goes suddenly, it could be a cracked tooth, exposed nerve, or a deep cavity reaching the inner pulp. This type of pain requires immediate attention to avoid worsening damage.

Constant, Throbbing Pain

A relentless, pulsating pain typically signals an infection or abscess beneath the tooth or gums. This is a true dental emergency that can escalate into a more serious health threat if left untreated.

Pain When Biting or Chewing

Pain that intensifies when applying pressure could indicate a fractured tooth, loose filling, or nerve involvement. Our urgent dental care in Katy can diagnose and treat the underlying cause to restore your bite.

Lingering Sensitivity to Temperature

If sipping a hot coffee or eating ice cream causes pain that lingers for more than 30 seconds, you may have advanced decay or a compromised nerve requiring prompt treatment.

Remember:

When it comes to dental pain, it’s always better to err on the side of caution. If you feel something is wrong, trust your instincts—and contact our emergency dentist in Katy, TX.

FAQ’s 

Q1. What qualifies as a dental emergency?
A true dental emergency involves severe pain, bleeding, swelling, broken teeth, or infection. If in doubt, call our office for guidance.

Q2. What if I knock out a permanent tooth?
Try to place the tooth back in its socket or keep it moist in milk. See our emergency dentist within 30 minutes for the best chance of saving it.

Q3. Will insurance cover my emergency dental visit?

Q4. Can I walk in without an appointment for a dental emergency?

Q5. How can I prevent dental emergencies?
