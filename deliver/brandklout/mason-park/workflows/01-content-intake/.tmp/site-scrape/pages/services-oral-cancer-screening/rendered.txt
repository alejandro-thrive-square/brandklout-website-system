The Power of Early Detection

When oral cancer is caught early, it’s much more manageable. The treatment is less invasive, the recovery is quicker, and the survival rate is significantly higher. According to the Oral Cancer Foundation, the five-year survival rate for oral cancer jumps to nearly 80–90% when detected early, compared to just 30–40% in late-stage diagnoses.

Early detection gives patients a fighting chance before cancer spreads to surrounding tissues or other parts of the body. It allows us to take swift, targeted action, which could mean the difference between a simple outpatient procedure and more aggressive treatments like surgery or radiation.

A Silent Threat in Common Areas

Unlike some cancers, oral cancer can appear in multiple areas, including:

The lips

The inside of the cheeks

The gums and jawline

The tongue and the floor of the mouth

The roof of the mouth (soft and hard palate)

The throat and tonsillar area


Because these areas are often hidden or ignored, signs of cancer can go unnoticed without a professional evaluation. Symptoms such as persistent sores, a lump in the throat, or voice changes may be dismissed as minor irritations — but they could be early warning signs of something more serious.

What to Expect During an Oral Cancer Screening

At Mason Park Dental, an oral cancer screening is a quick, painless, and potentially life-saving part of your routine dental visit. Dr. William Sung and our experienced team are trained to identify early signs of cancer or precancerous conditions in the mouth, which can make a significant difference in treatment outcomes. Here’s a detailed look at what you can expect during your screening:

1. Visual Examination

The first step involves a thorough visual inspection of your oral cavity. Using a bright overhead light and a dental mirror, we carefully examine:

Your lips and the corners of your mouth

The inner lining of your cheeks

The front and sides of your tongue

The hard and soft palate (roof of your mouth)

The floor of your mouth (under the tongue)

The back of your throat and tonsillar area


We’re looking for any unusual changes such as white or red patches, persistent sores, swelling, or discoloration. Even subtle irregularities can be signs of early-stage oral cancer or a precancerous lesion.

2. Physical Palpation

Next, we gently feel around the mouth and jaw area to check for any lumps, nodules, or areas of tissue thickening. This includes:

Gently pressing on the floor of your mouth and the underside of your tongue

Palpating your neck, under your jawline, and around your lymph nodes

Checking the sides of the throat and other soft tissues for firmness or asymmetry


This tactile check helps identify abnormal textures or swelling that may not be visible to the naked eye but could indicate underlying concerns.

3. Use of Advanced Screening Tools (If Needed)

In some cases, Dr. Sung may use enhanced oral cancer screening technology to get a better view of abnormal cells. These tools may include:

Velscope or similar light-based devices that use fluorescence technology to reveal changes in tissue structure

Tissue staining (Toluidine Blue dye) that adheres to areas of higher cellular activity, potentially flagging abnormal areas

Brush biopsies to collect cells for further lab evaluation if something suspicious is found


These tools help increase the accuracy of our screenings and ensure that nothing goes unnoticed.

4. Fast, Comfortable & Non-Invasive

Most importantly, your best oral cancer screening near me is completely non-invasive and takes just a few minutes. There are no needles, no pain, and no downtime. Many patients are surprised by how simple the process is — it’s integrated seamlessly into your regular dental checkup.

Who Should Get Screened?

Every adult can benefit from regular oral cancer screenings, regardless of age or current health status. Early detection is key to successful treatment, and screenings are quick, painless, and non-invasive. While screenings are beneficial for all, some individuals are at a significantly higher risk and should prioritize annual exams with a trusted provider like Dr. William Sung.

Tobacco Users
Whether it’s cigarettes, cigars, pipe smoking, chewing tobacco, or even vaping, the use of tobacco products dramatically increases the risk of developing oral cancer. These substances introduce harmful chemicals into the body that can damage the cells of the mouth, throat, and tongue. Long-term tobacco users are especially vulnerable and should be screened more frequently to catch early signs before they progress.

Alcohol Consumers
Heavy or chronic alcohol consumption is another leading risk factor for oral cancer. Alcohol can irritate the mucous membranes in the mouth, making them more susceptible to cellular damage and carcinogens. When alcohol use is combined with tobacco use, the risk increases exponentially. If you consume alcohol regularly, scheduling a screening is an important step in proactive healthcare.

HPV Exposure
The Human Papillomavirus (HPV), particularly HPV-16, is a known contributor to oropharyngeal cancers. This virus can be transmitted through oral contact and often goes undetected for years. Even individuals without traditional risk factors like smoking or drinking may still be vulnerable due to HPV exposure. Regular screenings help identify HPV-related abnormalities early—before they become more serious.

Family History of Cancer
If cancer runs in your family, your genetic makeup may place you at a higher risk, even in the absence of lifestyle-related triggers. A family history of oral cancer, or any type of cancer, makes preventive screenings essential. Understanding your risk and taking a proactive stance could lead to earlier detection and more successful treatment outcomes.

Others at Risk

Individuals with a history of frequent sun exposure (linked to lip cancer)

People over the age of 40

Those with poor nutrition or compromised immune systems

Individuals with a history of cancer elsewhere in the body
