Do You Need a Mouth Guard?

Not sure whether a mouth guard is something you really need? You’re not alone. Many patients are unaware they even require one—until symptoms begin to interfere with daily life or cause lasting dental damage. At Mason Park Dental, we believe prevention is the best treatment, and a custom-fit mouth guard is one of the most effective ways to protect your smile.

You may benefit from a custom mouth guard in Katy, TX if you:

Play Contact or High-Risk Sports

Athletes—especially those involved in football, hockey, basketball, martial arts, or wrestling—face a high risk of oral injury. One sudden impact can chip a tooth, cause jaw trauma, or result in soft tissue injuries like lip or gum lacerations. A sports mouth guard absorbs that impact and distributes the force evenly, significantly reducing the chances of injury.

Even for non-contact sports like skateboarding, cycling, or gymnastics, accidents can happen. Wearing a mouth guard helps you stay protected without compromising performance or breathability.

Wake Up With Headaches or Jaw Pain

Frequent morning headaches or sore jaw muscles can be early signs of nighttime grinding or clenching, also known as bruxism. Over time, this condition wears down enamel, creates tiny fractures in the teeth, and can lead to serious discomfort in the temporomandibular joint (TMJ).

A night guard serves as a cushion, preventing the upper and lower teeth from contacting and protecting your jaw from excessive pressure during sleep.

Grind or Clench Your Teeth at Night

You may not even know you do it—but the evidence builds up over time. Flattened teeth, receding gums, loose fillings, and unexplained tooth sensitivity may all point to grinding. Left unaddressed, bruxism can shorten your teeth, stress your jaw muscles, and lead to costly restorative work.

A custom night guard from Dr. William Sung is specifically designed to prevent this damage and help you sleep more comfortably, waking up without soreness or tension.

Want to Protect Crowns, Bridges, Veneers, or Implants

If you’ve invested in dental restorations such as crowns, veneers, implants, or bridges, protecting that investment is critical. One hard hit or a few months of grinding can chip or loosen these restorations—requiring repairs or replacements that could have been avoided.

A mouth guard offers a physical barrier between your teeth and potential harm, acting like an insurance policy for your dental work.

Have Been Diagnosed With TMJ Disorder or Bruxism

TMJ dysfunction affects the joint that connects your jawbone to your skull. Symptoms can include jaw stiffness, clicking or popping when chewing, and difficulty opening or closing your mouth fully. Bruxism, often a root cause or contributing factor, worsens this condition over time.

A professionally made TMJ mouth guard helps reposition your jaw slightly while you sleep, relieving strain on the joint and minimizing the discomfort and inflammation that follow.

Caring for Your Custom Mouth Guard

Proper care helps your guard last longer and remain hygienic. Here are our best practices:

Rinse with cool water after each use

Clean using a soft toothbrush and mild soap

Air dry completely before storing

Store in a ventilated container

Avoid hot water, which can warp the material


With good care, your mouth guard can last several years.

FAQs – Mouth Guards in Katy, TX

1. How long does a custom mouth guard last?

With regular care, your mouth guard can last 3 to 5 years. Children or heavy grinders may need more frequent replacements.

2. Can I use one mouth guard for both sports and sleep?

No. Sports guards and night guards serve different purposes and are made from different materials. You should not use one interchangeably.

3. Does insurance cover custom mouth guards?

Most plans cover night guards for bruxism. Coverage for sports guards varies—our team will help you check your benefits.

4. How do I clean my mouth guard?

Rinse after each use, clean with a soft toothbrush and soap, and store it in a dry, ventilated case. Never use hot water or bleach.

5. What are the signs that I need a night guard?

Common signs include morning headaches, worn teeth, jaw pain, and tight jaw muscles. If you wake up feeling discomfort, it’s worth getting evaluated.
