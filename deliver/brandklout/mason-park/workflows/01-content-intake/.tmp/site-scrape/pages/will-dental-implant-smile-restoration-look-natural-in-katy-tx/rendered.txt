What Are Dental Implants?

 

Dental implants are titanium posts surgically placed into the jawbone to serve as a stable foundation for replacement teeth. They are designed to look, feel, and function like natural teeth, making them a popular choice for those seeking a long-term solution to tooth loss.

 

Types of Dental Implants

 

The right type of dental implant can vary based on your specific needs and oral health. Familiarizing yourself with the different types of smile restoration can help you plan your smile. Here are the main types:

 

Endosteal implants – surgically placed into the jawbone
Subperiosteal implants – placed under the gum but above the jawbone
Zygomatic implants – anchored in the cheekbone for those with insufficient jawbone
Mini dental implants – smaller than traditional implants, suitable for narrow areas
Traditional implants – the standard form used in countless smile restorations

 

How Do Dental Implants Work?

 

To understand the functionality, dental implants integrate with your jawbone through osseointegration, allowing for stability like natural teeth. This process can take several months but is crucial for the implant’s success.

 

Implants play a pivotal role in smile restoration, providing functional and esthetic enhancements. After placement, the implants fuse with your jawbone, ensuring they remain securely in place. Once healed, they offer a firm 

foundation that supports artificial teeth, allowing you to enjoy a natural-looking smile that can last a lifetime. Contact us for a personalized consultation about your dental implant smile restoration options.

Functional Benefits

 

The benefits of dental implant smile restoration extend beyond esthetics; they fundamentally improve your ability to chew and speak effectively. When you lose a tooth, the surrounding teeth may shift, complicating your bite and leading to further dental issues. Implants are a reliable anchor, allowing you to enjoy your favorite foods without worry.

 

Keeping a strong jawbone is vital to maintaining dental health. Dental implants, like natural teeth, prevent bone loss by stimulating the jaw. This preservation helps retain facial structure, preventing the sunken appearance often associated with tooth loss.
