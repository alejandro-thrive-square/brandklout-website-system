When Is Oral Surgery Necessary?

While many dental issues can be treated with conservative procedures, there are times when oral surgery becomes the most effective — and sometimes the only — solution to protect your health, comfort, and smile.
At Mason Park Dental of Katy, Dr. William Sung carefully evaluates every patient’s needs to determine when surgical dental procedures are the right course of action.

You may require oral surgery if you experience:

Impacted Wisdom Teeth

Wisdom teeth that fail to emerge properly can cause crowding, pain, infection, and damage to nearby teeth.
When these third molars become impacted — stuck under the gums or bone — wisdom tooth removal through surgery is often the best option to prevent ongoing problems.

Severely Decayed or Fractured Teeth

When a tooth is too badly compromised to be saved with a filling, crown, or root canal, surgical tooth extraction may be necessary.
Leaving a severely decayed or fractured tooth untreated can lead to infection, bone loss, and broader oral health issues.

Jawbone Loss Requiring Dental Bone Grafting

Tooth loss, advanced gum disease, and trauma can cause the jawbone to deteriorate over time.
In cases where the bone is too weak to support dental implants or dentures, dental bone grafting becomes essential to rebuild the bone structure and maintain oral stability.

Infection or Abscess Needing Drainage

Untreated dental infections can spread quickly, putting your overall health at risk.
If you develop a serious infection or abscess, emergency oral surgery may be needed to remove the infected tissue, drain the abscess, and prevent complications like bone loss or systemic illness.

Preparation for Dental Implants

Successful implant placement requires a healthy jawbone and surrounding tissue.
Procedures such as tooth extractions or bone grafting often precede dental implant surgery to create the optimal conditions for long-term success.

Dental Treatment Requiring Surgical Support

In some cases, impacted teeth must be surgically exposed to allow dental braces or aligners to guide them into place.
Surgical exposure ensures teeth erupt correctly and align properly, supporting a healthier, more functional bite.

FAQs

1. What is oral surgery?
Oral surgery involves surgical procedures to treat problems in the mouth, jaw, and facial structures, such as tooth extractions, wisdom teeth removal, gum surgery, and jaw corrections.

2. Is wisdom tooth removal considered oral surgery?
Yes. Wisdom tooth removal often requires a surgical procedure, especially if the teeth are impacted or positioned improperly in the jaw.

3. Will I be awake during oral surgery?
It depends on the procedure. For many treatments, you will be awake but numbed with local anesthesia. For more complex procedures, we offer sedation dentistry to keep you comfortable and relaxed.

4. What are the differences between local anesthetic, nitrous oxide sedation, and IV sedation?
Oral surgery can feel intimidating, but we offer several sedation options to keep your experience as smooth and stress-free as possible. Here’s how they compare:

Local Anesthetic: This is the go-to for minor procedures, such as tooth extractions or gum treatments. Local anesthetic is administered directly to the treatment area, numbing only that specific spot. You’ll be awake and aware, but you won’t feel pain in the numbed region. It’s quick to wear off, so you can get back to your routine sooner.
Nitrous Oxide (Laughing Gas) with Local Anesthetic: For more involved procedures—like wisdom tooth extractions or implant placement—nitrous oxide can be paired with local anesthesia. Nitrous oxide helps you feel relaxed and less anxious while still keeping you awake. The effects wear off rapidly once the mask is removed, meaning you can often resume your day right away.
IV Sedation: Reserved for more complex surgeries or for those with significant dental anxiety, IV sedation delivers medication through a vein. You’ll feel deeply relaxed—often drifting off or losing memory of the procedure. Afterward, you’ll need a friend or family member to drive you home, and expect a bit more recovery time as the effects gradually taper off.

Not sure which option is right for you? Dr. Dave Cho will discuss your needs and help you choose the most comfortable path forward.

5. What are the benefits of dental implants for jawbone health and oral function?
When it comes to restoring both your smile and your bite, dental implants offer unique advantages beyond simply replacing missing teeth. One of their greatest benefits is the way they help preserve the health and strength of your jawbone. Unlike traditional bridges or dentures, dental implants act much like natural tooth roots, providing continuous stimulation to the jawbone whenever you chew. This stimulation is essential for preventing bone loss, which can occur when teeth are missing and the jawbone is left unsupported.

Here’s how dental implants support your overall oral health:

Prevention of Bone Loss: Implants help prevent the jawbone from shrinking or deteriorating, which is a common consequence of tooth loss.
Restored Chewing and Speech: With a stable foundation, you can eat your favorite foods confidently and speak clearly—no slippage or discomfort.
Long-Term Oral Structure: By integrating with the bone, implants help maintain the natural contours of your face and prevent that sunken look often seen with extended tooth loss.
Reliability and Durability: Dental implants are built to last and function just like natural teeth, so you can smile, laugh, and live life to the fullest.

If you’re considering options to replace missing teeth, dental implants don’t just fill the gap; they help protect your jaw and keep your oral function strong for years to come.

6. How can dental implants help restore oral health after tooth loss?
When you lose a tooth, your oral health can face several long-term challenges. Fortunately, dental implants offer a reliable, effective solution that does far more than simply fill a gap in your smile.

Here’s how dental implants can make a difference:

Preserve Jawbone Health: One of the biggest risks after losing a tooth is jawbone loss. Dental implants provide your jaw with the stimulation it needs to remain strong and healthy, much like natural tooth roots, helping to prevent bone deterioration over time.
Support Everyday Function: Missing teeth can make eating and speaking more difficult. Implants restore your ability to chew your favorite foods and speak clearly, so you can enjoy daily life without worry.
Maintain Facial Structure: Without tooth roots, bone loss can cause your cheeks and lips to sink inward, affecting your facial appearance. Because implants keep your bone intact, they help preserve your natural facial shape.
Protect Surrounding Teeth: Unlike traditional bridges, implants don’t require grinding down adjacent healthy teeth. This means the rest of your smile stays as intact and healthy as possible.
Long-Lasting Results: Implants are built to last, offering a permanent and stable solution for tooth loss with proper care.

In essence, dental implants help restore the foundation of your smile—improving both function and appearance while supporting long-term oral health.

7. What Are the Most Common Types of Oral Diseases and Pathology?
A healthy mouth isn’t just about a bright smile—it’s also about safeguarding yourself from a range of oral diseases that can impact both comfort and overall wellness. Understanding these common conditions can help you stay proactive with your oral hygiene and seek care before minor issues become major concerns.

Some of the most frequently encountered oral diseases and pathologies include:

Bacterial Infections: Most commonly seen as tooth abscesses or gum infections (periodontal disease), these result from bacteria thriving in plaque and tartar buildup.
Viral Conditions: Cold sores and fever blisters, often caused by the herpes simplex virus, are prevalent and can be recurrent.
Chronic Mouth Ulcers: Conditions such as canker sores (aphthous ulcers) can cause persistent discomfort and may require treatment if they become severe.
Oral Cysts and Tumors: These may be benign or malignant and can appear as growths or lumps in the mouth, gums, or jaw.
Fungal Infections: Oral thrush, caused by Candida yeast, is fairly common, especially in individuals with compromised immune systems or those using certain medications.
Oral Cancers: These serious conditions may present as unexplained sores, lumps, or patches in the mouth or throat—early detection is key.
Salivary Gland Disorders: Issues with saliva flow, such as stones or infections, can lead to swelling and discomfort.

Staying diligent with brushing, flossing, and regular dental checkups helps prevent many of these concerns. If you notice unusual symptoms, such as sores that don’t heal, new lumps, or persistent pain, schedule an evaluation for peace of mind and prompt care.

8. What qualifications and training do oral and maxillofacial surgeons typically have?
Oral and maxillofacial surgeons are dental professionals with advanced training you can trust. After earning their dental degree, they complete an additional four to six years of intensive, hospital-based residency programs—often in partnership with top teaching hospitals or organizations, such as the American Association of Oral and Maxillofacial Surgeons (AAOMS).

During this surgical residency, residents gain hands-on experience across multiple specialties, including general surgery, anesthesia, emergency medicine, otolaryngology (ENT), and plastic and reconstructive surgery. This cross-disciplinary training provides them with the expertise needed to manage complex facial and oral conditions safely and effectively.

When you see “oral surgery” on your treatment plan, you can rest assured your surgeon is not only a dentist but a highly trained surgical professional with comprehensive medical and dental knowledge.

9. What are the symptoms of oral pathology that may require a dental evaluation?
It’s important to monitor your mouth for certain changes that could signal a problem requiring prompt attention from our team. While most oral conditions are harmless, some symptoms may indicate underlying issues that need a professional evaluation.

Watch for signs such as:

Persistent sores or ulcers that bleed or fail to heal within two weeks
Unusual lumps or bumps in your mouth or on your neck
Red or white patches developing on your gums, tongue, or inner cheeks
Changes in the color, texture, or shape of the tissue inside your mouth
Swelling in your mouth or jaw that does not subside

Even if these signs seem minor, they can sometimes reflect deeper concerns. If you notice anything out of the ordinary, please reach out to us—early evaluation is the best way to protect your health and peace of mind.

10. What is Oral Pathology and Why Should You Watch for Changes in Your Mouth?
Oral pathology is the specialty focused on identifying and diagnosing diseases that affect the soft tissues inside your mouth—including the inner lining, also known as the mucosa. When healthy, this tissue appears smooth, pink, and resilient. Monitoring the mucosa for changes in color, texture, or overall appearance is important, as these subtle shifts can be early warning signs of underlying issues.

Common reasons to keep an eye out include:

Unusual discoloration—such as white, red, or darkened patches
Thickening, lumps, or sores that don’t heal
Persistent pain, bleeding, or swelling

While many changes in your mouth may be harmless or related to minor infections, some could signal more significant concerns that benefit from prompt evaluation. Regular self-checks and dental visits allow Dr. Cho and our team to spot and address potential problems early, leading to better treatment outcomes and peace of mind.
