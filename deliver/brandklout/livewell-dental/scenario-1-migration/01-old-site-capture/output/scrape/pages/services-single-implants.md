## What is a Dental Implant?


A single dental implant replaces one missing tooth without using the neighboring teeth for support. The implant post is placed in the jawbone, where it acts like an artificial tooth root. After healing, a custom abutment and crown are attached to restore the visible tooth.

Unlike a traditional bridge, a single implant stands on its own. It does not require reshaping the healthy teeth on either side of the gap. It also helps support the bone in the area where the tooth was lost.

At Livewell Dental, Dr. Chris uses 3D imaging, digital planning, and a custom crown design to help the implant fit your bite, your smile, and your long-term oral health.

## How We Place a Single Tooth Implant


Five steps. No guessing. No surprises.

We start with diagnosis and planning

A CBCT scan shows your bone structure in three dimensions. Intraoral scanning captures the position of nearby teeth, and bite analysis helps us understand how forces may affect the implant once it is restored. We measure, analyze, and plan before treatment begins.

Your implant position is planned before surgery

We map the planned angle, depth, and position of the implant based on your bone density, nearby anatomy, and adjacent teeth. When appropriate, a custom surgical guide is 3D-printed to help transfer that plan from the computer to your mouth.

Implant placement and temporary planning

The implant is placed using local anesthesia, and sedation is available if you prefer. In selected cases, a temporary tooth can be provided during healing. If bone density is low, grafting may be recommended before or during implant placement. PRF may also be used to support your body’s natural healing response.

Healing and integration

Over several months, the implant integrates with the surrounding bone. During this time, you may wear a temporary tooth or another temporary solution depending on your case. We monitor healing and guide you on what you can safely chew while the implant stabilizes.

Once the implant is healed, we design and place your permanent crown.

Once the implant has healed, we design and place the final crown. Digital models help guide the fit, and shade-matching tools help the crown blend with your surrounding teeth. The goal is a restored tooth that looks natural, feels comfortable, and functions well.

## The Technology We Use Is Chosen for Accuracy, Not Show


Every piece of equipment we use has a specific job: better information before we start, better precision while we work, and support for smoother healing afterward.


### CBCT 3D Imaging


A cone beam scan shows your jawbone in three dimensions. We see exactly where nerves run, where bone is thickest, and what the safest, most effective implant position looks like for your specific tooth.


### 3D Printing & Guided Surgery


Using data from your 3D scan, we can design a surgical guide that helps control the planned angle and depth. This supports more precise placement than relying on feel alone.


### CLEAN Implant System


We choose implant systems with strong quality standards and biocompatible surface design. What we place in your body matters, so we do not cut corners on implant selection.


### PRF / PRP / CGF


We may use growth factors derived from a small sample of your own blood to support tissue healing after surgery. This technique can help support the body’s natural healing response around surgical sites.


### Surgical Piezosurgery


An ultrasonic surgical tool that can help us work more precisely around delicate areas. In selected cases, it may reduce trauma to surrounding tissues compared with traditional instruments.


### Modern sedation and comfort techniques


Comfort options help patients feel calmer and more prepared during implant treatment. We review your health history and choose the safest approach for your needs.

How long does a single tooth implant take from start to finish?

Most cases take 3 to 5 months total. The placement appointment itself is usually under an hour. The majority of the timeline is healing time, while the implant fuses with bone. Once integration is confirmed, the final crown is placed in one or two short visits.

Does getting a dental implant hurt?

The procedure itself is done under local anesthesia, so you won't feel pain during placement. Sedation is also available. Afterward, most patients describe mild soreness for a few days, similar to having a tooth pulled. Over-the-counter medication typically handles it. Many patients return to light activity within a day or two, depending on the complexity of the case and their overall health.

How long do single tooth implants last?

The implant post is designed as a long-term replacement root, but its lifespan depends on bone health, gum health, bite forces, hygiene, and maintenance. The crown on top may need replacement over time due to normal wear.

What's the difference between an implant and a bridge?

A bridge requires filing down the two teeth on either side of the gap to support a connected three-unit restoration. An implant stands on its own. It doesn't touch neighboring teeth. It also preserves the jawbone beneath it, which a bridge cannot do. For many patients with healthy teeth on both sides of the gap, an implant may be the more conservative long-term option.

Can I get an implant if I've had bone loss?

In many cases, yes. If the bone has thinned since the tooth was lost, a bone graft can rebuild the area before or during implant placement. The CBCT scan we take at your first visit shows us exactly how much bone is available and whether grafting is needed.

Will my implant look like a real tooth?

That's the point. The crown is custom-made to match the shade, shape, and surface texture of your surrounding teeth. Dr. Chris designs it to fit your bite and smile line. The goal is a result that blends naturally with your smile.

What happens if I don't replace the missing tooth?

After a tooth is lost, the bone in that area can shrink over time. Nearby teeth may also begin to shift toward the open space, and the bite can change. Over time, this may lead to uneven wear, jaw discomfort, or more complex treatment needs. Replacing the tooth earlier often gives you more straightforward options.

How much would a single dental implant cost?

A single implant — covering the implant post, abutment, and crown — typically runs $3,000 to $6,000. Where you land in that range depends on a few things specific to your case.

The type of implant plays a role (titanium vs. zirconia, standard vs. premium components), and so does the complexity of your case — if you need a bone graft or sinus lift beforehand, that adds to the total. Standard pricing includes your consultation, imaging, the implant placement itself, the abutment, and the crown.
