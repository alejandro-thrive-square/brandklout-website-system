# Sedation Dentistry in Hackensack, NJ


For patients who have put off dental care because of fear, sensitivity, or a rough experience in the past. Comfortable, monitored sedation, so treatment finally feels manageable.


## What Is Sedation Dentistry?


Sedation dentistry helps you feel calm, relaxed, and at ease during dental procedures. It uses medication to reduce anxiety and sensitivity, so the appointment feels manageable rather than overwhelming. Depending on the level chosen, you may stay fully awake and simply feel relaxed, drift into a semi-conscious "twilight" state, or sleep through the whole procedure entirely.

What sedation is not is a shortcut. It is not a replacement for careful, gentle care, and it does not skip any part of the treatment. What it does is take the fear, the tension, and often the memory of the difficult parts out of the experience, so you can get the care you need without dread.

At Hackensack Center, sedation is available for anyone who wants it. For some patients, it is what finally makes long-avoided care possible.


## What Dental Anxiety Feels Like, and How Sedation Helps


Dental anxiety is common, and there is no reason to be embarrassed by it. For a lot of our patients, the fear is the hardest part of coming in, and sedation is what finally makes it possible. Here is how it changes the experience.

It Quiets the Fear – Anxiety before an appointment can be worse than the appointment itself. Sedation calms the nervous system, so you arrive and stay relaxed rather than tense.

It Takes Out the Memory – The medications we use produce a mild amnesic effect, so much of the procedure simply feels like a hazy blur afterward. For many patients, that alone changes everything.

It Helps Where Numbing Struggles – Some patients are difficult to fully numb, or feel every sensation despite local anesthetic. Sedation adds a second layer of comfort, so treatment is truly painless.

It Lets Us Do More in One Visit – Because you are relaxed for the full appointment, we can complete more treatment at once rather than breaking it into short visits. Fewer appointments, faster progress.


## Types of Sedation We Offer


Oral Conscious Sedation

The mildest option. You take a prescription sedative pill before your appointment, often one the night before to help you sleep and another the morning of. You stay awake and responsive, but deeply relaxed throughout the procedure. Well-suited to shorter appointments and moderate anxiety.

IV Sedation

A stronger, more precise level of sedation delivered intravenously. It places you in a semi-conscious "twilight" state, so you are unaware of the procedure and often will not remember it afterward. IV sedation also reduces post-operative swelling and discomfort, which makes it a good fit for implant surgery and longer visits.

General Anesthesia

The deepest level of sedation, administered by a specially trained dental anesthesiologist. You are fully asleep throughout the procedure and wake up with the treatment complete. General anesthesia is typically used for the most complex or full-arch cases, and for patients who prefer to be entirely unconscious for their care.


## How Your Sedation Appointment Works


Sedation is planned carefully in advance, so nothing about the appointment feels rushed or uncertain.


### Pre-Sedation Consultation


Before we sedate you for anything, Dr. Meskin reviews your health history, current medications, and allergies to confirm which sedation level is right for you. Some medical conditions may require clearance from your physician, and we will let you know if that applies.


### Pre-Appointment Instructions


You receive clear, specific instructions covering fasting, medications, clothing, and transportation. Following these correctly is what makes the sedation both safe and comfortable, so we walk through them with you.


### Sedation Administration


On the day of the procedure, sedation is administered by Dr. Meskin or, for general anesthesia and IV sedation, by our dental anesthesiologist. You are then eased into a calm, comfortable state before treatment begins.


### Monitored Treatment


Throughout the procedure, your vital signs are monitored continuously by trained staff. This is the standard for any level of sedation above oral, and it is how we keep the experience safe as well as comfortable.


### Recovery and Discharge


After treatment, you rest in our recovery area until you are fully alert and stable. You will need a responsible adult to drive you home and stay with you for a few hours, since the effects of sedation can linger for the rest of the day.


## Who Might Benefit From Sedation Dentistry


Sedation dentistry is not just for people with severe phobia. It is for anyone who wants the experience of dental care to feel easier, and it is an especially good fit if any of the following apply:

Fear or phobia of dental procedures

Difficulty getting fully numb with local anesthetic

A strong gag reflex

Sensitive teeth or sensitive oral nerves

A history of a difficult past dental experience

Complex or lengthy treatment ahead of you

Full-arch implant surgery or other significant procedures

Discomfort holding your mouth open for long periods

If any of these apply, ask about sedation at your consultation. Dr. Meskin will help you choose the level that fits your treatment and how you want to feel.


## Before and After Your Sedation Appointment



### Before the Appointment:


Avoid eating or drinking for 10 hours before your appointment

Take your normal medications with a sip of water, unless we instruct you otherwise

Do not smoke for 24 hours before your procedure

Wear comfortable, loose clothing

Remove jewelry, nail polish, and contact lenses

Brush your teeth as normal

Arrange for a responsible adult to drive you home


### After the Appointment:


Rest for the remainder of the day

Do not drive, operate machinery, or return to work

Avoid alcohol for at least 24 hours

Take any prescribed medications exactly as directed

Stick to soft foods and cool liquids at first

Have your companion stay with you for a few hours

Call us if anything feels wrong or unexpected

## Frequently Asked Questions


Is sedation dentistry safe?

Yes, when it is properly planned and administered. Before any sedation, we review your health history, current medications, and allergies to confirm you are a good candidate. Your vital signs are monitored throughout the procedure. General anesthesia and IV sedation are handled by a dental anesthesiologist trained specifically for these levels of sedation.

Will I be asleep the whole time?

It depends on the level. Oral sedation keeps you relaxed but awake. IV sedation puts you in a "twilight" state where you may drift in and out but usually will not remember the procedure. General anesthesia puts you fully asleep. Dr. Meskin will help you choose the level that fits.

Will I feel any pain?

No. Sedation is used alongside local anesthetic, so the treatment area is fully numb regardless of the sedation level. Patients typically describe the experience as calm and pain-free, and often say the memory of it is hazy or absent.

How long does the sedation last?

It depends on the level. Oral sedation can leave you drowsy for several hours. IV sedation and general anesthesia typically wear off within a few hours, but you should plan to rest for the remainder of the day and avoid driving or making important decisions until the next morning.

Do I need someone to drive me home?

Yes, for any sedation level beyond very light oral sedation. You will not be permitted to drive yourself, and we ask that a responsible adult stay with you for at least a few hours after your appointment.

Can I eat before my appointment?

No, not for most sedation levels. You will need to avoid eating and drinking for 10 hours before your appointment. We will send you complete pre-appointment instructions so nothing is unclear.

How much does sedation cost?

It depends on the level and the length of your procedure. We will include sedation in your written treatment estimate up front, so there are no surprises. Financing options are available if cost is a concern.

Is sedation covered by insurance?

Sometimes, particularly when sedation is medically necessary for a specific procedure. Our team will verify your insurance benefits before treatment and let you know what may be covered.
