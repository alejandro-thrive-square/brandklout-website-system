# All-On-X Recovery: What To Expect The Day After Same-Day Teeth Surgery


By DR. ALAN MESKIN

Updated On:

August 22, 2026


## Contents


Text Link

You did it. You walked into Hackensack Center for Dental Implants this morning with failing teeth or a worn-out denture, and you are walking out with a complete set of fixed teeth in your mouth. All-On-X same-day teeth surgery is one of the most transformative procedures in modern dentistry, and the next 24 to 48 hours of recovery set the foundation for your long-term success.

This guide walks you through exactly what to expect after your All-On-X procedure with Dr. Meskin, hour by hour and day by day for the critical first 2 days. Following these instructions carefully will keep you comfortable, protect your new teeth, and help your implants integrate successfully with your bone.


## The Drive Home: Hours 0 to 4


You had IV sedation. You will be groggy, sleepy, and possibly emotional as the medication wears off. Your driver should plan to stay with you for the next 24 hours, or arrange for a family member to take over once you are home.


### What To Expect


Your mouth will feel numb for 2 to 4 more hours from local anesthesia

You may feel some mild bleeding or pink saliva, which is normal

Your lips and cheeks may feel swollen or puffy

You will have a complete set of temporary teeth attached to your implants already

You may feel a slight strange sensation as you adjust to the new fixed teeth


### Right When You Get Home


Rest in a recliner or propped up with pillows, head elevated

Apply ice packs to your cheeks (20 minutes on, 20 minutes off) for the next 24 hours

Take prescribed pain medication BEFORE the numbness fully wears off

Sip cool water and clear liquids slowly, no straws

Do not look in the mirror for a thorough inspection yet, you will feel more swollen than you actually look

Rest is the most important thing during the first 24 hours. Your body is healing from a significant procedure and needs your attention.


## The First Evening: Hours 4 to 12


By the evening of your surgery day, the sedation will be mostly out of your system but you should still rest. Light activity around the house is fine, but no driving, no major decisions, and no signing legal documents until tomorrow.


### What To Eat


For the first 24 hours, stick to room-temperature soft liquids and very soft foods that require no chewing. Good options:

Cool smoothies (no straw, sip from the cup)

Cold yogurt

Lukewarm soup or broth

Applesauce

Pudding or mashed banana

Avoid hot temperatures for the first 24 hours because the heat can increase bleeding. Avoid anything that requires chewing. Your new teeth are functional but need 3 to 5 months for the implants underneath to fully integrate with bone, and putting heavy chewing pressure on them too soon can disrupt healing.


### Pain Management


Take prescribed pain medication on a schedule for the first 48 to 72 hours, not just when pain becomes severe. Staying ahead of pain is much easier than catching up to it. Most patients report manageable discomfort, more like a dull ache than sharp pain, especially when on schedule with their medication.


## Day 1: The First 24 Hours


Day 1 is the most important for setting up successful long-term healing. Your job today is simple: rest, manage swelling, take medications on schedule, and protect your new teeth.


### Morning of Day 1


You may wake up with more swelling than you went to bed with. This is completely normal. Swelling peaks at 48 to 72 hours, so today and tomorrow will feel worse than today before things improve.

Continue ice packs every 20 minutes throughout the day. Take pain medication on schedule. Sip cool water and soft foods throughout the day to stay hydrated and nourished.


### Salt Water Rinses Start Today


Beginning 24 hours after surgery, start gentle salt water rinses. Mix 1/2 teaspoon of salt in 8 ounces of warm water. Gently swish, then let the water drip out of your mouth rather than spitting forcefully. Rinse 3 to 4 times per day, especially after eating anything. Do not use commercial mouthwash, which contains alcohol that can irritate the surgical sites.


### Do Not Brush Your New Teeth Yet


Do not brush your healing teeth or surgical sites for the first 24 hours. After 24 hours, you can use a soft toothbrush to gently brush the chewing surfaces of your healing teeth (the part that bites food), but avoid the gum line until your follow-up visit.

The first 10 to 14 days require a soft diet. Yogurt, smoothies, scrambled eggs, soft pasta, and well-cooked vegetables are your best friends.


## Day 2: When Things Get Worse Before Better


Most patients report that day 2 and day 3 are actually more uncomfortable than the day of surgery itself. This is when swelling peaks and bruising may appear on the cheeks or jaw. This is normal and not a sign that anything is wrong.


### Continue The Routine


Ice packs every 20 minutes for any remaining swelling

Pain medication on schedule, not just when it hurts

Salt water rinses 3 to 4 times per day

Soft food diet, no chewing yet

Rest as much as possible, light walking around the house is fine


### What You Can Eat on Day 2


By day 2 you can introduce more variety while still avoiding chewing:

Soft scrambled eggs

Cottage cheese

Mashed potatoes (lukewarm, not hot)

Oatmeal or cream of wheat

Soft pasta with light sauce

Soft fish (tilapia, cod)

Refried beans

Avocado

Continue avoiding hot temperatures, hard or crunchy foods, anything seedy, and chewy meats. Your new teeth are temporary and not strong enough for normal chewing yet.


## The Soft Food Diet: Weeks 1 to 12


For the first 12 weeks after All-On-X surgery, you are on a progressive soft food diet. The implants underneath your new teeth are fusing with your jawbone, and heavy chewing pressure during this critical period can disrupt the bond.

Week 1 to 2: Liquids and very soft foods only, no chewing

Week 3 to 6: Soft foods that require gentle chewing, no hard or crunchy

Week 7 to 12: Most foods are fine, but still avoid extremely hard items

Week 12 onward: Once implants are fully integrated, return to normal diet

Once you receive your final permanent zirconia bridge at the 3 to 5 month mark, you can eat nearly everything that natural teeth can eat. Most patients describe biting into their first apple or steak as one of the most emotional moments of their treatment.


## When To Call Us


Call Hackensack Center for Dental Implants at (201) 488-8866 immediately if you experience:

Bleeding that does not stop after 4 hours of pressure

Severe pain not controlled by prescribed medication

Swelling that worsens after day 4 instead of improving

Fever above 101 degrees Fahrenheit

The temporary bridge becomes loose, broken, or feels different

Pus or foul taste from any surgical site

Numbness in the lip, tongue, or chin that does not resolve within 48 hours


## Your First Follow-Up Visit


You will return to see Dr. Meskin at 1 to 2 weeks after surgery for your first follow-up. We check healing progress, remove any non-dissolving stitches, adjust your temporary bridge if needed, and answer questions. Visit our What To Expect page for a full overview of the All-On-X timeline.

In Conclusion

The first 48 hours after All-On-X surgery are the most demanding of your recovery, but they are also temporary. With rest, ice packs, pain medication on schedule, soft foods, and gentle salt water rinses, most patients move through the first 2 days without major issues.

The key is to be patient with yourself and trust the process. Your new teeth are already in your mouth, your implants are integrating below the surface, and in 3 to 5 months you will return for your final permanent zirconia bridge and resume eating everything you love.


# Frequently Asked Questions


How long is recovery after All-On-X same-day teeth surgery?

Most All-On-X patients return to light daily activities within 3 to 5 days after surgery. The gum tissue heals within 2 to 3 weeks, and the implants fully integrate with the jawbone over the next 3 to 5 months. Your final permanent zirconia bridge replaces the temporary bridge at the 3 to 5 month mark, completing your full recovery.

What can I eat after All-On-X surgery?

For the first 24 hours, stick to cool liquids and very soft foods like smoothies, yogurt, applesauce, pudding, and lukewarm soup with no chewing. By day 2, add soft scrambled eggs, cottage cheese, mashed potatoes, oatmeal, soft pasta, soft fish, refried beans, and avocado. Avoid hot temperatures, hard or crunchy foods, and chewing for the first 12 weeks while implants integrate.

Will the temporary bridge fall out after All-On-X surgery?

No. Your temporary bridge is securely attached to your implants the day of surgery and is designed to stay in place for the full 3 to 5 month healing period. It is much stronger than a removable denture. However, you should still avoid hard or chewy foods that could put excessive pressure on the bridge. If the bridge becomes loose or breaks, contact our office immediately.

How painful is All-On-X recovery?

Most patients describe All-On-X recovery as more uncomfortable than painful, similar to having multiple teeth extracted. Discomfort peaks at 48 to 72 hours after surgery and is well controlled with prescribed pain medication. Take medication on schedule for the first 2 to 3 days rather than waiting until pain becomes severe. Most patients transition to over-the-counter ibuprofen by day 4 or 5.

When can I brush my All-On-X teeth?

Do not brush your healing teeth for the first 24 hours after surgery. Starting on day 2, you can use a soft toothbrush to gently brush the chewing surfaces of your temporary teeth, avoiding the gum line and surgical sites. Continue salt water rinses 3 to 4 times per day. After your first follow-up visit, Dr. Alan Meskin will give you specific instructions for cleaning around your implants.

When can I drink alcohol after All-On-X surgery?

Avoid alcohol for at least 72 hours after All-On-X surgery. Alcohol can interfere with healing, interact with prescribed medications, and increase bleeding. After 72 hours, light alcohol consumption is generally fine, but avoid heavy drinking for the first 2 weeks. Do not drink alcohol while taking prescription pain medication or opioids.

Can I smoke after All-On-X surgery?

No. Smoking significantly increases the risk of implant failure and is particularly damaging during the 3 to 5 month osseointegration period when your implants are fusing with your bone. Smokers have 2 to 3 times the implant failure rate compared to non-smokers. We strongly recommend quitting at least 2 weeks before surgery and avoiding all tobacco products for at least 4 weeks after.

Why does my swelling get worse on day 2 and 3?

This is completely normal. Swelling typically peaks at 48 to 72 hours after All-On-X surgery and then gradually subsides over the following week. Apply ice packs to your cheeks (20 minutes on, 20 minutes off) during the first 24 hours, then switch to warm compresses on day 3 if swelling lingers. If swelling worsens after day 4 instead of improving, contact our office.

How do I clean my All-On-X temporary teeth?

After the first 24 hours, gently brush the chewing surfaces of your temporary bridge with a soft toothbrush. Continue salt water rinses 3 to 4 times per day. Avoid the gum line and surgical sites during the first 2 weeks. Once cleared at your follow-up visit, you will receive specific instructions for using an interdental brush or water flosser to clean around and underneath the bridge.

When can I return to work after All-On-X surgery?

Most patients with desk jobs return to work 3 to 5 days after All-On-X surgery, although some patients prefer to take a full week off to rest and recover comfortably. Avoid jobs that require heavy physical activity, public speaking, or food service for at least 7 days. Plan to take at least 2 to 3 days off work as a minimum, and consider working from home if possible for the first week.
