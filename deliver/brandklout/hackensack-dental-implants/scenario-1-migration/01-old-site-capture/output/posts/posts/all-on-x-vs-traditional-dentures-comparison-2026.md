## FAQ

**What is the main difference between All-On-X and traditional dentures?**

Traditional dentures rest on top of your gums and are held in place by suction or adhesive. All-On-X dental implants are permanently anchored into your jawbone with 4 to 8 titanium implants supporting a fixed zirconia bridge. The fundamental difference is that dentures sit on gums while All-On-X is bolted into bone, which drives every other difference in comfort, eating, speech, and bone preservation.

**Is All-On-X more expensive than traditional dentures over a lifetime?**

All-On-X generally has a higher initial cost than traditional dentures. However, denture owners may have continuing expenses for relines, adjustments, adhesives, repairs, and replacement. All-On-X restorations also require professional maintenance and can eventually need repair or replacement. The total long-term cost depends on the patient and treatment plan, so request itemized estimates for both options before deciding.

**Can I eat normally with traditional dentures?**

Traditional dentures restore about 25% of your natural biting force, which makes steak, raw vegetables, crusty bread, apples, corn on the cob, and many nuts difficult or impossible to chew. Many denture wearers gradually adapt to softer foods, which can lead to nutritional gaps. All-On-X restores roughly 90% of natural biting force, so most foods can be eaten normally.

**Do dentures cause bone loss?**

Yes. When natural teeth are removed and replaced with dentures, the jawbone stops receiving chewing stimulation and resorbs about 25% in the first year, then continues shrinking gradually. This is why long-term denture wearers develop a sunken facial appearance and need frequent denture relines. All-On-X stops bone loss entirely because the implants stimulate the bone like natural tooth roots.

**How long do All-On-X dental implants last compared to dentures?**

All-On-X implants are intended as a long-term treatment, but longevity depends on implant integration, general health, oral hygiene, bite forces, and maintenance. The bridge may eventually require repair or replacement because of normal wear. Traditional dentures generally require more frequent adjustments, relines, and replacement as the mouth and jaw change.

**Will my speech change with dentures or All-On-X?**

Traditional dentures can shift during speech, causing clicking sounds and slurred consonants, and many denture wearers report self-consciousness in social settings or on the phone. All-On-X teeth do not move because they are anchored in the jawbone, so speech sounds exactly like it did with natural teeth, with no clicking or slipping.

**Can I get All-On-X if I have been wearing dentures for years?**

Many long-term denture wearers can qualify for All-On-X, but eligibility depends on their remaining bone, oral health, medical history, and treatment goals. Some patients may need bone grafting or another advanced approach. A 3D CBCT evaluation allows the treatment team to assess your anatomy and recommend the safest appropriate option.

**How do I clean All-On-X teeth compared to dentures?**

All-On-X teeth are cleaned just like natural teeth with twice-daily brushing and flossing with a special threader, plus two professional cleanings per year. There is no removal, no overnight soaking, and no adhesive. Traditional dentures must be removed daily for cleaning, soaked overnight in denture solution, and adhesive must be applied each morning.

**Is there an option between dentures and All-On-X?**

Yes. Implant-supported removable dentures, sometimes called overdentures or snap-on dentures, use implants to stabilize a removable restoration. They can improve retention, chewing, and comfort compared with traditional dentures while remaining removable for cleaning. Pricing depends on the number of implants, bone condition, sedation needs, and restoration design.

**How do I decide which option is right for me?**

The best way to decide is through a consultation and 3D CBCT evaluation with Dr. Alan Meskin. Your bone quality, oral health, medical history, lifestyle, maintenance preferences, goals, and budget should all be considered. The recommended treatment should be based on your individual needs rather than assuming one option is appropriate for everyone.
