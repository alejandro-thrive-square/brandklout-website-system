When veneers are the wrong choice

Not all veneers are the same thing.

The word veneer covers two quite different treatments, and the difference shows up years later. Composite bonding is resin shaped onto the tooth in the chair: quicker and cheaper, but more opaque, more prone to staining, and typically lasting around four to eight years. Porcelain is made to fit you, holds its color, and lasts ten to twenty years or more with proper care.

What gets checked first

Candidacy before cosmetics.

01

Gum health

Veneers sit at the gum line, so the gums have to be healthy before anything is designed or prepared.

02

Decay and soundness

A veneer covers a tooth; it does not repair one. Anything structural is treated first.

03

Bite and grinding

Grinding and clenching shorten the life of any restoration, so how you use your teeth is part of the assessment.

04

What you actually want

If the goal is straighter teeth rather than covered ones, Invisalign or Six Month Smiles is usually the better answer.

Two visits, one design

How a veneer case actually runs.

01

Evaluation and candidacy

Gums, decay, and bite are checked, and Dr. Bennett says whether veneers are the right route for what you want to change.

02

Preparation and design

A small portion of enamel is removed from the front of each treated tooth so the veneer sits flush, and the design is settled with you.

03

Made to fit you

The veneers are made to your agreed shade, shape, and proportion, and finished in the practice’s own lab.

04

Fitted and bonded

The finished veneers are fitted and bonded at the second visit, and the bite is checked before you leave.
