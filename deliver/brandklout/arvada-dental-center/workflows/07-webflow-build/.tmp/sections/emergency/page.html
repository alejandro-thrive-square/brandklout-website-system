If this is a medical emergency

Neck or head pain, uncontrolled bleeding, dizziness, or severe trauma need 911 or the nearest medical facility, not a dental appointment. Medical personnel are better equipped for those symptoms, and waiting on a dental slot costs you time you may not have.

Right Now

Dental problems get worse quickly and rarely settle on their own. Find what is happening to you.

Sudden or severe toothache

Pain that keeps you awake, or that has stopped responding to painkillers.

Knocked-out tooth

Time-critical. Keep the tooth moist, handle it by the crown not the root, and call immediately.

Fractured or broken tooth

From an accident, a sports injury, or biting down on something hard.

Infected or abscessed tooth

Swelling, a bad taste, or fever alongside the pain. Infection needs treating, not waiting out.

Loose tooth or lost filling

A filling that has come out, or a tooth that has started to move.

Chipped or cracked crown

Damage to existing work, including a crown that has come off entirely.

Painful or inflamed gums

Gums that bleed, throb or have swollen up over a short period.

Broken or damaged denture

A denture that has cracked or no longer fits, leaving you unable to eat properly.

The First Minutes

A knocked-out tooth, in order

This is the one dental emergency where the clock genuinely matters. Four things, in this order, before anything else.

01

Pick it up by the crown

The crown is the part you chew with. Do not handle the root.

02

Keep it moist

In milk, or in your own saliva. Drying it out is what does the damage.

03

Do not scrub it

Rinsing it clean sounds sensible and is exactly the wrong instinct.

04

Call immediately
