### What Is Gum Disease?

Most of the common people are not familiar with the term gingivitis, which is also known as gum inflammation. This inflammation is the early stage of [gum disease](https://arvadadentalcenter.com/gum-disease-treatment/) which can happen due to:

- Poor oral hygiene
- Illness
- Genetica
- Hormonal Changes
- Smoking
- Medication
- Sleep Apnea

However, the most common reason for gum disease is poor oral hygiene. If the patients do not floss and brush the plaque will build up on their teeth and cause inflammation. If you do not treat gingivitis on time, it can turn into gum disease (periodontitis).

In periodontitis, the plaque in your teeth will turn into tartar, which can irritate the bone or tissue. The tartar cannot be treated by [maintaining oral hygiene](https://arvadadentalcenter.com/oral-surgery/), but it requires a professional to improve it.

### What Is LANAP Laser Treatment?

[LANAP treatment](https://arvadadentalcenter.com/lanap-treatment/) for gum disease, which stands for laser-assisted new attachment procedure. This procedure is cleared by the FDA, which provides patients with a less painful option for [periodontal diseases](https://arvadadentalcenter.com/signs-of-periodontal-disease/).

The LANAP laser treatment is known for its successful process of treating patients. By using this process, professional dentists can target the main area of inflammation and swelling without damaging the healthy gum tissues.

### LANAP Vs Traditional Gum Surgery?

#### Traditional Gum Surgery

Osseous or traditional gum surgery is one way to treat periodontitis. In this surgery, the dentist uses the scalpels to remove the damaged gum tissues. Then, reach the bone and gums to look for any infection in pockets to remove them surgically.

This is one of the most effective processes, but it is morе invasivе and can takе a long timе to hеal. Howеvеr, patients can also feel pain and discomfort for somе timе aftеr thе procеdurе.

#### LANAP Laser Treatment

LANAP is a minimally invasive method to eliminate damaged gun tissues. In this process, a special type of laser is used to eliminate the gum tissues that are damaged with a specific light frequency. It will also kill all the bacteria in the patient’s mouth, which makes the healing process and tissue growth fast.

LANAP only targets and removes the diseased tissue and preserves the healthy gum tissue. However, traditional gum surgery may involve removing both healthy and diseased tissue.

One of the best advantages of LANAP laser surgery is that the gum tissues take a very short time to heal. The LANAP is a quick process, and the healing time will be cut in half compared to the traditional surgery.

### What To Expect For LANAP Procedure Denver?

Before undergoing the LANAP Laser treatment, our periodontists will take your dental history and discuss your current condition. Our professional expert may take an x-ray of your gums and teeth to check the areas for any decay or infection to determine if laser gum surgery is the right option for you.

Our periodontist will give you a heads-up on the LANAP procedure, how it works, and the time it will take to complete. Once you are here for treatment, your periodontist will give you local anaesthesia and antibiotics. This will help you relax thoroughly during the procedure and lower the risk of infection.

When the procedure starts, your dentist will take the pocket depth measurements by using bone sounding. The gum pocket depth will indicate the exact amount of bone loss. Then, the affected areas will be disinfected by the laser. After the laser treatment, the dentist will use ultrasonic scalers to disinfect the calcified plaque, gum pockets, and debris from the roots.

Next, the laser is used again to create a stable blood clot and stimulate the regrowth of healthy gum tissue. The laser’s energy will help in sealing the treated area, which will promote healing and reduce the risk of infection. Once the LANAP procedure is complete, your dentist will provide you with specific instructions for post-treatment care. This may include recommendations for pain management, oral hygiene practices, and follow-up visits to monitor your healing progress.
