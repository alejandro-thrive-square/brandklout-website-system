### Professionals who are trained to place dental implants in Denver

#### **General Dentist**

Tooth loss can now be fixed with dental implants by a lot of general dentists. General dentists are experts at keeping teeth healthy and fixing small problems with teeth. Because of this, they don’t have much experience with oral surgery and aren’t the best choice for placing dental implants.

#### **Cosmetic Dentists**

Cosmetic dentists are mostly interested in how your teeth and smile look. Because of this, they would recommend implants that look natural over dental restorations like dentures. While some cosmetic dentists may choose to put in the implants themselves, most of the time they work with oral surgeons to get the best results.

#### **Periodontist**

After completing dental school, periodontists undergo additional training to specialize in treating the gums and jawbone. They typically possess a deeper understanding of this area of the mouth compared to general dentists, having dedicated more time to studying and working with patients in this field. Consequently, periodontists are better equipped than general dentists to perform procedures such as implant placement.

#### Oral Surgeon

When you need dental implants, the best dentist to see is an oral surgeon. Keep in mind that getting implants put in is surgery, and they need to be held firmly to the jawbone. This means that going to a dentist whose main job is surgery will keep the patient under the care of a professional with the most experience and knowledge.

For some people, bone grafts are needed before they can get an implant. The oral surgeon is in charge of doing this.

### What is the typical lifespan of dental implants?

![](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/693c51e685b19f0d0c149cab/69a5c623daf39044ea4ecc49_financing-dental-implants%20in-arvada.webp)

Dental implants are a highly favorable choice for durable tooth replacement, as they closely resemble natural teeth and necessitate only the same level of care. What is the typical lifespan of dental implants? The response to this inquiry is primarily contingent upon the patient’s decisions and overall oral hygiene practices.

The technological advancements in dental implants over the past decade or two have significantly reduced the occurrence of implant failures caused by rejection or functional problems. The most probable causes of implant failure are now attributed to the improper utilization of the implant, as well as pre-existing medical conditions and diseases.

A dental implant is typically composed of biocompatible materials such as titanium or ceramic.

#### The longevity of implants is attributed to their composition:

- An implant is a titanium or zirconia post that is surgically inserted into a patient’s jawbone to act as a foundation for a crown.
- An abutment is securely attached to the implant and serves as the support for the crown.
- A crown is a prosthetic tooth made of ceramic that is placed on top of the abutment to mimic the appearance and functionality of a natural tooth.
- Typically, the crown and abutment are more vulnerable to harm compared to the implant itself due to their exposure and active involvement in biting and chewing food.

By adhering to a consistent routine of brushing and flossing, the implant screw can endure indefinitely, provided that the patient undergoes biannual dental check-ups. The crown typically has a lifespan of approximately 10 to 15 years before it may require replacement due to normal deterioration.

Nevertheless, practicing meticulous oral hygiene and handling it with care has the potential to prolong the lifespan of the crown beyond 15 years.

The positioning of the mouth also plays a role in determining the longevity of a dental implant.

Implants positioned in the posterior region of the oral cavity are subject to greater mechanical stress during the process of chewing, thus resulting in a higher likelihood of accelerated deterioration compared to implants located in the anterior region.

### COMPARISON BETWEEN DENTAL IMPLANTS AND ALTERNATIVE METHODS OF TOOTH REPLACEMENT

A dental implant is a durable and lasting method for replacing missing teeth, which has become the favored choice for both patients and dentists. A dental implant serves as a full replacement for a natural tooth and provides the ability to bite and chew without the risk of slipping, which is a common issue with dentures.

Unlike dentures, implants are permanently inserted, requiring the same cleaning routine as your natural teeth, including brushing twice daily and flossing. Implants, being affixed to the interior jawbone, do not exert pressure on adjacent teeth like dental bridges often do, and do not necessitate any specific cleaning procedures.

### What are the potential causes of dental implant failure?

Dental implants offer a durable solution for replacing missing or damaged teeth, provided they receive proper care. However, certain conditions can lead to premature implant failure. Patients with diabetes or other pre-existing medical conditions, such as cancer, are more susceptible to implant failure. As mentioned, dental implants require regular brushing and flossing to maintain the health of the gums and surrounding teeth, which remain vulnerable to damage. Inadequate oral hygiene can lead to periodontal disease, negatively impacting the success of a dental implant.
