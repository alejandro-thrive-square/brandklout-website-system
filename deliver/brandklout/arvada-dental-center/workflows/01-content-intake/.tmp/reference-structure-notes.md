# Reference Structure Notes — Arvada Dental Center & Implants

**Asana parent task**: `1217040406855586`
**Written**: 2026-08-05 from the captures in [`reference-captures/`](./reference-captures/)

Per [ASANA-DISPATCH.md](../../../docs/ASANA-DISPATCH.md#how-to-use-the-reference-urls--read-this-before-designing),
the references are a **wireframe and a content brief, not a visual target**. These notes are the
extracted skeleton — section order, the offer/proof/CTA rhythm, and the copy angles. The visual
design of the six pages is original work on the client's own brand.

## What the references have in common

Strip away the individual styling and every reference runs the same nine-beat structure:

1. **Hero** — one promise, one CTA, proof badge in the first screen
2. **Qualifier** — a short self-diagnosis ("is this you?") that makes the visitor self-select
3. **Offer** — the money moment, stated in a box with terms
4. **Mechanism** — why this practice/procedure works
5. **Proof** — reviews, ratings, before/after, named patients
6. **Doctor** — one named human with credentials
7. **Process** — 3–4 numbered steps that de-risk the decision
8. **FAQ** — objection handling
9. **Close** — restated CTA with contact details

Every page built here uses that spine. What changes between pages is **which beat leads** and how
loud the offer is — an emergency visitor and a full-arch visitor are at opposite ends of the
deliberation spectrum and cannot be sold the same way.

## Per-reference extraction

### `ref-implants-gps` → Implants Landing Page
`smile.dentalimplantsgps.com/dental-implants/ppc/`

Section order: hero ("Replace Your Teeth & Restore Your Self-Confidence **in One Day**") → "What
Does That Mean To You?" → free-consult CTA → 5 value pillars (Specialist Doctors / Advanced
Technology / Smile in One Day / Right In Your Neighborhood / Customized Pricing) → long-form named
patient story with an explicit **Before / After** two-column list → **6-question symptom qualifier**
("Are your teeth painful or do they move?" … "Do you feel ill or tired?") → "If you answered YES to
any of the above…" → authority block → **anti-pricing argument** → financing → doctor bios →
reviews → locations.

Copy angles worth keeping:
- **"in One Day"** as the headline promise — it is the single strongest differentiator and Arvada
  can make the same claim truthfully ("place a whole new set of teeth in a single visit").
- The **symptom qualifier** is the best idea in the whole reference set. It converts a price-shy
  visitor into a diagnosed one before the number appears.
- *"Any clinic that displays generic fees is giving you a generic treatment plan. That's why we
  offer a Free Consultation."* — a pre-emptive answer to "why is there no price?". Arvada **does**
  publish a price, so the built page inverts this: lead with the real number and use it as proof of
  transparency, which their own review ("Pricing was fully transparent, no hidden surprises") backs.

### `ref-aox-yjoms` → AOX (All-on-X) Landing Page
`yjoms.com/about-full-arch/`

Much calmer and more clinical than the GPS page. Order: hero ("A NEW SMILE. A FRESH START. TEETH
THAT FEEL LIKE YOUR OWN.") → testimonials early → **"Full-Arch May Be Right for You If You:"**
qualifier list → 4 benefit cards (Fixed Stable Teeth / Eat with Confidence / Natural Appearance /
Improved Quality of Life) → "Advanced Planning. Precision Execution." → **What to Expect** 3-step
process → "You Don't Have to Keep Living This Way" → "A Long-Term Investment in Your Health and
Confidence" → FAQ → close.

Copy angles: benefit framing is **life-quality, not clinical**. The "Long-Term Investment" section
is where cost gets reframed before it is stated. Arvada's own site already has the comparison table
(Removable Dentures vs Implant Supported vs All-on-4) which does this job better than prose — that
becomes the AOX page's centrepiece.

This is what separates AOX from the general Implants page: **Implants = "you are missing a tooth",
AOX = "you are missing an arch, or you are living in dentures."** Different visitor, different pain.

### `ref-veneers-avenue` + `ref-veneers-omalley` → Porcelain Veneers Page

Avenue: hero → "LUXURY SMILE, FLEXIBLE PAYMENTS" → why-this-doctor 4 pillars → **"REAL PATIENTS,
REAL RESULTS"** → "THIS IS YOUR MOMENT" → **"AM I A CANDIDATE FOR VENEERS?"** → clinical honesty
section (gum health / decay risk / grinding) → **"WHEN A CROWN MAY BE THE BETTER OPTION"** →
aftercare → FAQ.

O'Malley: hero → named patient journey (Kelley & Jeff) → "What Are Ultra-Thin Veneers?" → **a case
study with hard numbers** (Appointments / Months Time / Percent Change / Total Investment) →
before & after.

Copy angles: cosmetic pages sell on **taste and candour**, not urgency. Two ideas carried over:
- The **"when a veneer is the wrong answer"** section. Counter-intuitively it is the highest-trust
  block on the page, and Arvada's own veneers copy already does this ("Veneers are wonderful for
  hiding problem areas… However, they do not address functional concerns").
- The **case-stat strip**. Arvada publishes "as few as two visits", which is the same move.

### `ref-emergency-smilecenter` → Emergency Dentistry Landing Page

Order: hero ("Emergency Dentist Open Now" + "Same-Day Appointments" / "Sedation Available" +
**two** CTAs, call and book) → **"Are You Experiencing a Dental Emergency"** 4-symptom triage grid
(Severe Toothache / Broken or Chipped / Knocked-Out / Abscess), each with a one-line description →
services checklist → "Why Choose Us" 4 reassurances → locations with phone numbers → FAQ → close.

Copy angles: this page is **triage, not persuasion**. Sections are short, the phone number is
everywhere, and the copy names symptoms in the visitor's own words ("Throbbing pain that won't go
away, keeping you up at night"). Arvada's own emergency page already lists 8 conditions and 5
symptoms verbatim — those map straight onto this structure.

One thing the reference does **not** have that the built page must: Arvada is **closed
weekends and after 5pm**. "Open Now" cannot be claimed. The built page uses a live open/closed
status instead, which is more useful and honest.

### `ref-nextdoor-ghl` → Next Door + Dentist Near Me Landing Pages
`sites.leadconnectorhq.com/preview/fhJtcZQi3uNr35Y2kKYC` — a **$189 New Patient Special** page

Order: hero (**"$189 New Patient Special"** as the H1, "Your journey to a healthier, happier you
begins here", one CTA) → rating strip ("Excellent ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Based on 1,700+ reviews") → **"Voted
Neighborhood Favorite"** → **offer box** ("Starting at $189" + itemised inclusions + terms in
parentheses) → doctor bio with 4 credential bullets → testimonials → **insurance carrier logo
wall** → service grid → close → **offer terms & conditions in full**.

This is the archetype for both NPS-style Arvada pages. Notes:
- The offer is the **H1**, not a section. Everything else supports it.
- The **itemised inclusion list** is what makes a flat fee feel like a deal. Arvada's $109 has
  exactly this: Exam + X-Rays + OralID Cancer Screening.
- The insurance wall answers "will you take mine?" before it is asked. Arvada's constraint is the
  inverse and must be stated plainly: **"We Don't Accept HMO and Medicaid."**
- The reference's testimonials are **lorem ipsum placeholders**. A useful reminder of exactly what
  not to ship — the built pages use named, real reviews.

### `ref-house-smartarches` + `ref-house-novismile` → batch-wide house references

Both cited on every client in the batch. Smart Arches is a **multi-step qualifier funnel** — the
page *is* a form, one question per screen. Novi Smile is a conventional full-arch practice site.

The Smart Arches pattern is already present on Arvada's own site (their "See If You Qualify For
Dental Implants Below" widget asks exactly one question per step). Since it is both a house
reference **and** something the client already runs, the stepped qualifier is used as the lead
conversion mechanism on the implant-family pages rather than a plain contact form.

## Decisions this produced

1. **Nine-beat spine on every page**, with the lead beat varying by intent.
2. **Stepped qualifier** on Implants + AOX (matches the house reference and the client's own site);
   **single fast form** on the NPS pages; **call-first** on Emergency.
3. **Offer loudness scales with deliberation**: $109 is the H1 on the NPS pages; $18,795 appears
   mid-page on the implant pages after the value is built; Emergency and Veneers lead with access
   and candour instead of a number.
4. **Nothing is claimed that the review record or the opening hours would contradict** — no "open
   24/7", no "never a wait", no invented statistics.
