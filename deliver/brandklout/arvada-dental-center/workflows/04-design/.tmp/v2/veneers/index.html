WHEN VENEERS ARE THE WRONG CHOICE

Not all veneers are the same thing.

"Veneer" covers two quite different treatments, and the difference shows up years later. Composite bonding is resin shaped onto the tooth in the chair: quicker and cheaper, but more opaque, more prone to staining, and typically lasting around four to eight years. Porcelain is made to fit you, holds its colour, and lasts ten to twenty years or more with proper care.

WHAT GETS CHECKED FIRST

Candidacy before cosmetics.

01

Gum health

Veneers sit at the gum line, so the gums have to be healthy before anything is designed or prepared.

02

Decay and soundness

A veneer covers a tooth; it does not repair one. Anything structural is treated first.

03

Bite and grinding

Grinding and clenching shorten the life of any restoration, so how you use your teeth is part of the assessment.

04

What you actually want

If the goal is straighter teeth rather than covered ones, Invisalign® or Six Month Smiles is usually the better answer.

Evaluation and candidacy

BEFORE YOU COMMIT

The things people ask first.

Still unsure? A conversation with the team can help you understand what applies to your specific situation.

Ask us at (303) 421-7611›
How many visits does it take?+
As few as two. The first prepares the teeth and settles the design; the second fits the finished veneers.
Will they look obviously fake?+
That is a design question rather than a materials one. Each case is designed against your facial features, skin tone and gum line, which is exactly why the shade, shape and proportion are decided with you rather than off a chart.
Is enamel really removed?+
Yes. A small portion from the front of each treated tooth, so the veneer sits flush rather than proud. It is not reversible, which is why the candidacy checks happen first.
What will it cost?+
It depends entirely on how many teeth are treated, so the practice quotes in writing after examining you rather than publishing a per-tooth figure. Monthly payment plans are available if you would rather spread the cost.
