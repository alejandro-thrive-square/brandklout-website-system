How an implant works

Three parts that behave like a real tooth

A dental implant has a titanium or zirconium post that sits in your jawbone the way a tooth root does, an abutment on top of it, and the crown that attaches to the abutment. In the weeks after surgery the post fuses with the bone, which is what makes the result stable rather than merely attached.

  • Fully guided All-on-X placement for accuracy and precision on full-arch cases.
  • 3D scanning before surgery — planning happens digitally, not in the chair.
  • IV sedation available — Dr. Bennett is board certified to administer it himself.

Your options

Four ways to replace what's missing

Which one is right depends on how many teeth are gone and how much bone is left to work with. That is what the consultation is for.

Single tooth implant

Replaces one tooth without touching the healthy teeth on either side, and stops the shifting that follows a gap.

Multiple teeth

Bridges and crowns supported by implants — no adhesives and none of the dietary restrictions that come with partial dentures.

Implant-supported denture

Attaches to surgically placed posts, so it holds far better than a conventional denture.

Full arch (All-on-X)

A whole new set of teeth on four or more implants — often placed the same day as surgery. See the full-arch page →
