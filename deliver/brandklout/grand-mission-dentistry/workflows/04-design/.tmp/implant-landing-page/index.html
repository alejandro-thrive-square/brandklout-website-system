Solution breakdown

Why are dental implants the best solution?

Dental implants are often the best solution for tooth loss because they replace the root as well as the tooth — which is what keeps the jawbone underneath working.

Look natural

The crown is matched to the colour, shape and size of the teeth around it. Nobody can tell which one it is.

Don't hurt

The area is fully numbed, and sedation is available for every implant procedure we place.

Easy to take care of

Brush it and floss it like a real tooth. No adhesives, no soaking, nothing to take out.

Keep your mouth healthy

The implant stimulates the bone the way a root does, so the jaw doesn't shrink away under the gap.

Can last a lifetime

Implants have been documented surviving nearly 40 years. Cared for properly, this is a once-only repair.

Leave your other teeth alone

A bridge means filing down the healthy teeth on both sides of the gap. An implant leaves them alone.

What we place

One tooth, several teeth, or a full arch

Single implant crown

The most natural way to replace one missing tooth. A custom crown on your own implant, matched to the teeth beside it.

Implant bridge

Two implants anchor a bridge across several missing teeth in a row — without filing down the healthy teeth either side.

All-on-X full arch

Four to six strategically placed implants support a full arch of fixed, non-removable teeth.

Preliminary procedures

Sometimes the site has to be prepared first

After your consultation, Dr. Sung may recommend one of these before the implant goes in. It isn't a setback — it's what makes the implant last.

01

Bone graft

Rebuilds jawbone that has shrunk away since the tooth was lost, so the implant has something solid to anchor into.

02

Socket preservation

Done at the time of extraction. Fills and protects the socket so the bone holds its shape while it heals.

03

Gum tissue regeneration

Restores the gum around the site, which is what gives the finished crown a natural margin instead of a visible line.

04

Ridge augmentation

Widens or heightens a jaw ridge that has flattened, usually after a tooth has been missing for a long time.
